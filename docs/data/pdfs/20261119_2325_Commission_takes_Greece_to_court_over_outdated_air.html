Commission takes Greece to court over outdated air traffic technology
By Tommaso Lecca · Nov 19, 2026, 3:10 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — The European Commission announced Thursday that it would refer Greece to the Court of Justice of the EU for failing to implement the latest aircraft identification technology required under EU air traffic rules.

The technology, the Commission said in a statement, helps air traffic controllers to “accurately track and differentiate between aircraft.”

In addition to aircraft positions, the new systems provide data on altitude, speed and trajectory. This allows controllers to improve surveillance and air traffic management, “enhancing surveillance services and improving the efficiency of air traffic control … ultimately further improve air traffic safety,” the Commission said.

Greece’s outdated air traffic technology has raised concerns among air traffic controllers’ unions, particularly following a major airspace shutdown in early 2026 caused by malfunctioning radio systems.

Under a 2018 regulation, EU countries were required to introduce the latest systems, with more detailed requirements set out in a separate regulation that became applicable in 2020.

The Commission sent a letter of formal notice to Greece in December 2024 and a reasoned opinion a year later — the two steps in the infringement procedure that precede a referral to the Court of Justice of the EU.

Greece responded to the Commission’s reasoned opinion by reporting progress on updating its systems, but “the Commission considers that efforts have been insufficient to meet the obligations,” the EU executive said Thursday.
