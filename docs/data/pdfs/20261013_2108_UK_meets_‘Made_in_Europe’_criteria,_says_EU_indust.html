UK meets ‘Made in Europe’ criteria, says EU industry chief
By Francesca Micheletti, Giorgio Leali · Oct 13, 2026, 2:07 PM · View in your browser

PARIS — Britain meets the “reciprocity” criteria for “Made in Europe” laid out in the EU’s proposed Industrial Accelerator Act, industry chief Stéphane Séjourné told POLITICO’s Competitiveness summit in Paris on Tuesday.

“With the U.K., today there is reciprocity, so they fit into reciprocity criteria,” said the commissioner as he explained that the core issue with the “Made in Europe” provisions is whether third countries offer the same level of access to public markets as the EU.

Séjourné warned that the same cannot be said of Canada, describing “Buy Canadian” as a “protectionist measure.”

“We will open our public procurement markets at the condition that European companies are treated the same as Canadian ones,” he said, adding that the topic will be discussed on his next trip to Canada in two weeks’ time at the EU-Canada Summit.

“Today we have around 40 public procurement agreements between the EU and third countries,” Séjourné said. Of these, he added, there are around 20 that are reciprocal and not asymmetric. “On these twenty, which are often OECD countries, there is no problem,” he said.

The IAA would steer public procurement and subsidies toward European manufacturers in sectors including renewable energy, steel and automobiles. But it has sparked clashes over which countries should qualify for preferential treatment.

A faction of center-right EU lawmakers moved to block the proposal last week at the European Parliament, as negotiators are still forming their positions on the divisive bill: Germany and export oriented EU countries favor a broad “Made in Europe” scope in opposition to France’s vision of a narrow club.

Francesca Micheletti reported from Brussels. Giorgio Leali reported from Paris.
