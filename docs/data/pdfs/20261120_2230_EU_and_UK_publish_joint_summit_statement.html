EU and UK publish joint summit statement
By Sophie Inge · Nov 20, 2026, 2:29 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — After months of wrangling, the U.K. and EU have finally come to an agreement on the key issues of agrifood, emissions trading and youth visas.

In a draft joint statement issued at a leaders summit in Brussels on Friday, the U.K. and EU set out the terms of these deals, together with a whole host of commitments on security, defense, health, technology and migration.

The document provides clarity on the summit deals, as well as the future direction of the U.K.-EU relationship. But at the time of publication, EU and U.K. officials couldn’t agree on whether Friday’s deals were contingent on an agreement on tuition fees, with the two sides providing different interpretations.

New details set out in the final text include an aim to apply the emissions trading scheme by 2028. The agreement will exempt EU and U.K. goods from the other’s carbon border taxes.

The document also confirms the implementation date of March 2028 for the sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) deal — which will see Britain align with EU agrifood standards.

Meanwhile, the youth experience scheme — which would allow 100,000 young people from the EU to spend up to three years working or studying in Britain and vice versa — is slated to be applied in 2028, with opportunities set to be available by “no later than January 2030.”

Crucially, on the outstanding issue of tuition fees, the text says that an agreement will be reached “within six months from the date of the summit.” The finalized text removes previous demands for “significantly reduced undergraduate/equal treatment” included in a previous draft of the statement, suggesting there may be some movement on the level of fees that would apply to EU students.

POLITICO reported earlier on Friday that the U.K. has been pushing for an intermediate cut to fees without granting full “home fees” status. Two EU diplomats, granted anonymity to discuss the plans, said London had so far offered fees of £12,000.

Close attention will also be paid to wording on economic security in light of Britain’s push to be included in the bloc’s ‘Made in Europe’ regime, which U.K. firms fear could exclude them from EU supply chains.

While it does not mention the rules explicitly, the statement says both the U.K. and EU will “strive, wherever possible, to limit negative effects on one another in their measures impacting specific supply chains.”

Meanwhile, in the case of shared supply chains, the U.K. is “bound to ensure in its policies and national legal order the same outcomes as regards other international partners as the relevant EU measures.”
