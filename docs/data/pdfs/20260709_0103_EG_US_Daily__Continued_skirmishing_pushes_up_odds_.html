Continued skirmishing pushes up odds of Hormuz closure

 
  
 
 
 
  • Continued skirmishing pushes up odds of Hormuz closure
  • Trump's arms sales announcements are positive for US defense industry
  • Affordability pressure on big corporations will persist
 
 
 
 

Continued skirmishing pushes up odds of Hormuz closure. Skirmishing in the Strait of Hormuz, the US rescinding Iran’s oil sanctions waiver, and the risk of a resumed US blockade increases the odds of an escalation that closes the Strait of Hormuz (35%, up from 25%). 

  • Tit-for-tat exchanges will feature in the “shaky peace” basecase (60%), though the limited scale of the strikes suggests neither side is ready for a resumption in general hostilities. Such skirmishing was also frequent following the initial ceasefire in April.   

  • Flows through Hormuz have recovered—in line with EG expectations—with a recovery to 70-80% likely by the end of September so long as the shaky peace holds. 

  • Iran is maintaining an aggressive stance in the strait, both to retain leverage over the US amid ongoing negotiations and to maximize gains from the war; though it seeks a permanent tolling system, it will likely have to settle for a compromise with Gulf states that includes more limited financial rewards. 

  • For more, please see Iran, MENA Crisis: Skirmishing will continue as Iran pushes its luck in Hormuz, 8 July 2026. 

Trump's arms sales announcements are positive for US defense industry. Before the start of the NATO Summit President Trump met with Turkish President Erdogan and hinted that he may approve F-35 sales to Turkey, reigniting a yearslong controversy. Drawing further attention from Summit attendees, Trump also announced that Ukraine will receive licenses to produce Patriot interceptors. 

  • Since 2019, US-Turkey arms sales have been frozen due to Turkey’s purchase of Russian S-400 missile defense systems, which incurred sanctions under US statute. While a limited number of F-16's were sold to Turkey in 2024 in exchange for its approval of Finland and Sweden’s accession to NATO, Turkey has remained barred from the F-35 program since 2020 and is ineligible to receive the jets until it removes Russian systems from its soil and commits not to acquire any more.  

  • The certification of these terms, however, falls to the State Department and Department of Defense and is unlikely to constrain Trump’s political calculus despite significant bipartisan congressional opposition in both chambers. Secretary of State Marco Rubo has the discretionary authority to override objections if necessary.  

  • Trump’s announcement of Patriot coproduction in Ukraine is a welcome sign to NATO partners, but it will have minimal strategic impact in the short-to-medium term; tech transfer, licensing, and supply chain reformation processes mean it will likely take years before a production line is stood-up.  

  • Both announcements are positive for the US defense industry, as they will preserve American in-roads and open new business opportunities despite the growth of “Buy European” sentiment on the continent.  

Affordability pressure on big corporations will persist. The Department of Agriculture’s requests to large grocers that they reduce beef prices ahead of the July 4 holiday is the latest instance of the pressure campaign the White House will maintain on consumer-facing companies going into the midterm elections in November.  

  • Affordability remains a top issue for voters, dragging down Republicans, but with few legal or policy mechanisms at its disposal to bring down prices directly, the White House will opt for relying on a combination of public and behind-the-scenes pressure.  

  • This pressure will only increase moving forward, especially as tariff refunds appear to be going out rapidly, with Customs and Border Protection reporting it has finalized $71 billion in claims as of 29 June. The administration will press companies to pass on those refunds to consumers in some form—pressure that will also come from Democrats.  

  • The White House will claim price cuts that some companies have implemented as a victory, likely emboldening the administration to push harder on other companies as well. How companies respond to this pressure—and particularly whether they reduce prices in tandem with tariff refunds—will likely be an important factor in determining whether they are in the administration’s good graces in 2027.

Explanation of Political Trajectories (ie, “Trajectories”)

Eurasia Group's (EG) Political Trajectories are indicators of the net impact of political factors on the macro business environment in a country over specific timeframes. For each country, two Trajectories are provided, a short-term (6 months) and a long term assessment (24 months). Trajectories are assigned by Eurasia Group’s country analysts through a structured qualitative process. Each Trajectory represents a net assessment, indicating how political changes in a country impact the macro business environment, defined as the economic and investment climate in the country. Once initiated, Trajectories are updated both periodically and as events warrant. Trajectories may be suspended from time to time if there is no active analyst opinion or analyst coverage, but the opinion or coverage is expected to resume. Trajectories can be:

Positive: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a positive impact on the macro business environment

Neutral: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a neutral or negligible impact on the macro business environment

Negative: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a negative impact on the macro business environment

Disclaimers: Trajectories are solely for informational purposes and should not be relied upon as investment advice. Trajectories do not indicate asset price movements, and have no value in forecasting market prices. Trajectories are based on data and analysis we deem to be reliable; they are not guaranteed as to accuracy or completeness. The Trajectories indicate the political outlook in a single country, over time. Trajectories do not address sector-specific or city/regional political risks that are often different than the country-level outlook. Trajectories represent an analyst’s net qualitative assessment of a country's political outlook. EG sales, analytic staff and products may provide analysis reflecting different opinions than those expressed by the Trajectories.
