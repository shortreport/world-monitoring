Daily Brief:  EU likely to harden China stance

summary

the top line

EU, China—EU gearing up for tougher China stance

  • The EU debate over China is hardening rapidly, largely driven by a shift in Germany. EU officials do not believe China will take meaningful steps to address their concerns and will respond on two parallel tracks.
  • The first is the development of a new instrument, which some senior officials are describing as the EU’s Section 301. It will likely be built around three pillars: overdependencies, subsidies and currency manipulation, and solidarity. The instrument will allow the EU to target entire sectors rather than individual products and is intended to be country-agnostic, making it harder for China to retaliate. However, this will take time to negotiate and adopt, with its first use unlikely before 2028.
  • The second track will focus on deploying the EU’s existing toolbox more aggressively. The EU has adopted new safeguards on steel and imposed a customs duty on small parcels. The aim is to show meaningful action before EU leaders next meet on 15 October.

MENA Crisis—Conference call notes

  • The shaky peace basecase is over. EG has shifted to a 55% basecase of continued US-Iran escalation through mid-August, with Strait of Hormuz traffic already falling to 5–15% of pre-war levels. 
  • Oil prices have moved into a $75–$95 per barrel range under the current low-intensity standoff, while the escalatory base case points to a $90–$110 band, with upside risk if Bab el-Mandeb is also disrupted. 
  • Iran's core demand—permanent control over Hormuz—is non-negotiable under the current regime, and the US will likely have to accept some form of divided control as the long-term equilibrium.

US—ICE returning to the headlines is a risk for Republicans

  • Two fatal shootings by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers—in Texas last week and Maine on 13 July—raise the prospect that the Trump administration’s deportation operations will become a major national news story for the first time since the climbdown in Minnesota earlier this year.  
  • If the story catches on nationally, it would likely be bad news for Republicans, as ICE polls poorly among the general public.  
  • That one of the shootings happened in Maine, home to a competitive Senate race this year, could accentuate its impact; Senator Susan Collins (R-ME) briefly commented on the shooting, and will likely seek to distance herself from the administration on it.
  • The shootings are unlikely to make the Department of Homeland Security back track on its recent deportation surge, even if tactics change slightly.

Mexico, US—Legal action against ICE will test the limits of US-Mexico relationship

  • Amid growing domestic pressure, President Claudia Sheinbaum’s administration initiated legal action against ICE agents and private detention center operators after 17 Mexicans died in US custody or during immigration enforcement operations.
  • Sheinbaum urged all political parties to support the government’s response, framing the issue as a matter of national sovereignty and human rights rather than partisan politics. 
  • Although the legal strategy does not target the US government directly, President Donald Trump’s administration will likely view it as a challenge to its immigration enforcement agenda, adding friction to an already strained bilateral relationship. 
  • US frustration is already growing over Mexico’s handling of the investigation involving Sinaloa Governor Ruben Rocha. Combined with migration disputes and Mexico’s continued reluctance to take stronger action against senior Morena figures, the tensions will increase the likelihood of US escalation on security-related issues.

Panama—Administration pushes mine decision toward year-end

  • The ministerial committee overseeing the future of the Cobre Panama mine is evaluating the results of a third-party audit and expects to submit a recommendation to President Jose Raul Mulino before the end of 2026.
  • The Mulino administration is highly committed to reopening the mine. However, sensitivities around a rushed reopening point to a longer timeline, which may also be driven by ongoing talks with First Quantum around the terms of a new contract.
  • In the meantime, the administration is seeking to shape public opinion by focusing on both the economic costs of continued closure and the environmental risks associated with leaving the site inactive. While an eventual reopening is likely, it will probably prompt social pushback, and now looks likely to be a lengthier process than initially expected.

Explanation of Political Trajectories (ie, “Trajectories”)

Eurasia Group's (EG) Political Trajectories are indicators of the net impact of political factors on the macro business environment in a country over specific timeframes. For each country, two Trajectories are provided, a short-term (6 months) and a long term assessment (24 months). Trajectories are assigned by Eurasia Group’s country analysts through a structured qualitative process. Each Trajectory represents a net assessment, indicating how political changes in a country impact the macro business environment, defined as the economic and investment climate in the country. Once initiated, Trajectories are updated both periodically and as events warrant. Trajectories may be suspended from time to time if there is no active analyst opinion or analyst coverage, but the opinion or coverage is expected to resume. Trajectories can be:

Positive: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a positive impact on the macro business environment

Neutral: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a neutral or negligible impact on the macro business environment

Negative: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a negative impact on the macro business environment

Disclaimers: Trajectories are solely for informational purposes and should not be relied upon as investment advice. Trajectories do not indicate asset price movements, and have no value in forecasting market prices. Trajectories are based on data and analysis we deem to be reliable; they are not guaranteed as to accuracy or completeness. The Trajectories indicate the political outlook in a single country, over time. Trajectories do not address sector-specific or city/regional political risks that are often different than the country-level outlook. Trajectories represent an analyst’s net qualitative assessment of a country's political outlook. EG sales, analytic staff and products may provide analysis reflecting different opinions than those expressed by the Trajectories.
