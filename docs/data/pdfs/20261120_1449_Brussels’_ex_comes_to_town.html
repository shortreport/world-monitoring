DRIVING THE DAY

WHEN YOUR EX SHOWS UP AGAIN: Should the EU get closer to the U.K. — or to Canada? As Andy Burnham comes to Brussels today to negotiate a new relationship, 10 years after Britain voted to leave, the contrast in feelings toward the ex-partner compared with the exciting new one is palpable.

Couples therapy? There’s little of the enthusiasm that greeted Mark Carney’s love-in with Europe, when Brussels made the unprecedented offer to Canada of becoming the EU’s first associate member. Instead, the bloc is struggling to muster even a shrug at the prospect of the one that got away returning somewhat to the fold.

Since U Been Gone: “We didn’t want them to leave. They broke up with us,” a European diplomat told me, summing up the mood. “Now we are over them.”

Playing hard to get: Prime Minister Burnham supports rejoining the EU and is eager to court the bloc — but has also said there’s “no rush” and won’t say how close he wants to get to Europe. Instead, he will present options and give the British public a say in due course, as he deals with reluctance to revive the topic in his own Brexit-weary Cabinet, my London colleagues report.

DTR, U.K.: Sandro Gozi, chair of the EU Parliament’s delegation to the British parliament, told us Burnham “certainly needs to clarify” what he wants out of the partnership. (Listen to our interview here.)

That hesitancy explains why Brussels is moving more cautiously than it did with Canada, an EU official, granted anonymity because of the confidential nature of the discussions, explained. Ottawa was keen to get as close to the bloc as possible for a non-member, whereas London left and is now hedging on whether it wants to come back. The bitter memory of Brexit still looms large over the discussions, a second EU official agreed.

What’s on the table: The meeting between Burnham, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President António Costa — with their ministers and commissioners in tow — is set to deliver a package of agreements after a late breakthrough in negotiations last night. That’ll include deals on food standards and carbon emissions trading and a youth mobility scheme allowing up to 100,000 young people from the EU (and a similar number of Britons) to live, work and study across the Channel temporarily. The EU dropped its demand for European students attending U.K. universities to pay the same tuition fees as British students, the FT reports.

Which is all, of course, still well short of the perks London enjoyed as a member of the EU before leaving in 2020. Brussels knows that — and while it’s happy to grant some concessions and work together on shared challenges, there is little enthusiasm for a big reset with its one and only ex.

Tough negotiators: That’s evidenced by the intense last-minute wrangling over the summit’s joint statement, with a special Coreper meeting of ambassadors called yesterday to hash out an agreement among bickering capitals wary of giving away too much.

How romantic: If the U.K. ever does want to rejoin, though, the EU wouldn’t say no. Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal told me at last week’s summit in Dublin that the EU and U.K. belong together. “I told Andy, we miss you in the EU,” he said. European Commissioner Glenn Micallef told my colleague Zoya Sheftalovich: “The door is open, we want them back.” It’s hard to think of a better endorsement for the EU than a scenario in which Britain returns from the wilderness.

The ball’s in London’s court: Burnham wants to see Britain rejoin in his lifetime and may feasibly pitch it to voters as soon as 2029, my London colleagues report. But for now, the U.K. is still figuring out what it wants. And until it does, Brussels isn’t going to do the chasing.

NEGOBOX PREVIEW

GET READY FOR MORE CUTS: António Costa is set to float sweeping cuts to the EU’s next seven-year budget in a proposal due to be unveiled tomorrow morning.

EU regional policy, known as cohesion policy, is likely to be hit for the first time since the start of the negotiations, while farmers’ subsidies are expected to remain untouched, five EU diplomats told Playbook.

While cuts to regional funds are expected to be cosmetic, they would still be a major setback for a coalition of 17 countries from Eastern and Southern Europe, known as the “Friends of Cohesion.” Total budget cuts could amount to €40 billion from the previous proposal put forward by Ireland, which holds the presidency of the Council of the EU, according to two diplomats.

Cheerful Commission: One of the few budget lines that could be topped up is the plethora of rainy-day crisis funds that were slashed by Ireland last month, the diplomats said. That would be welcome news for the European Commission, which favors more spending flexibility.

Another thorny issue involves new EU-wide taxes, or “own resources,” to finance the budget. Costa is seizing on a weakened Donald Trump to explore a tax on U.S. tech giants such as Google and Meta. The digital levy could replace a controversial business tax proposed last year by the Commission, known as CORE.

Lighter touch: Another option is to maintain the hallmarks of the Commission proposal largely untouched tomorrow and test the waters on a digital tax during next week’s leaders’ summit. “It’s quite likely that this question will be asked to leaders,” said an EU diplomat. Gregorio Sorgi has more details in this story.

20-SECOND PLAYBOOK PRIMER

The European Court of Auditors says the EU isn’t acting fast enough to protect people from “forever chemicals” — but what are they? They are synthetic chemicals that resist heat, water, grease and stains, and break down very slowly. Known as PFAS (that’s per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances), they have been linked to health problems including cancer and reduced fertility. The EU’s chemical safety watchdog is proposing a long list of temporary exemptions to an upcoming EU-wide ban on PFAS chemicals, in a bid to protect key manufacturing sectors.

PARLIAMENT TRAVEL COSTS

HEY, BIG SPENDERS: Senior Parliament figures are sounding the alarm about how much money MEPs are spending on travel.

At a meeting with group leaders on Wednesday, EPP chief whip Jeroen Lenaers called for a new, streamlined process to rein in costs, three people told Max Griera. Leaders had just approved more than €12 million in travel for the next six months.

How much? Nearly €1 million on four committee missions to Australia — three in the same week. More than €2 million on trips to other costly destinations: two trips apiece to Singapore and the U.S., plus three apiece to Brazil and Brussels’ new sweetheart, Canada.

No, really, how much?: The constitutional affairs committee will spend a quarter of a million euros flying to Australia to study constitutional alterations ahead of “possible Treaty change,” according to the document showing all committee requests.

Nearly half the total budget goes on flying interpreters around. In one case, 10 interpreters will accompany just five MEPs.

Don’t expect new restrictions: Rules already exist to limit spending but leaders routinely ignore them. “One for you, one for me, politicians give each other favors,” said a senior Parliament official. “Leadership can’t really change much because otherwise MEPs will revolt.”

BRUSSELS CORNER

WEATHER: Light showers and wind. High of 11C.

IT’LL BE COLD THIS WEEKEND: But Saturday’s sunshine is your cue to get outside. Playbook recommends a 60-to-90-minute stroll through Bois de la Cambre, around the lake and along its wooded paths. Or bring your bike, if walking sounds suspiciously like relaxation. Here are other tips for the weekend …

All I want for Christmas is … hygge: Brussels’ “Winter Wonders” Christmas market doesn’t open until next Friday, but the Danish Christmas Market this weekend will turn a church into a little slice of Scandinavia. Expect hotdogs, gløgg, pastries, herring, roasted pork and a youth choir. Time to belt out “Det eneste, jeg ønsker mig til jul, er dig” — yes, that’s “All I Want for Christmas Is You” in Danish.

Prefer a Belgian Christmas? Bruges’ Winter Glow and the Durbuy Christmas Market kick off this weekend, while Hasselt is already in festive mode.

Old is the new new: Europe’s largest vintage design market brings more than 185 dealers selling 20th century furniture, lighting, ceramics and collectibles at Gare Maritime at Tour & Taxis. There’s even a treasure hunt for unusual objects. Tickets range from €10 to €20, depending on when you visit. Bonus: It’s indoors, should Sunday’s weather go vintage Brussels.

Prefer something free? La Tricoterie hosts its illustrators’ salon on Sunday, from 11 a.m. to 6 p.m., with fanzines, prints, engravings and original drawings. There’s also an illustration workshop from 1 p.m. to 3 p.m. (booking required). Your chance to discover the next big artists before their work becomes unaffordable.

NEXT STOP — THE DANCE FLOOR: Ever dreamed of dancing to electronic music in a railway station? No judgment here. Listen Festival celebrates its 10th anniversary Saturday, turning venues including Brussels-North and Central stations into dance floors. Tiga, Avalon Emerson, Mala, Dillinja and local DJs are on the bill.
