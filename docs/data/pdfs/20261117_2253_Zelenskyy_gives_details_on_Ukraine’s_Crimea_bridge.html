Zelenskyy gives details on Ukraine’s Crimea bridge operation
By Veronika Melkozerova · Nov 17, 2026, 2:52 PM · View in your browser

KYIV — Ukraine’s destruction of part of the Kerch bridge linking Russia to Crimea was the result of a carefully planned operation involving Ukrainian weapons, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Tuesday.

Zelenskyy described the bridge strike as a response to Russian attacks against Ukrainian bridges that began a month ago.

“We have repeatedly offered Russia steps toward de-escalation — and every proposal has been met with loud rejection. Russia strikes our infrastructure every day, including bridges in Zaporizhzhia, Dnipro, Kyiv, and the Odesa region. We are restoring our infrastructure. But we are also reminding Russia that war will always return to where it began,” Zelenskyy said in a statement.

The Russia-installed governor of Crimea, Sergei Aksenov, confirmed the strike, saying the bridge was closed until further notice, but provided no details of the damage.

Russia launched a bombing campaign against Ukrainian bridges on Oct. 1, choking logistics and causing huge public transport problems. The Russian defense ministry reported hitting Kyiv’s North and South bridges across the Dnipro River, linking the two sides of the Ukrainian capital.

Ukraine said its attack against the $3.7 billion Kerch bridge is part of its response to the Russian campaign. The road and rail bridge is a key supply line for the 2.5 million people living on the Crimean Peninsula, illegally annexed by Russia in 2014, as well as for Russian forces stationed there.

The road portion has collapsed, and the extent of damage to the parallel rail bridge is uncertain, although the Russian transport ministry said Tuesday that limited traffic will resume on a single railway track. Without the bridge, Russia will have to rely on ferries and overland transport through occupied Ukraine — both routes subject to attack by Kyiv.

“Partial destruction of the bridge affects logistics, supply chains, and the morale and psychological state of the families of Russian military personnel, intelligence officers, and officials,” said Andrii Klymenko, head of the Black Sea and Baltic Strategic Research Monitoring Group.

Zelenskyy said the operation was carefully planned and gave a long list of the missiles and drones used.

“Our warriors gradually destroyed air defense systems, radars, electronic warfare equipment, and other designated targets on the way to the final objective. For this, they used Dovbush, Vendetta, Da Vinci, Sichen, Mich-2000, Ruta, Palianytsia, An-197, FP-1, FP-5 Flamingo, and R-360 Neptune,” the president said.

For the final stage, the Ukrainian army used Palianytsia drones to strike a road section of the bridge, while Neptune missiles, Ruta cruise missiles and FP-5 Flamingo heavy missiles struck the railway section.

“Using Flamingo, our warriors carried out a precision-guided strike — and the communications uplink worked flawlessly until the very last seconds,” Zelenskyy said, thanking Ukrainian weapons manufacturers.

Dutch-Ukrainian defense startup Destinus Group said that its Ruta missiles were used over two consecutive days. “Today, seven RUTA missiles hit the bridge, causing one span to collapse.”

“For years, the Russians convinced the entire world that the Crimean Bridge was one of the most heavily protected structures on the planet. Today, parts of it lie on the seabed,” said the press service of Ukraine’s SBU security agency.

Russian pro-war bloggers confirmed the scale of the attack.

“More than 10 Flamingo cruise missiles hit the target, plus a large number of drones. Given Russia’s air defense problems, the bridge could be ‘crushed’ by sheer numbers, without needing to overpower the defenses,” said the Rybar and Dva Mayora channels.

Russian officials downplayed the attack, with Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov saying the bridge would be rebuilt even better than it was before.

Russia-installed Sevastopol Governor Mikhail Razvozhaev said: “Supplies of food, fuel, diesel and other necessary goods continue. The bridge was not used for the delivery of goods that much, so the incident did not affect the supply of the city. Deliveries continue as usual.”

He said the bridge is largely used by private cars and buses. “Now the main task is to work out alternative logistical solutions for people who need to return home or continue their journey,” Razvozhaev said, asking people not to travel to Crimea until the authorities announce alternative routes.

Since Oct. 10, Ukrainian forces have targeted six other bridges near Crimea, creating growing problems for Russian logistics.

“The problem of destabilizing the rear in Crimea — and the entire southern grouping of forces— will only intensify if the railway section of the bridge is destroyed or significantly damaged, as this route is the most efficient one,” said Klymenko.

Disabling the bridge could make it more difficult for Russian forces to attack Odesa, Kherson and other regions along the Black Sea, said Ukrainian navy spokesperson Dmytro Pletenchuk.

“With the bridge damaged and land routes to Crimea under Ukrainian operational control, it would now be significantly harder to deliver those drones Russia sends to Ukraine from the peninsula,” he said.
