DRIVING THE DAY

RIBERA REDUX? As energy moves back up the agenda and Spanish politics heats up ahead of elections there, Ursula von der Leyen’s latest hire is — on paper, at least — pretty much the political mirror image of Executive Vice President Teresa Ribera. Coincidence? People Max Griera and I talked to don’t think so.

Enter Cristina Lobillo Borrero: The new senior adviser for the clean transition in von der Leyen’s cabinet has landed in the heart of the president’s inner circle, advising on the EU’s energy policy as the bloc scrambles to lower costs for households and industry, strengthen its competitiveness and reduce its dependence on imported fossil fuels.

Why that hasn’t gone unnoticed: Lobillo is an energy heavyweight, a longtime Commission hand described as professional, knowledgeable and friendly by those who’ve worked with her. She also has connections with Spain’s center-right People’s Party (PP), which is leading the polls ahead of the country’s 2027 general election. Ribera, the Spanish commissioner responsible for a clean, just and competitive transition, hails from the country’s Socialist party, currently second in the polls.

Von der Leyen brought in Lobillo to serve as a “counterweight” to Ribera, one lawmaker from the European People’s Party — the European home of the PP — told Playbook, granted anonymity to speak frankly about how the party sees the civil servant. The move has also set up a direct line to Spain’s center-right at the heart of the Commission, the lawmaker said.

It’s no secret, the MEP argued, that Ribera and von der Leyen don’t always see eye to eye on energy. Lobillo gives the Commission president her own trusted voice on the file — and ensures the topic isn’t left entirely in the hands of her forthright executive vice president.

“The president’s cabinet is made up of a selection of the most experienced officials in their respective fields,” Commission spokesperson Paula Pinho told Playbook, adding that Lobillo was picked as “one of the most respected and senior energy officials” in the Commission.

“We have different profiles,” Ribera told Playbook. Lobillo has “a long and solid technical career in the European Commission. I trust her,” Ribera said, adding that the two have always had a very good relationship.

Complementary, not contradictory: Lobillo “is not there to undermine Ribera or anything of the sort; she acts following Ursula’s lead,” a second MEP told Playbook.

Lobillo proved particularly valuable to von der Leyen during the energy crisis and the scramble to ditch Russian gas, when she led a special task force to decouple from Russian fossil fuels, three Commission officials told Playbook. She became especially close to von der Leyen’s powerful chief of staff Bjoern Seibert during that period, two said — effectively becoming the key official giving them the technical background on energy.

“She is there to implement the energetic priorities of the president,” said one of the officials, adding that “with how vertical the Commission is, being in the direct line of command of von der Leyen, that makes her the most influential Spanish official in Brussels.”

Madrid subplot: Lobillo has no formal ties with Spain’s PP but her political origins are in working with PP heavyweight Miguel Arias Cañete — first in José María Aznar’s government, later as his chief of staff when he became EU energy and climate commissioner.

Regardless, her connections mean the EU’s center-right is eyeing her up as a potential key power player if the PP takes charge in Madrid next year, with the EPP lawmaker saying she is potential ministerial material back home. “If she has to defend a Spanish position, people know her and trust her at the Commission, the same in Council and Parliament,” the first MEP said.

OSLO’S BEEN TOO SLOW

NORWEGIANS WOULD? Norway wants to rejig the way it goes about influencing EU policy — and start lobbying Brussels earlier to better protect its own interests.

The key change: Norwegian officials should start working on a proposal when it is put forward by the European Commission, rather than waiting until EU countries have given their final sign-off, according to new government proposals.

That might sound obvious to the average Playbook reader … but it would mean a significant change in approach for Norway which, despite not being part of the EU, adopts most of the bloc’s rules due to its membership of the European Economic Area (EEA). “We are making changes that make it possible to start promoting Norwegian positions and interests earlier – that is where the real room for action lies,” Foreign Minister Espen Barth Eide said.

Areas of interest: “We will now be earlier and more active in the development of European policy, particularly in the area of industrial policy,” Trade and Industry Minister Cecilie Myrseth said. Oslo also wants closer cooperation with the EU in areas outside — or only partly covered by — the EEA agreement, including health preparedness, defense and security, competitiveness and economic security.

Long time coming: “The business community had long called for concrete measures to ensure a more proactive administration of the EEA agreement,” Anne Line Kaxrud, deputy head of international affairs at the Confederation of Norwegian Enterprise, told Mari Eccles.

But but but: Norway’s ability to influence EU policy “remains limited since its many agreements with the EU do not permit participation in the EU’s decision-making bodies. Only EU membership can change that,” said Oda Helen Sletnes, a senior adviser at the consultancy Kreab and a former Norwegian ambassador.

EU, TRUMP EVENT SWAP

STILL ON BOARD: The EU hasn’t joined Donald Trump’s Board of Peace. Yet Brussels and the board’s top officials keep turning up at each other’s events. The courtship continues at the U.N. General Assembly — another sign that Ursula von der Leyen’s parallel diplomatic track with Washington is alive and well.

Oops, she did it again: Mediterranean Commissioner Dubravka Šuica — once dubbed by Playbook as “von der Leyen’s other top diplomat” — will attend a high-level Board of Peace briefing in New York today, an EU official confirmed.

On the menu: In an invitation seen by Playbook, the briefing promises an update on the Board’s “roadmap and ongoing negotiations,” its immediate priorities and projects and “the broader outlook for the next phase of our collective efforts.”

This isn’t Šuica’s first Board of Peace meeting. She also showed up for the inaugural gathering — though the Commission has been careful to stress that attending that party does not mean joining the club.

All back to mine: The board is returning the favor, with its High Representative Nickolay Mladenov attending an EU delegation event today showcasing two European waste and water projects for Gaza, one EU official said. “We need to work together with all actors — internationally, regionally and on the ground — to create the conditions for sustainable recovery, peace and stability,” Šuica told Playbook. “This is just the beginning.”

The real diplomatic coup would be Jared Kushner, Trump’s son-in-law and peace envoy, who has been invited to the EU event. He joined an EU conference in July by video link. The State Department declined to say whether he will show up.

There’s substance behind the mingling: Some board projects could overlap with the EU’s Team Gaza initiatives, one EU official said. Nobody quite knows yet how the two tracks will fit together — but they’re increasingly trying to find out.
