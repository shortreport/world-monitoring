EU starts hybrid car investigation to implement China import deal
By Koen Verhelst · Oct 30, 2026, 11:02 AM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — The European Commission launched a safeguards investigation on Friday into hybrid vehicles, paving the way for import quotas agreed with China earlier this month.

The Commission has previously said it intends to impose the curbs from Dec. 1. Friday’s notice starts a 21-day consultation period for industry and other interested parties.

The investigation follows a deal struck by EU Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič during a visit to Beijing, under which China agreed to limit exports of Chinese-made hybrids to 350,000 vehicles a year — less than half projected imports for 2026. The agreement marks a first step toward reducing the EU’s growing trade deficit with China.

Imports of hybrid vehicles increased by 49 percent between 2023 and July 2026, according to the Commission’s legal notice, which warns that the surge threatens EU carmakers.

“The increase in imports appears to be the result of unforeseen developments, such as rapidly growing production in third countries, the closing of markets as a result of trade-restrictive measures adopted by a number of third countries in recent years, making the Union an attractive export destination, and the ensuing risk of further increased imports on the Union market,” the notice said.

Safeguards are a trade defense tool that allows the Commission to impose temporary restrictions even before an investigation is completed. Unlike other trade measures, they would apply to imports from all countries, and not just China.

That means other major hybrid exporters, including Japan, South Korea, Turkey and the United Kingdom, will also face quotas. Friday’s notice does not specify how those countries will be treated.
