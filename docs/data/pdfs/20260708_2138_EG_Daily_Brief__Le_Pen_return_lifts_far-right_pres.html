Daily Brief: Le Pen return lifts far-right presidential odds

  
 
 

Le Pen returns  |  US-Iran skirmish  |  Modi-Takaichi summit  |  US-China relationship  |  Colombia fiscal outlook  |  Maine Senate race  |  Upcoming conference calls  |  EG in depth

  
 
 

summary

  • Marine Le Pen’s unexpected electoral eligibility makes next year’s French presidential race more competitive for the far-right.
  • The US and Iran will continue to exchange strikes, but this does not mean the deal will collapse.
  • The India-Japan summit will drive bilateral industrial policy designed to hedge against China and the US.
  • China has high expectations of a constructive relationship, but US actions will disappoint Beijing.
  • Colombia’s fiscal situation is more critical than is usually believed, and requires structural measures.
  • Regions, countries, and sectors covered in this edition include the US, China, Colombia, France, India, Iran, Japan, Mexico, and oil.
   
 
 

the top line

France—Le Pen’s return to the race makes a far-right victory more likely next year

  • The French far-right leader Marine Le Pen has emerged as the frontrunner for the first round in next year’s presidential election after an appeal court ruled that she could run—contrary to EG's expectations—and she confirmed late Tuesday that she would do so.
  • EG’s adjusted probability tree foresees a close run-off on 2 May between Le Pen and Edouard Philippe, the former centrist prime minister, given that the experienced Le Pen will prove to be a stronger candidate than her 30-year-old number two, Jordan Bardella.
  • Overall, EG’s election model now gives Le Pen a 42.7 % chance of becoming the next president of the republic (compared to 35.9% originally for Bardella) and Philippe a 43.2% chance.

Iran, MENA Crisis—Skirmishing will continue as Iran pushes its luck in Hormuz

  • Skirmishing in the Strait, the US rescinding Iran’s oil sanctions waiver, and the risk of a resumed US blockade increases the odds of an escalation that closes the Strait of Hormuz (35%, up from 25%).
  • Exchanges of fire between the US and Iran are likely to occur as part of the shaky peace basecase (60%), though the limited scale of the strikes suggests neither side is ready for a resumption in general hostilities. 
  • Flows through Hormuz have recovered—in line with EG expectations—with a recovery to 70-80% likely by the end of September so long as the shaly peace holds.
  • Iran is maintaining an aggressive stance in the strait, both to retain leverage over the US amid ongoing negotiations and to maximize gains from the war; though it seeks a permanent tolling system, it will likely have to settle for a compromise with the Gulf states that includes more limited financial rewards.
  • Crude prices are up to $78 per barrel on the developments, signaling worry, but not fear, of a deal collapse.

India, Japan—Modi-Takaichi summit will drive spurt of government-backed joint ventures

  • Japanese Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae’s first visit to India operationalized bilateral economic security initiatives designed to hedge against uncertainties in relations with both the US and China.
  • With the energy crisis top of mind, the bilateral cooperation will deliver Japanese support to kickstart India’s green hydrogen and biofuel ambitions.
  • After a modest start, increasing signs of success in industrial projects are encouraging more ambitious plans in the areas of AI, space, and financial regulation.

US, China—Letter from Cliff Kupchan: High expectations in Beijing challenge stable US-China relations

  • US-China relations have entered a period of “constructive strategic stability” and ties are strong.
  • China’s expectations for the relationship are very high, and some US policies will disappoint Beijing. 
  • Leaders in China object to unilateral US policies in Iran and Venezuela but will not push back.
  • Beijing is focused on sharply reducing regional government debt and regional bureaucrats are being told to prioritize debt reduction over growth.

Colombia—Fiscal outlook: a conversation with Juan Sebastian Betancur

  • In a conference call with EG, Juan Sebastian Betancur, technical director of Colombia’s Autonomous Fiscal Rule Committee (CARF), said the country’s fiscal situation is critical and requires urgent structural measures. 
  • CARF estimates that the 2026 primary deficit will far exceed the projection in the medium-term fiscal framework, reaching 4.1% of GDP rather than 2.1%, unless corrective measures are adopted; a decree to defer spending is still pending. 
  • Meeting the 2027 fiscal target would require an adjustment of about 3.7% of GDP, entailing both revenue measures and spending cuts, even though the incoming administration has indicated a preference for the latter.
  • Read more here. Reach out to discuss: latinamerica@eurasiagroup.net.



 

US—Anyone but Platner leaves Democrats better off in Maine

  • Democratic candidate Graham Platner will likely be dropping out of Maine Senate race, where he is challenging Republican Senator Susan Collins.  
    • He has until 13 July to exit the race, and the state Democratic committee has until 27 July to name a replacement, who will not go through the primary process.
    • Finding a replacement might not be straightforward, as most high-profile Democrats had already declined to run earlier campaigns against him, leaving one of the losers in the gubernatorial primary, like former Maine Senate president Troy Jackson and former deputy CDC director Nirav Shah, as the most likely options.
  • Recent polling suggests Platner’s personal issues were dragging him down relative to the state’s partisan baseline. Another figure would likely run closer to that baseline. On net, that means the race still leans narrowly Democratic, with the selection of the next Democratic candidate the next key signpost.

eg in depth

Mexico—Sheinbaum moves to contain fallout from renewed scrutiny over “El Mayo” capture: Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum presented a detailed report on the circumstances surrounding the 25 July 2024 arrest by US law enforcement agents of Ismael “El Mayo” Zambada—co-founder of the Sinaloa drug cartel—as she seeks to address renewed questions about the extent of US operations on Mexican soil. Newly released US court documents show that Zambada pleaded guilty to drug trafficking charges on 25 August 2025 and received a life sentence. Sheinbaum will try to keep the Zambada case from becoming a broader political liability and reassure her base that she will not cede further ground on sovereignty issues. Even so, the US will continue to pressure her administration through more unilateral actions as Sheinbaum refuses to extradite politicians with credible ties to criminal organizations. Explanation of Political Trajectories (ie, “Trajectories”)

Eurasia Group's (EG) Political Trajectories are indicators of the net impact of political factors on the macro business environment in a country over specific timeframes. For each country, two Trajectories are provided, a short-term (6 months) and a long term assessment (24 months). Trajectories are assigned by Eurasia Group’s country analysts through a structured qualitative process. Each Trajectory represents a net assessment, indicating how political changes in a country impact the macro business environment, defined as the economic and investment climate in the country. Once initiated, Trajectories are updated both periodically and as events warrant. Trajectories may be suspended from time to time if there is no active analyst opinion or analyst coverage, but the opinion or coverage is expected to resume. Trajectories can be:

Positive: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a positive impact on the macro business environment

Neutral: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a neutral or negligible impact on the macro business environment

Negative: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a negative impact on the macro business environment

Disclaimers: Trajectories are solely for informational purposes and should not be relied upon as investment advice. Trajectories do not indicate asset price movements, and have no value in forecasting market prices. Trajectories are based on data and analysis we deem to be reliable; they are not guaranteed as to accuracy or completeness. The Trajectories indicate the political outlook in a single country, over time. Trajectories do not address sector-specific or city/regional political risks that are often different than the country-level outlook. Trajectories represent an analyst’s net qualitative assessment of a country's political outlook. EG sales, analytic staff and products may provide analysis reflecting different opinions than those expressed by the Trajectories.
