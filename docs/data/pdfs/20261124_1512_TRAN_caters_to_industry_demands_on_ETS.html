DRIVING THE DAY

TRAN PROPOSES INDUSTRY-FRIENDLY CHANGES TO ETS REVISION: TRAN adopted its opinion Monday night on the revision of the Emissions Trading System, proposing changes that align with several demands from the aviation and shipping industries. The final vote on the opinion, presented by rapporteur Matteo Ricci, saw 26 MEPs vote in favor, 11 against and three abstain.

Delayed expansion: The extension of the ETS to flights from EU airports to non-EU destinations within 5,000 kilometers of Frankfurt, a measure strongly opposed by airlines, would be postponed by 18 months, from Jan. 1, 2029, to July 1, 2030, according to the adopted text.

Aviation revenues for decarbonization: TRAN MEPs also called for countries to channel at least 70% of revenues generated by aviation ETS allowances back into aviation decarbonization, in line with carriers’ long-standing demand that carbon-pricing revenues be reinvested in their sector.

More allowances for SAF, maritime: TRAN MEPs also supported an increase in ETS allowances reserved to support airlines using sustainable aviation fuels from 110 million credits to 300 million. They also proposed increasing allowances to support sustainable maritime fuels and decarbonization technologies from 110 million credits to 300 million.

Limiting Made in Europe preference: As anticipated by Morning Mobility, the restrictions on sustainable fuel support proposed by the Commission to favor fuels produced in the EU were scaled back by TRAN’s compromise amendments. The amendments would provide 5% of additional support for eligible fuels produced in the EU and a further 5% for fuels made from feedstocks sourced in member countries.

The Commission’s proposal would allocate allowances to support only sustainable aviation and maritime fuels produced either in the European Economic Area or in third countries with carbon pricing systems linked to the ETS, with an additional 10% support for fuels from European feedstocks.

Addressing carbon leakage: TRAN’s opinion also addresses concerns from the EU maritime sector by extending from 150 to 300 nautical miles the distance that a non-EU port with container-handling infrastructure could be designated as a neighboring transshipment port. Expanding the geographical criterion should reduce the incentive to divert traffic and transshipment operations to non-EU ports.

ENVI has the deciding voice: Although TRAN’s opinion on the ETS revision will help shape Parliament’s position ahead of negotiations with national governments, the committee primarily responsible for this file is ENVI, which is expected to adopt its own text in early December before the final vote in Strasbourg.

Aviation wants more: Despite TRAN’s attempt to cater to the industry, airlines remain dissatisfied. A4E reiterated its call for at least 500 million SAF allowances and for today’s eligibility rules to remain in place, the lobby’s director, Ourania Georgoutsakou, told Tommaso

Happier maritime lobby: The World Shipping Council’s Simon Bergulf, meanwhile, said it was “encouraging to see political differences being bridged to find solutions that support both climate goals and European competitiveness.” He described the increase in allowances for sustainable fuels as “really positive.”

MARITIME

EXCLUSIVE — IMO MAPS OUT POSSIBLE LANDING ZONES: An International Maritime Organization internal document obtained by Martina shows how negotiators are trying to organize the main country proposals aimed at cutting emissions from shipping into a single menu. It ranges from softer or alternative fuel-intensity targets to different compliance routes, fund structures and incentives for zero- and near-zero-emission fuels. The paper is meant to facilitate discussions now taking place in London and does not represent a compromise text.

Where the gaps are: On the emissions pathway, options include keeping two trajectories, moving to a single reviewed pathway, linking targets to commercially viable fuels or switching to an absolute-emissions approach. Some would soften the early years and tighten later; others would make future targets depend on fuel availability, affordability and scalability.

And on the money: The paper lays out payments into a fund or facility, direct project contributions, investment-based compliance and a route with no financial mechanism. It also lists cash rewards, fuel multipliers, extra surplus units or no dedicated incentive.

What happens next: Delegations are using this week’s technical session to test where positions overlap and which elements could feed into a later “bridging proposal,” before the political-level committee meets next Monday to take up the framework.

EXCLUSIVE — SHIPPING GROUPS PUSH BACK ON PARLIAMENT OVER INDIAN RECYCLING YARDS: Eight major shipping associations are urging MEPs to reject a motion objecting to the Commission’s plan to add two Indian ship recycling facilities to the EU’s approved list, according to a letter shared exclusively with Morning Mobility ahead of publication. The groups argue the yards passed the bloc’s safety and environmental checks and should not be excluded simply because they are outside the OECD.

Their case: Ahead of a planned vote on Wednesday, the groups say the two yards have invested heavily to meet both EU rules and the Hong Kong Convention, and that adding them would help ease what they describe as a structural shortage of compliant recycling capacity.

Not the same market: They also argue EU and South Asian yards are largely complementary: EU facilities tend to handle smaller offshore, fishing and inland vessels, while South Asian yards recycle much of the large ocean-going merchant fleet. The signatories include ECSA, BIMCO, CLIA, ICS, Intercargo, Interferry, Intertanko and the World Shipping Council.

LE PEN HITS TURKEY OVER LIBYA: Right-wing French presidential candidate Marine Le Pen accused Turkey of violating the law of the sea and undermining other countries’ territorial integrity thanks to its maritime border deal with Libya, which she called “unacceptable.”

CARS

ELECTRIC VEHICLES GAIN GROUND: All-electric vehicle registrations in the EU increased 50% in the first 10 months of the year compared to the same period in 2025, according to data released today by car lobby ACEA. Across the EU, 2.2 million EVs were registered, with 25 of the 27 countries reporting double-digit growth.

The standouts: Italy’s EV sales increased 63%, from 67,000 in 2025 to 109,000 in the first 10 months of this year. Spain’s sales over the same period grew 44% to 117,000. EVs now account for 23.4% of the EU’s market, up from 16.4% a year earlier.

Chinese gaining ground: Across all powertrains, European manufacturers saw their sales either stagnate or decline. However, all-electric Tesla saw its sales grow 70% over the first 10 months of the year to 198,000. Chinese carmakers — focused on EVs and hybrids — saw their collective sales increase 81% year-over-year, growing from 549,000 in the first 10 months of 2025 to 991,000 during the same period in 2026, according to a POLITICO calculation.

The hybrid problem: Plug-in hybrids continue to be popular with consumers, growing 17%. The European Commission is set to publish today its provisional quotas for hybrids entering the EU, starting Dec. 1 (we reported on this here).

QUOTAS ARE OUT: The EU imposed provisional safeguard measures on global hybrid car imports this morning, the executive announced in the Official Journal.

Basics: Taking the form of quotas and starting from Dec. 1 as the Commission had announced before, the measures will be valid for 200 days. While affecting all global imports, the law also implements a deal with China to cap its annual exports at 350,000 vehicles. One of the articles in the law specifies Chinese-origin hybrids will need to have a “stamped export quota certificate.”

Calculation: All countries that supplied more than 2% of the EU’s imported hybrids, received a quota based on their trade volume in 2025, plus 15%. “This approach took account of the significant growth of the Union market for new hybrid vehicles, which expanded by an average of 15% over the preceding three years,” the Commission writes.

The numbers: China will be able to export 191,780 hybrids to the EU for the duration of these temporary measures. Japan is coming in at number two, at just over 170,000, followed by Turkey, the U.K., Korea, Mexico, Morocco, Serbia and finally the U.S.

Lowered the threshold: The Commission set a lower threshold than normally for countries to qualify for a national allocated quota. While the bloc usually considers 5% the minimum to receive such a specific quota, in this case it went with 2%. This allows the U.S. to get a specific quota, where BMW and Mercedes build plug-in hybrids that are shipped to the EU.

Hard stop: “Imports in excess of the applicable quota should not be permitted,” the regulation spells out.

Locking it in: These provisional measures will apply until June 18, 2027. The Commission will need the approval of 15 EU countries representing 65 percent of the bloc’s population to lock in the measures for four years. Considering only Cyprus abstained from a vote on the current measures, that majority seems to be there for the moment.

EU-UK TRADE FORUM: At last week’s summit, the EU and the U.K. quietly created a new forum to tackle trade policy between the two. It could soon be used to determine the U.K.’s status in the “Made in Europe” discussions, our London colleague Jon Stone reports.

The context: The U.K. — and automakers on both sides of the Channel — want assurances that the country will qualify as Made in Europe under the Industrial Accelerator Act. In exchange, Brussels wants the U.K. to match its tariffs on Chinese EVs.

Ongoing talks: A senior EU official tells POLITICO that there won’t be one big blanket discussion that decides whether the U.K. is a part of the Made in Europe club. Instead, there will be separate discussions about each area — like automotive and steel, both name-checked in the summit conclusions. That’s where the Dialogue on Economic Security comes in. This is the forum through which negotiation over coordination on external trade policy will take place.

POLITICAL ADVERTISEMENT

**A message from IRU – International Road Transport Union: A scaled-up transition to zero-emission road transport requires a functional charging ecosystem combining public infrastructure with complementary depot charging. For most commercial transport operators, relying exclusively on public charging makes little business sense, given operational constraints and electricity costs. Charging at depots, during loading and unloading or overnight parking, allows operators to better integrate charging into their operations. The AFIR review should therefore promote supportive national frameworks for depot charging, including streamlined permitting, investment support and better coordination between relevant authorities and transport operators. The objective is to create enabling conditions for investment and make the transition commercially viable.**
