Spain pitches tiered approach to ‘Made in Europe’ rules
By Francesca Micheletti, Ben Makuch · Sep 24, 2026, 12:54 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — Spain is pushing a three-layer mechanism for determining which non-EU countries get the “Made in Europe” label in the European Commission’s landmark industrial policy bill.

A seven-page paper by the Spanish Secretariat of State for Industry, seen by POLITICO, sets out an approach to Made in Europe that the authors say is more granular and better tailored to the different strategic sectors covered by the Industrial Accelerator Act, which is part of the agenda of Thursday’s meeting of EU competitiveness ministers in Brussels.

Geographic scope is the most contentious part of the Commission’s industrial policy proposal, which favors EU produced goods for certain sectors — such as clean technologies and automotive — in public procurement and subsidies. France leads the camp of countries that want a narrow definition of “Made in Europe,” while Germany is pushing for a broad list of third countries to be included.

Madrid’s proposed approach, Spain writes, “would allow the Union to target conditionalities where they are most needed, notably in sectors facing acute strategic vulnerabilities, while avoiding unnecessary constraints in value chains that already display sufficient resilience.”

The proposal splits countries into three categories and suggests that these should be applied dynamically to different industry sectors, depending on how strongly the sector should be anchored in Europe.

The Commission could, for example, establish that a wind turbine needs a certain number of components to be made in tier one countries, whilst other parts can be sourced from tier two or three, with these criteria evolving over time.

The EU’s 27 member countries are part of the first layer; while the second tier includes European Economic Area members and “trusted partners,” to be defined according to certain “legal and effective reciprocity criteria.”

A third tier would be reserved for countries that have a free-trade agreement, customs union or procurement agreement with the bloc. These would be “incentivized to deepen their trade agreements with the EU in order to be upgraded to layer 2,” the paper says.
