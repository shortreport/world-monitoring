DRIVING THE DAY

COOLING THE CEUTA CRISIS: The European Commission’s big task this week? Finding an off-ramp for Italy and Spain. The pair has been locked in a bitter political argument over border controls that threatens to undermine the EU’s Schengen passport-free travel zone — restrictions Rome initiated after around 72,000 people crossed from Morocco into Spain’s North African exclave of Ceuta at the start of the month. Almost all of those who arrived have since returned.

No rest: The Spanish and Italian governments held talks with European Internal Affairs Commissioner Magnus Brunner over the weekend as Brussels pushed for the two sides to row back on the tit-for-tat border checks.

EU officials are targeting online networks used by traffickers to prevent a repeat and ensure Italy can drop its measures without losing face, three EU officials involved in the talks told Playbook, granted anonymity to speak about the talks. The Commission is also attempting to revive a long-stalled anti-smuggling directive.

Unexpected escalation: The spat comes as Spain’s socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez and Italy’s right-wing government spar over migration policy, with both facing elections next year in which the issue will be a major dividing line for voters. But the response has left travelers from both countries facing airport lines at the peak of holiday season because they’re having to go through passport checks.

The U.S. has also fanned the flames, with the State Department accusing Sánchez of a “shameful refusal to defend its people from the migrant invasion.” Adding to the pressure, an estimated 70,000 people took to the streets of Ceuta Sunday to demand answers from authorities and “solidarity” from Spanish society, El País reports.

Rome has already hinted at a climbdown. Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said measures targeting ships and planes from Spain will remain in place until Aug. 15, amid claims smugglers will organize a second wave of attempted entries on that date. But after that, “when the situation has returned to normality, we’re ready to reinstate the previous arrangements,” he said.

Tech support: EU Tech Sovereignty Commissioner Henna Virkkunen summoned Meta and TikTok for meetings last week and will continue discussions today, to work out how to eliminate digital channels the platforms say were used by people smugglers to encourage many of those who crossed into Ceuta.

Taking them offline: “Platforms identified a combination of smugglers offering services with various illegal routes or fake documents,” said one of the EU officials who took part in the meetings, which focused on “what can be done [to] prevent a recurrence.”

Like this: A Meta spokesperson confirmed to Eliza Gkritsi that it has a team monitoring the situation in Ceuta in real time to remove “content that offers to provide, facilitate or seek human smuggling services.” TikTok said it has established a task force and is using advanced technology to tackle exploitative schemes.

The bigger lesson: The crisis, which saw an estimated 100 people die trying to reach the EU, shows the bloc needs to “learn from the events [and] further strengthen infrastructure and deployments, combat smuggling networks and effectively and efficiently manage returns,” Commission spokesperson Guillaume Mercier told POLITICO.

Until next time: According to Karel Lannoo, head of the Centre for European Policy Studies think tank, even after this row, Schengen will continue to be weakened because “the Commission has been very weak on enforcement” in the past when countries like Germany imposed their own checks on people entering their territory.

OUTFLANKED ON THE RIGHT: Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni’s tough talking on immigration is designed to lock in the support of her base against far-right firebrand Roberto Vannacci ahead of next year’s general election, Giorgio Leali and Jacopo Barigazzi write.

BIG AND BREAKING

GAZA PLAN STALLS: Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Sunday he would not accept a 15-point plan to end the war in Gaza drawn up by Donald Trump’s Board of Peace — a major blow to the White House’s efforts to freeze the conflict.

DRY ME A RIVER: EU funds to compensate farmers during droughts are inadequate in the face of billions of euros in losses this summer, with dry spells and wildfires across much of the continent, Bartosz Brzeziński and Agnes Rønberg write.

HYBRID WAR WARNING: Germany faces “daily” hybrid warfare attacks, the country’s Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt warned after a drone packed with explosives was detected at Leipzig airport last week.

CHANGING OF THE GUARD: Hungary’s parliament is set to appoint former Supreme Court chief András Baka as the country’s president this week, after it ousted Tamás Sulyok, an ally of former Prime Minister Viktor Orbán.

4 MORE THINGS GETTING US TALKING

NO EASY WAY OUT: A bitter political divide in Poland’s ultraconservative opposition may not be enough to ensure Prime Minister Donald Tusk wins next year’s parliamentary election, Wojciech Kość reports.

SEA OF ROBOTS: Unmanned submarines and robo-dogs are patrolling the North Sea as oil and gas companies automate dangerous jobs in their scramble for remaining undersea reserves.

BABY BUST: Western countries are facing declining birth rates and nobody can agree why, Marc Felix Serrao reports for POLITICO Magazine.

DIGITAL DEMANDS: The EU isn’t doing enough to help telecom companies detect and block child sexual abuse material, four industry CEOs have said in a letter to European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, European Parliament President Roberta Metsola and European Council President António Costa.

BRUSSELS CORNER

WEATHER: Mainly sunny, top of 27C.

IT’S BACK: Brussels’ mesmerizing flower carpet is returning to Grand Place on Thursday until Sunday, Sebastian Starcevic writes. Inspired by famed Japanese woodblock print “The Great Wave off Kanagawa” by Katsushika Hokusai, this iteration will feature 500,000 begonias and dahlias, with a light and sound show.

Don’t despair: While tickets for the Town Hall tower and balcony are sold out online, you can still grab tickets for the latter on the day in person. You can also just head to Grand Place to watch the show from the ground without a ticket, if you don’t mind the crowds. It takes places every two years, so once the flowers have wilted, your next chance will be in 2028.

Second carpet: For the first time, a second flower carpet will be installed this year at Place de la Bourse. This one’s an homage to Belgium’s brewing heritage.

THE SHORTS OF IT: MEP Fidias Panayiotou’s decision to wear shorts to a memorial event in Cyprus over the weekend has sparked a debate about appropriate attire, Philenews reports.

TOP OF THE PLAYBOOKS

Berlin: Steffen Bilger may not have been Friedrich Merz’s first choice as Germany’s transport minister, but he’s off to a flying start.

London: Andy Burnham’s summer good vibes will be put to the test this week, with the state of England’s prison estate shaping up as a key political battlefield.

Washington: Francesca Hong looks set to win this week’s Democratic gubernatorial primary in Wisconsin — but some in the party worry her past views could turn off voters in November’s general election.
