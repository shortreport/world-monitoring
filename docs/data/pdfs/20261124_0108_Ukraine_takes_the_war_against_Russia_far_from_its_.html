Ukraine takes the war against Russia far from its borders
By Veronika Melkozerova · Nov 23, 2026, 5:07 PM · View in your browser

KYIV — Ukraine regularly hits targets deep inside Russia; now it’s admitting to an attack far beyond the war zone in North Africa.

The attack — an effort to harm Russia’s ability to project power into distant regions—highlights Ukraine’s growing technological prowess but it also carries the risk of diplomatic blowback against Kyiv.

The Security Service of Ukraine, or SBU, said its forces on Sunday sank the Russian corvette Merkury anchored in the Libyan port of Benghazi. Also hit was the Angara, a Russian dry cargo vessel that Kyiv claimed was being used to transport weapons.

In another attack, a cargo ship sailing under the Russian flag was damaged by a drone attack in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday morning, the defense ministry in Helsinki said. Finnish authorities said that Ukraine carried out the attack in international waters; however, Ukraine has not admitted responsibility.

Ukraine has expanded its attacks against Russian targets well outside the war zone in an effort to exert more pain on Moscow, said Mykola Bielieskov, research fellow at the Ukrainian National Institute for Strategic Studies.

“Given the fact that frontline operations and strikes against oil refineries and the defense-industrial complex are insufficient to compel the enemy to end the war immediately and unconditionally, Ukraine is seeking additional tools of pressure,” he said.

The Libya operation is the first time Ukraine has claimed credit for such a distant strike, but there was little point in denying it after the Benghazi government posted videos of the attacks.

There are also accusations that Ukrainian forces have long been active in other theaters.

Earlier this year, Greece said it would launch an investigation after a naval drone carrying explosives was discovered in the Ionian Sea. Greek and Ukrainian media identified it as likely a Ukrainian-made Magura drone.

Sudan, Mali and Niger have accused Ukrainian special operatives of getting involved in their internal affairs; Russia’s mercenary troops are present in all three countries. Kyiv has strongly denied those accusations.

“These operations have demonstrated the remarkable agility and boldness of Kyiv’s intelligence services in attacking vulnerable parts of the Kremlin’s sprawling security apparatus and its global footprint,” said the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace in a report issued last year, adding: “The persistence of such attacks suggests that Kyiv will continue to look for ways to punish Russia beyond the front line.”

Libyan anger

After Sunday’s attacks, local Libyan authorities filed a complaint to the United Nations and demanded an investigation.

“We call on Ukraine to provide an official explanation of its military activities in Libya,” said Osama Hammad, the head of Libya’s parliament-appointed government.

Ukraine said the operation was carried out from the sea. A so-called mothership transported seven Ukrainian-made sea drones to the area and launched them, the SBU said.

Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov on Monday denounced the Gulf of Finland attack.

“All coastal countries are undoubtedly concerned about this. It is very dangerous. It is escalating tensions in the region,” he said, adding: “Of course, we hope that the Baltic states will use their influence to dissuade the Kyiv regime from continuing these dangerous terrorist practices.”

On the Libya attacks, Peskov said the Kremlin has “noticed [Ukrainian President Volodymyr] Zelenskyy’s statement on ship destruction,” but refused to issue any further comments.

While there is no debate that the Merkury is a military vessel, Kyiv said that the cargo vessel was also a legitimate target.

“The Defense Forces of Ukraine have once again demonstrated that the Russian military fleet and its logistics have no safe routes, even far beyond the Black Sea,” the SBU said. “Such unique special operations disrupt the enemy’s logistical capabilities and force Russia to pay for its aggression, even at a strategic distance from Ukraine.”

Ukraine has not claimed credit for the March sinking of the Russian-flagged Arctic Metagaz LNG tanker sailing between Libya and Malta, said Igor Semyvolos, executive director of the Association of Middle East Studies think tank. Moscow says Kyiv was to blame.

“A tanker is a civilian vessel; striking one would raise environmental concerns and lead to complaints with the International Maritime Organization. A corvette, by contrast, is an aggressor’s warship — a legitimate military target. It is safe to claim responsibility for such a strike,” Semyvolos said.

The sinking of the Metagaz did send a cautionary signal to Moscow, said Sebastian Rötters, sanctions campaigner at Urgewald, an NGO, forcing Russia to ship its LNG “onto longer and more expensive routes.”

“The latest strikes near Benghazi show that risks have not disappeared,” he said.

Zelenskyy underscored on Monday that the Libyan attacks were part of a broader campaign aimed at getting Moscow to end the war.

“Russia can feel our response all the way from western Siberia, where its oil refineries are burning, to the coast of Libya, where its warships are sinking,” he said. “And we have offered Russia steps to reduce the fighting dozens, dozens of times. And we have never been the ones to raise the stakes.”

Martina Sapio contributed to this report.
