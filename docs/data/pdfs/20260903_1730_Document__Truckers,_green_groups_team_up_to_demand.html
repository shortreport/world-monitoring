Document: Truckers, green groups team up to demand EU truck dialogue
By Martina Sapio · Sep 3, 2026, 10:29 AM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — The International Road Transport Union and green NGO Transport & Environment are jointly asking European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen to convene her first strategic dialogue dedicated solely to decarbonizing heavy-duty vehicles, according to a letter obtained by POLITICO.

The unlikely alliance of road transport operators and one of Europe’s most influential green transport NGOs want an initial meeting in the coming weeks involving hauliers, environmental groups, truckmakers and infrastructure providers, arguing that the sector requires attention at the highest political level.

The Strategic Dialogue on the Future of the European Automotive Industry “has placed political attention on the challenges facing the automotive sector and in particular passenger cars,” the groups write, arguing heavy-duty vehicles “face very different operational, economic and infrastructure realities, which calls for a dedicated discussion.”

Strategic dialogues are collaborative consultation forums launched by the European Commission to bring together industry leaders, civil society and policymakers to help shape policy.

Around 7 million heavy-duty vehicles operate on EU roads, accounting for close to 25 percent of the bloc’s road-transport greenhouse gas emissions. Their green transition will affect logistics costs, industrial competitiveness and Europe’s energy security, as well as its ability to meet climate targets.

The joint request does not mean the two groups have settled their differences. IRU rejects binding purchase targets before operators have adequate charging infrastructure, affordable electricity and access to finance, favoring an incentive-led transition with particular support for smaller hauliers.

T&E backs binding national targets and wants Brussels to propose mandatory zero-emission goals for heavy-duty corporate fleets by 2027 to create demand for clean trucks and charging networks.
