Escalation likely as Iran sees a path to controlling Hormuz

 
  
 
 
 
  • Escalation likely as Iran sees a path to controlling Hormuz
  • Trump will use primetime speech to push election interference narrative
  • Blanche will win confirmation and serve as a Trump loyalist
 
 
 
 

Escalation likely as Iran sees a path to controlling Hormuz. With the shaky peace over, the US is likely to escalate military action as before against Iran by mid-August (55% odds), with the continuation of low-intensity fighting and de-escalation both lower-probability scenarios. In either an escalatory or continued standoff scenario, shipping though the Strait of Hormuz will be significantly constrained to under 10% of prewar levels and oil prices will be pushed higher, to the $90-110 price range. 

  • Iran will be tempted to escalate to secure permanent influence over the strait; it is unsatisfied by the financial incentives of the June deal and seeks the bigger prize of permanent control over the Strait of Hormuz. Vectors for escalation include disrupting Red Sea traffic via Houthi allies in Yemen or strikes on regional energy infrastructure. 

  • American options for reopening the strait through force alone are limited. The US is working under a limited timeframe, with the depletion of oil and munition stockpiles making conflict more painful the longer it continues, and a campaign to fully reopen the strait would require a far greater commitment than Trump is likely to authorize. 

  • Likely targets for new US strikes therefore include Iran’s remaining nuclear facilities and stockpiles of ballistic missiles as it seeks to weaken Iran enough to shift the balance of power in future negotiations. 

  • For more, please see Iran, MENA Crisis: Escalation likely as Iran sees path to Hormuz control, 15 July 2026.

Trump will use primetime speech to push election interference narrative. President Trump will reportedly focus on Chinese interference in the 2020 election in his primetime speech tonight, which could include the declassification of intelligence on the issue.  

  • For Trump, this will fill two roles. It will keep up his longstanding narrative that the result of the 2020 election was illegitimate, a narrative that has supporters among congressional Republicans (see quote below). It will also maintain pressure—albeit indirectly—on reluctant Republican members of Congress to pass the SAVE America Act on voter ID.  

  • Calling out China as having conducted election interference through influence campaigns is very unlikely to rock the boat on the US-China relationship; China is unlikely to react unless there is a tangible threat to Chinese interests.  

  • From the perspective of the conduct of the midterms, two developments would be most notable: if Trump proactively signals any intent to contest the results of close races if there is pre-election evidence of foreign interference, or if Trump denies the legitimacy of voting infrastructure, particularly voting machines, in Democratic-led states. Both would presage post-election legal fights that could, in tail-risk scenarios, lead to uncertainty about certification of election results in November.  

Blanche will win confirmation and serve as a Trump loyalist. Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche faced tough questioning from both parties yesterday in his Senate confirmation hearing yesterday, but he remains likely to be confirmed by the Senate to lead the Department of Justice (DOJ) on a full-time basis.   

  • Senator Thom Tillis’s (R-NC) remark that he is leaning toward a Yes vote is key: Tillis has been a key skeptic of Blanche’s confirmation over concerns regarding the White House’s “anti-weaponization” fund.  

  • Blanche will likely serve as a Trump loyalist in the role. Trump’s three previous attorneys general have all clashed with the White House, leading to their eventual departures., Blanche has a closer personal relationship with Trump—Blanche served as Trump’s defense attorney in his 2023 New York trial—that both puts him on stronger footing with the White House and signals a closer alignment.  

  • That means Blanche will keep up Trump’s legal campaigns against his political enemies, both continuing current actions and initiating new ones. Democrats will make what they will characterize as the politicization of federal law enforcement a major campaign talking point in the midterms and thereafter, but that likely won’t force the administration into a reversal.  

Quote of the Day 

"There were concerns expressed not only about Georgia, but about other states." - Sen. Cynthia Lummis (R-WY) in response to a reporter's question about the legitimacy of Georgia Democratic Senators Jon Ossoff and Raphael Warnock's elections. Ahead of President Trump's national address on election interference, this quote signals that GOP members are lining up to support the President's criticisms of the 2020 election and to potentially sow doubt regarding electoral losses in the 2026 midterms.

Explanation of Political Trajectories (ie, “Trajectories”)

Eurasia Group's (EG) Political Trajectories are indicators of the net impact of political factors on the macro business environment in a country over specific timeframes. For each country, two Trajectories are provided, a short-term (6 months) and a long term assessment (24 months). Trajectories are assigned by Eurasia Group’s country analysts through a structured qualitative process. Each Trajectory represents a net assessment, indicating how political changes in a country impact the macro business environment, defined as the economic and investment climate in the country. Once initiated, Trajectories are updated both periodically and as events warrant. Trajectories may be suspended from time to time if there is no active analyst opinion or analyst coverage, but the opinion or coverage is expected to resume. Trajectories can be:

Positive: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a positive impact on the macro business environment

Neutral: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a neutral or negligible impact on the macro business environment

Negative: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a negative impact on the macro business environment

Disclaimers: Trajectories are solely for informational purposes and should not be relied upon as investment advice. Trajectories do not indicate asset price movements, and have no value in forecasting market prices. Trajectories are based on data and analysis we deem to be reliable; they are not guaranteed as to accuracy or completeness. The Trajectories indicate the political outlook in a single country, over time. Trajectories do not address sector-specific or city/regional political risks that are often different than the country-level outlook. Trajectories represent an analyst’s net qualitative assessment of a country's political outlook. EG sales, analytic staff and products may provide analysis reflecting different opinions than those expressed by the Trajectories.
