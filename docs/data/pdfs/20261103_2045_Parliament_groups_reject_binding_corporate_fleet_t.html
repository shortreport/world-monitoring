Parliament groups reject binding corporate fleet targets
By Jordyn Dahl · Nov 3, 2026, 12:44 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — A compromise proposal approved by leading groups in the European Parliament rejects the Commission’s binding targets for electrifying corporate fleets, according to a draft obtained by POLITICO.

The Commission proposal set country-specific targets based on GDP to electrify corporate fleets — vehicles owned or leased by companies for business purposes.

But the Parliament compromise — reached by the European People’s Party, Renew and the Socialists and Democrats — would make the targets “aspirational” instead of binding.

EU countries have similarly balked at the mandatory targets, with a blocking minority in the Council of the EU holding up negotiations. With both Council and Parliament signaling opposition, that means the Commission proposal is in trouble.

Parliament’s environment, transport and internal market committees are voting on the package on Wednesday. A plenary vote is expected in December to establish Parliament’s final position for interinstitutional negotiations.

The corporate fleets regulation is part of a broader Automotive Package put forward by the Commission in December 2025 that will reform emission targets for automakers, along with cutting regulation through the automotive omnibus. The Greens signed onto the corporate fleets compromise but did not support the other agreements.

The most-watched aspect of the omnibus is the utility factor, which estimates the share of driving done on electric power and is used to calculate a plug-in hybrid’s official emissions. The current measurement is based on lab data, while the Commission wants to use real-world data, which shows drivers use the combustion engine far more frequently than the lab numbers indicate.

A separate Parliament platform proposal from October, also obtained by POLITICO, would freeze the utility factor until Dec. 31, 2029. The automotive industry has lobbied heavily for that to happen, as carmakers would be able to sell more plug-in hybrids while still meeting emissions reduction targets. A Franco-German compromise reached last month similarly proposed freezing the calculation until 2030.

Parliament’s draft agreement would freeze the utility factor only for plug-in hybrids with an electric range of 120 kilometers or more, while the Franco-German deal wants it to apply to vehicles with a range of 80 kilometers.

The Parliament deal would also require the factor to be updated each year after 2030 so real-world emissions are taken into account in the same way as for internal combustion engine cars.

As POLITICO previously reported, the centrist coalition in Parliament has already reached an agreement on the reforming the EU’s 2035 de facto combustion engine ban. The deal proposes lowering the emissions target from the current 100% reduction from 2021 levels to 87.5%.

The regulation has milestones along the way to 2035, which are meant to help automakers keep pace with emissions reductions. The current law requires automakers to cut emissions by 50% by 2030, but the Commission proposed weakening that by allowing automakers to calculate average emissions over three years, from 2030 to 2032. Spreading the number of years makes it easier to reach the goal.

The Parliament compromise would water that down even further by allowing automakers to average the emissions over a five-year period instead — from 2030 to 2035 — according to the final CO2 text also obtained by POLITICO.
