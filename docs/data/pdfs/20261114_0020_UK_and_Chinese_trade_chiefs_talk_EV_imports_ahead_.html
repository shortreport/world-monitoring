UK and Chinese trade chiefs talk EV imports ahead of Brexit summit
By Graham Lanktree · Nov 13, 2026, 4:19 PM · View in your browser

LONDON — Britain and China’s trade chiefs discussed Chinese investment and electric-vehicle imports, as the U.K. faces EU pressure to impose tariffs on the sector ahead of a key Brexit ‘reset’ summit next week.

Chinese Minister of Commerce Wang Wentao spoke with British Business and Trade Secretary Jonathan Reynolds on Wednesday, a day after the U.K. trade chief’s trip to Brussels for a round of high-level talks with EU counterparts.

U.K. Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Thursday that Britain shares the EU’s Chinese import “problem”, hinting he is open to aligning with EU-style tariffs of up to 35% on Chinese-made electric vehicles to secure access to Brussels’ Made in Europe scheme.

“On Made in Europe, Europe’s issue is Britain’s issue, which is obviously, particularly imports from China, causing the problem,” Burnham told reporters.

Jaguar Land Rover has warned that the U.K.’s exclusion from the EU industrial scheme — which would allow British carmakers to access public support across the bloc, including EV grants and corporate fleet incentives — could put £2.5 billion to £3.5 billion in annual sales across the industry at risk. The value exceeds the U.K.’s total annual car exports to China.

China, however, has threatened to retaliate against the U.K. if it raises tariffs on Chinese vehicles.

During Wentao’s video call with Reynolds “at the latter’s request” on Wednesday, the “two sides exchanged views on trade and economic issues of mutual concern, such as electric vehicles,” according to a translation of a readout from China’s Ministry of Commerce.

The two officials also discussed strengthening trade cooperation between the countries, a U.K. government readout said. “This included discussion of the U.K.’s Industrial Strategy target of increasing U.K. auto production and the role of international investment,” the readout stated.

A 2024 study calculated that Chinese EV makers received roughly $231 billion in state subsidies between 2009 and 2023. During the same year, Brussels slapped tariffs of up to 35% on battery electric vehicles from China.

China’s share of the U.K.’s battery electric and plug-in hybrid car market has surged this year. China’s market share of plug-in hybrids jumped to 51.4% in the first three quarters of 2026, from 22.7% in 2025. Battery electric vehicles rose to 32.9% of the market from 26.1% in the same period, according to data from Britain’s Society of Motor Manufacturers and Traders.
