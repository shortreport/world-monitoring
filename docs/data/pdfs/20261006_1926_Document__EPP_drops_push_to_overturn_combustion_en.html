Document: EPP drops push to overturn combustion engine ban
By Jordyn Dahl, Rasmus Buchsteiner · Oct 6, 2026, 12:24 PM · View in your browser

The European People’s Party is backing off from its campaign promise to overturn the EU’s 2035 zero-emission mandate for automakers that acted as a de facto combustion engine ban, according to a position paper obtained by POLITICO.

The European Commission’s proposed Automotive Package replaced the ban in 2025 with a target to reduce emissions by 90% for all cars sold after 2035, after strong pressure from the EPP and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz.

The party has now backed down from its earlier pledge. The Commission’s proposal “provides a solid basis for technological neutrality,” the paper says. “The task now is to translate this into clear, effective legislation that gives Europe’s automotive industry the certainty it needs.”

But the EPP is still pushing to further weaken the emission targets.

“Environmental and climate legislation is important but must not come at the expense of millions of well-paid jobs and the survival of an entire industry in Europe,” the paper says.

The automotive sector is cutting thousands of jobs and shuttering factories in Germany, prompting an economic and political crisis in the country as the far right uses the turmoil to gain more voters.

The EPP is hosting a car summit Tuesday evening at the Schaeffler plant in Haguenau, France. Party chief Manfred Weber is attending, along with Matthias Zink, the CEO of Schaeffler and president of the EU car supplier lobby CLEPA, according to a draft agenda also obtained by POLITICO.

Weber is meeting earlier in the day with Mercedes-Benz CEO Ola Källenius, who is also the current head of car lobby ACEA.

Negotiations are underway on the package across the EU institutions, with a vote in plenary scheduled for Nov. 23.
