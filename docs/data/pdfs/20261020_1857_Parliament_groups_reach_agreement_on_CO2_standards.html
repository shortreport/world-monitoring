Parliament groups reach agreement on CO2 standards for cars
By Jordyn Dahl · Oct 20, 2026, 11:56 AM · View in your browser

The European People’s Party struck a deal with the Socialists and Democrats and Renew groups in the European Parliament that would lower the EU’s 2035 emissions reduction target for automakers to 87.5% from 2021 levels, three people familiar with the negotiations told POLITICO.

According to the Parliament deal reached on Monday night, 5% of the emissions reduction would be unconditional and 7.5% would have to be offset with green steel — a concession to the S&D.

Last year in its Automotive Package, the European Commission proposed lowering the 2035 target from an effective combustion engine ban with a 100% emissions reduction to a 90% cut. Automakers would be required to offset the extra emissions with investments in alternative fuels and green steel.

The EPP is the lead negotiator on the CO2 file, but talks were at a standstill until Paris and Berlin came out with a joint stance on the regulation earlier this month. The two capitals proposed a 90% unconditional 2035 target.

Like the Franco-German deal, the Parliament agreement would maintain the utility factor for plug-in hybrids at its current level based on laboratory data until Jan. 1, 2030.

The factor would only be frozen for plug-in hybrids with a minimum electric range of 120 kilometers. The French-German deal proposed freezing the factor for cars with a range of 80 kilometers.

The factor calculates plug-in hybrid emissions based on how often a vehicle’s electric motor is used versus its combustion engine. Keeping the calculation based on laboratory data rather than real-world results — which show that drivers rely more on the engine than on the electric motor — will allow automakers to sell more plug-in hybrids and reach the broader emission targets.

The Commission proposal would give automakers producing small electric vehicles a 1.3 super credit — which makes a qualifying car count as more than one vehicle — with the initiative going into effect in 2034.

Just like the Franco-German compromise, the Parliament deal would make a 1.2 super credit available to an EV of any size, so long as it meets “Made in Europe” requirements. Small “Made in Europe” EVs, meanwhile, would get a 1.4 super credit.

Manufacturers would be eligible for the credits once the legislation is signed, likely in 2027; there would be no time limit for the credits in the Parliament agreement.

The EPP scored one of its biggest asks: the creation of a new vehicle category called VEEF, or Vehicles running Exclusively on Eligible Fuels. The party — and especially Italian MEP Massimiliano Salini, who is the lead negotiator on the file — wants alternative fuels to be a bigger part of the solution in lowering emissions.

The deal was reached in the ENVI Committee, which is leading negotiations for Parliament. The legislature will need to sign off on the agreement before it becomes Parliament’s official position.
