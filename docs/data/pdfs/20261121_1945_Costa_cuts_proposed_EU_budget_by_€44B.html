Costa cuts proposed EU budget by €44B
By Gregorio Sorgi · Nov 21, 2026, 11:44 AM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — António Costa proposed shaving another €44 billion off the EU’s planned seven-year budget, according to a fresh set of numbers unveiled on Saturday.

For the first time since the budget negotiations began last year, EU regional spending, known as cohesion policy, is a target for reductions. The European Council president proposed reducing it by €8 billion compared with the last proposal, while farmers’ subsidies remained untouched.

This is a major setback for a coalition of 17 countries from Eastern and Southern Europe — including Poland, Italy and Spain — who rejected potential cuts to cohesion policy only a few days ago. However, the proposal is likely to be welcomed by a German-led group of Northern countries pushing for cuts across the board.

“This proposal is fit for tackling the challenges we are facing in our societies, and gives the
European Union the necessary capacity to act,” Costa said on Saturday morning.

Costa, who is guiding discussions over the 2028-2034 budget which needs to be agreed by all EU governments, suggested the reductions, which are based on current prices, from the previous proposal presented by Ireland, in its role holding the rotating presidency of the Council of the EU, last month.

Overall, the cuts amount to a reduction in size of the budget by €204 billion, or 10.2%, from the European Commission’s original proposal in July last year.

In another major development, Costa suggested introducing a new tax on large digital companies such as Google and Meta to help finance the budget over the coming years.

In recent years, Brussels held back from levying this tax for fear retaliation from Washington, but the Republicans’ bruising defeat in the midterm elections earlier this month created an opening.

These ideas set the scene for fraught discussions among the EU’s 27 leaders during a summit next week. Even then the agreed budget will have to be approved by the European Parliament.

Despite governments disagreeing over the size of the budget, Costa is confident that governments will reach a deal among themselves during a summit of EU leaders starting on Dec. 17.

“I believe this proposal can bring us closer to a final agreement. And I will continue to work with all leaders to reach that objective,” he said.

Big cuts

EU governments are split between those that want a smaller budget focused on defense and competitiveness, and those that think it should be larger and spent on traditional priorities such as farming and cohesion.

Costa proposed modest cuts to cohesion — which had been spared from previous rounds of cuts due to opposition from many governments — to appease Germany and its allies, the so-called frugals.

However, budget savings also came from foreign aid which was cut by €7 billion, and competitiveness which was axed by €20 billion.

One of the few budget items to receive additional funding was a rainy-day crisis fund that was downsized by Ireland last month.

This is seen as a concession to the Commission, which supports more spending flexibility.

Going after big tech

Costa proposed new taxes and other adjustments to generate €60 billion annually for the EU budget.

Crucially, he replaced an unpopular corporate tax proposal known as CORE, which was put forward by the Commission last year, with a levy on tech multinationals with a turnover exceeding €750 million per year globally and over €50 million in the EU. POLITICO first reported the move earlier this week.

The proposed digital levy will cover online advertising, digital intermediation services and user-generated data for companies.

Most governments opposed CORE — a lump sum on companies’ revenues exceeding €100 million — over fears that it would undermine EU competitiveness.

To generate additional resources for the budget, Costa also suggested increasing two largely uncontroversial taxes on non-collected plastic and electronic waste that flows directly to the bloc’s budget by levying an additional 25 cents per kilo.

The proposal also envisions a higher share of a carbon imports’ tax that flows to the EU cash pot to 100% from the current 75%, and exploring levies on crypto firms and national spectrum auctions.

In a further concession, Costa also suggested phasing in two taxes on tobacco products and polluting firms, known as the Emissions Trading System, for countries that are overwhelmingly affected.

Costa argued that the new proposed taxes will keep national contributions to Brussels under control.

“With this proposal we have managed to come very close to the current level of national contributions,” he said.
