Right-wing revolt threatens to bog down EU budget
By Martina Sapio, Aude van den Hove · Nov 24, 2026, 12:41 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — The far-right Patriots for Europe group plans to challenge the European Parliament’s negotiating mandates on several files in the EU’s next budget, according to four EU officials with knowledge of the discussions — a move that could drag out discussions on the seven-year plan in the chamber.

The challenge would stop the files, which have already been discussed at a committee level, from going straight to talks with national governments. Instead, the mandates would need a plenary vote, opening the door to amendments that could rehash positions committees had already settled.

“The merger of several programs [in several budget proposals] is accompanied by an increasing concentration of allocation authority in the hands of the Commission,” a PfE official told POLITICO. Moreover, most of the PfE’s amendments in the budget have been rejected, they said.

The official added that “several delegations outside of the PfE” have told them they would support the challenge.

Another official said the Patriots are set to target the European Competitiveness Fund, the Connecting Europe Facility and FP10, the research program that would succeed Horizon Europe. A third official said the group was likely to challenge all the mandates, but that it was not yet clear whether other groups or lawmakers would back the move.

The Parliament fight comes as EU leaders prepare to discuss the budget at a summit on Thursday, following the negotiating box presented by European Council President António Costa on Nov. 21. A drawn-out plenary fight would slow Parliament’s side of the process just as governments try to move toward a deal.

Parliament’s rules allow a political group or lawmakers representing at least 72 members to request a plenary vote on a decision to start negotiations. They have until midnight Tuesday to do so, according to a Parliament notice. The Patriots have about 86 members.

POLITICO asked the Patriots for comment but did not immediately receive a response.

Marianne Gros contributed reporting.
