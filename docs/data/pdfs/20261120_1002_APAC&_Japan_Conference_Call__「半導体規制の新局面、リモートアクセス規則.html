Upcoming Conference Call

APAC& Japan Conference Call: 「半導体規制の新局面、リモートアクセス規則とNvidiaを巡る余波がアジアに突きつける課題」

この度のユーラシアグループのカンファレンス・コールでは、地政学テクノロジー部門責任者のヘザー・ウェスト、同部門ディレクターのシャオメン・ルー、ならびにグローバル・マクロ部門ディレクターのジェラルド・ディピッポを迎え、米国の新たな半導体輸出規制による中国のAIチップ調達への制限、東南アジアのデータセンター拠点への負担、そしてAPEC後の米中緊張下でアジアのテック業界の勢力図がどう変わりうるのかについて解説いたします。

Wednesday, 25 November at 10:00 am JST / 9:00 am SGT / 12:00 pm AEDT
Submit questions in advance here.

Topics:

  • 中国のAIチップへのアクセス:新たなリモートアクセス規則と、国防授権法(NDAA)に付随する半導体関連法案が、中国の計算力(多くは海外のデータセンターを経由)へのアクセスをどのように制限するか。また、トランプ氏に取り締まりを緩和する余地がどの程度残されているか
  • Nvidiaスキャンダルと北京の対応:2027年に向けた3つのシナリオ──管理された安定の継続、規制の法制化、あるいはチップ製造装置への規制強化が休戦を深刻なリスクに晒す可能性──、そしてAPEC後に中国がどのような報復に出る可能性が高いか
  • AIインフラ:データセンターを禁止するのではなく条件を課す米国各州の対応、ASEAN各国政府による承認プロセスの厳格化、そしてCHIPS法の資金、リショアリング(国内回帰)、半導体関税の現状
  • アジア全体でのデカップリング:中国・日本・韓国の半導体メーカーおよび装置メーカー間の勝者と敗者、米中の間で板挟みになるデータセンター拠点、そしてファーウェイとの5G・6Gを巡る競争
  • China’s access to AI chips: how the new remote access rule and the chip bills attached to the defence bill will tighten China’s access to computing power, much of which runs through data centres abroad, and how much room Trump keeps to soften enforcement
  • The Nvidia scandal and Beijing’s response: three scenarios for 2027 (managed stability, controls written into law, or curbs on chipmaking tools that put the truce at serious risk), and how China is likely to retaliate after APEC
  • AI infrastructure: why US states will put conditions on data centres rather than ban them, how ASEAN governments are tightening approvals, and where CHIPS Act funding, reshoring and semiconductor tariffs stand
  • Decoupling across Asia: winners and losers among Chinese, Japanese and Korean chipmakers and toolmakers, data-centre hubs caught between Washington and Beijing, and the contest with Huawei over 5G and 6G networks

About Heather West

Heather West is a Washington, DC-based expert on technology policy who has spent her career translating between the people who build technology and the people who need to understand it. At Eurasia Group, she advises clients on the political and regulatory risks shaping the technology landscape, with a focus on AI, cybersecurity, data regulation, digital infrastructure, competition and sovereignty, and global regulatory strategy.

Heather brings twenty years of experience across the private sector, civil society, and the technology industry. She has held senior policy roles at Venable, Meta, Mozilla, Cloudflare, and Google, where she shaped policy and product direction for emerging technologies in partnership with executives, product teams, and global policy leads. Throughout her career, she has also worked with policymakers to help shape the future of tech sector regulation. She began her career at the Center for Democracy and Technology. She holds fellowships with the Center for European Policy Analysis, the National Security Institute at George Mason University's Antonin Scalia Law School, and the Canadian Chamber Future of Business Centre.

She is a recovering computer scientist, with dual degrees in Computer Science and Cognitive Science from Wellesley College and a Diploma in Global Business from Saïd Business School at the University of Oxford.
