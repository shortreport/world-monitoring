Booking CEO tells Brussels to ‘take the handcuffs off’ as AI rivals move into travel
By Jacob Parry, Eliza Gkritsi · Nov 18, 2026, 3:51 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — Booking CEO Glenn Fogel wants the EU to loosen its digital rules on his company, which he says is vital for one of Europe’s few tech champions to compete with U.S. and Chinese rivals.

“Take the handcuffs off Booking so we can compete fairly in a global world,” Fogel said Wednesday when asked in an interview in Brussels whether companies such as OpenAI should face the same rules as Booking.

The Amsterdam-based company is the only European player designated as a gatekeeper under the Digital Markets Act, the EU law that imposes extra obligations on the biggest digital platforms. It is also designated a Very Large Online Platform under the Digital Services Act, another major European regulation.

Fogel said AI agents were turning travel into a battleground for companies far larger than its primary website Booking.com, like OpenAI, Google and Meta, which are “not held back” by EU rules.

“We are the ones being regulated more strongly,” he said before a scheduled meeting with Henna Virkkunen, the EU Commissioner overseeing tech. “We are restricted as a platform selling travel, they are going out there to do that, and Europe is doing nothing, nothing.”

Fogel said his main concern about AI is “that our competitors are able to go faster than we are because of the different regulatory schemes that we are held to and the others are not.”

Since November 2024, EU rules have barred the company from stopping hotels from offering lower prices elsewhere and limited how it combines customer data across its services. Booking was also blocked from buying eTraveli, a Swedish online flight-booking website, in 2023. The company announced Wednesday that it is appealing an EU court ruling that had upheld the veto.

Old rules, new rules

Both Google and Meta are designated as gatekeepers under the DMA and VLOPs under the DSA. OpenAI recently entered the fold of EU rules as a Very Large Online Search Engine and must also follow the AI Act.

Meta is facing multiple investigations under the DSA, and in some cases the EU Commission has accused the platform of breaking the platforms’ rules. Booking is also being investigated for allegedly failing to handle the risk of fraud.

Asked about the Digital Fairness Act, proposed last week, the Commission’s new consumer protection initiative covering online practices, Fogel said he had not looked closely at it but urged caution.

“Before we create a new rule, let’s check and make sure the old rules don’t already apply,” he said.

He described the accumulation of EU rules, from the DMA and DSA to national digital services taxes, as “a death by a thousand cuts” and warned that “at some point, it makes it less attractive to continue to build business in Europe.”

Booking.com is the flagship of U.S.-listed Booking Holdings, which also owns Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK and OpenTable. Fogel, a former investment banker, joined the company in 2000, led the acquisitions that created Booking.com and has run the group since 2017.

Despite its Nasdaq listing, Fogel said most of its staff are in Europe and that it pays more tax in the Netherlands than in any other country. “Where somebody lists their stock is completely irrelevant,” he said.

Fogel said there is a place for regulation. “You can’t have a market without rules,” he said. “The only thing I absolutely believe is right is that if you are competitors, you should all be treated by the same rules.”
