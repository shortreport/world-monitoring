Irish Council presidency proposes stricter emissions target for automakers
By Jordyn Dahl · Nov 5, 2026, 11:02 AM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — The Irish Council presidency’s proposed compromise on the Automotive Package would keep a more stringent 2035 emissions reduction target for automakers, according to a steering note seen by POLITICO ahead of Thursday’s working party meeting.

The proposal would set a 90% emissions reduction target for 2035 compared to 2021 levels instead of the current 100% reduction. Automakers would have to offset extra emissions through investments in alternative fuels and low-carbon aluminum and steel.

It’s close to the European Commission’s proposal for a 90% reduction but which limited offsets to alternative fuels and green steel.

The position differs from the proposal by the leading committees in the European Parliament, which earlier this week backed an 87.5% reduction target with various offsets.

It’s also firmer than the compromise deal between France and Germany that proposed a 90% unconditional target.

Unlike both Parliament’s draft and the Franco-German deal, the Irish presidency compromise would not establish a new category for vehicles running exclusively on alternative fuels.

That is tied to positions on the utility factor — which estimates how often a driver uses the electrical motor versus the combustion engine in a plug-in hybrid. The current measurements are based on lab data, which overestimate how much the motor is used. The Commission wants to use real-world data showing the combustion engine is used more frequently.

In exchange for not establishing a new vehicle category, the Council deal would freeze the utility actor until January 2030 — mirroring the view of the Parliament committees.
