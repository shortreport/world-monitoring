Document: EU negotiators make headway on military mobility’s emergency mechanism
By Martina Sapio · Sep 30, 2026, 2:05 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — Despite a slow start, EU countries and the European Parliament appear to be making headway on some of the most politically contentious parts of the bloc’s military mobility plan, according to a late-September negotiating document obtained by POLITICO.

Earlier September documents, seen by POLITICO, suggested governments were still grappling with major political questions, including the emergency mechanism, designed to relax normal transport rules to facilitate military movements during crises, and the Solidarity Pool, designed to identify and share scarce dual-use assets.

But the negotiating text dated Sept. 18 marks agreed provisions on the emergency mechanism, pointing to progress on one of the proposal’s main sticking points.

Under the compromise, when the emergency mechanism is activated, countries and infrastructure operators would give military transports priority access to dual-use roads, railways, ports, inland waterways, airports, airspace and other transport infrastructure. National coordinators would help alert operators and coordinate requests.

The text also seeks to limit disruption to civilian transport, saying that traffic management and coordination should come first, with cancellations of civilian services a last resort. Countries could still prioritize rescue, police and other operations needed to protect life, critical infrastructure or public security when timely military access cannot be ensured.

The military mobility regulation is designed to make it faster and easier to move troops, military equipment and supplies across the EU.

EU governments and lawmakers are aiming to reach a deal on the regulation in December, according to a tentative timetable seen by POLITICO.
