DOCUMENT: Irish Presidency floats ETS safeguard for international credits
By Ben Makuch · Nov 16, 2026, 2:00 PM · View in your browser

The Irish Presidency has proposed a contingency plan for the EU’s future use of international carbon credits in the Emissions Trading System, according to a draft compromise obtained by POLITICO.

Under the proposal, the linear reduction factor — the annual rate at which the emissions cap shrinks — would tighten if those credits fail to materialize or enough “high-quality” credits aren’t available from outside the bloc. That would leave European industry carrying a greater burden of emissions reductions in the coming decade.

“A linear reduction factor from 2036 of 1.7% should apply where the expected volume of purchased international credits is 260 [million],” the Irish write; “of 1.9% where that volume is 208 [million]; of 2.1% where that volume is 156 [million]; of 2.3% where that volume is 104 [million]; of 2.5% where that volume is 52 [million]; and of 2.7% where that volume is less than 52 [million].”

While this is the most advanced compromise the Irish have circulated to the Council of the European Union so far, the text largely aligns with the European Commission’s July review but diverges slightly on international credits.

The Irish text would “initiate” and flesh out a 2031-35 pilot phase for international credits before full deployment in 2036, whereas the Commission said such a pilot “may be considered.”

There are also changes to the conditionality attached to free allowances, with 80% released once a decarbonization plan is approved and the remaining 20% linked to the start of construction on an eligible project. Meanwhile, sectors covered by by the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism would be given a longer transition period, with the phaseout of free allowances extending until 2037.
