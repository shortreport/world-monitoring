Document: ETS fight heats up in Parliament as Greens counter EPP proposal
By Ben Makuch, Tommaso Lecca · Oct 6, 2026, 2:36 PM · View in your browser

The Greens in the European Parliament want to scale back free carbon allowances for industry and to prioritize electrification against what the EU executive has proposed in its overhaul of the bloc’s carbon market, according to draft amendments obtained by POLITICO.

The group’s biggest clash with Parliament’s lead negotiator on the Emissions Trading System, Peter Liese of the center-right European People’s Party, centers on the linear-reduction factor — the rate at which carbon permits disappear each year from the ETS.

The left-leaning group wants to keep the LRF unchanged at a 4.4% annual decrease until 2035, and 2.7% after that. Liese’s proposal, meanwhile, asks for a 3.4% LRF from 2031 to 2035 and then 2.3% from 2036 onwards.

The Commission originally proposed 3.7% from 2031 to 2035, then 1.7% from 2036 to 2040.

Michael Bloss, the Greens lead on the ETS, told POLITICO that this would punish early movers.

“Steelmakers, cement producers and utilities took Brussels at its word. They invested billions on the strength of today’s rules, and those rules must hold until 2035,” he said.

A key battleground is how ETS revenues are spent, with the EPP proposing 75% goes back to ETS industries to fund decarbonization. But the Greens wants those funds to be steered towards intensifying electrification, and propose renaming the proposed Industrial Decarbonization Bank the “Industrial Electrification and Decarbonization Bank.”

On aviation, the Greens propose expanding carbon levies on “all flights departing from an airport situated in the European Economic Area.” The current scope only applies to flights within the European Economic Area.

The Commission’s July proposal extends the ETS to international flights bound for destinations within 5,000 kilometers of Frankfurt starting in 2029. The Greens’ proposed blanket extension “should apply from 2027.”
