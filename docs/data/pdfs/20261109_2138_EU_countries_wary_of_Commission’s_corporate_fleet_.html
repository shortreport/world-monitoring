EU countries wary of Commission’s corporate fleet data collection push
By Jordyn Dahl · Nov 9, 2026, 1:37 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — Member states are worried about meeting reporting requirements under the European Commission’s proposed corporate fleets regulation, according to an Irish Council presidency steering note seen by POLITICO.

The proposed regulation would set mandatory targets for countries to electrify their corporate fleets — vehicles purchased through public funds or which are bought with government subsidies.

But capitals say they either do not have access to the required vehicle and company data to calculate their progress toward any mandatory targets or that the effort to cross-reference vehicle data across the different registries would be an “undue burden.”

The Irish presidency is proposing that the Commission create a forum for best practices among member states on the most effective way to collect and report the required vehicle data, the steering note said.

National diplomats will share their experiences and potential costs of data collection efforts at a Tuesday working party to discuss the topic, as well as giving further views on what scope the requirements should take.

Although the Commission put forward the mandatory targets, there is a blocking minority in Council against the measure. The European Parliament’s draft position has also scrapped binding targets in favor of aspirational ones.
