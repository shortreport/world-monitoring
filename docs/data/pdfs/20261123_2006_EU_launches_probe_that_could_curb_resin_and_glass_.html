EU launches probe that could curb resin and glass fiber imports
By Koen Verhelst · Nov 23, 2026, 12:05 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — The European Commission has launched an investigation that could lead to restrictions on imports of resins and glass fiber products used in the automotive, defense and construction industries.

The so-called safeguard investigation applies to imports from all countries and follows months of behind-the-scenes preparations. China is fueling much of the surge in imports, including by investing in production capacity in countries that have trade deals with the EU.

The probe highlights a new approach to EU trade defense, bringing longer industrial supply chains under scrutiny in a single investigation covering 10 products.

Imports of the products under investigation rose by 29% between 2019 and 2025, according to the Commission. Estimated global production capacity exceeds EU consumption by 450% percent.

Alongside three varieties of glass fiber reinforcements, the probe covers resins and the chemical precursors and other inputs used to produce them.

The EU can impose provisional import restrictions once it has sufficient evidence, but would need the support of a majority of member countries to introduce measures lasting four years.

The investigation is the EU’s fourth safeguard proceeding this year — a record. The others have targeted transformer steel, hybrid vehicles and PET plastics.
