Lula’s search for legacy is the wildcard for his fourth term
18 Nov 2026 08:00 EST
Discover more on the Portal
 

summary
  • Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva’s search for legacy is the wildcard for his fourth term, with the most likely outcome avoiding a hard landing in 2027 but not stagflation.
  • The fight over Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook will likely be a source of volatility in White House-Fed relations next year, but it is unlikely to stop the Fed’s tightening cycle.
  • Ghana’s government is likely to increase capital spending ahead of the 2028 elections, but Finance Minister Cassiel Ato Forson’s presidential ambitions will help contain fiscal risks.
  • Negotiations between Panama’s government and First Quantum will likely reopen the Cobre Panama copper mine, with 70% odds of a restart in the first half of 2027.
  • Countries and sectors in this edition of the Daily Brief include the US, Brazil, Ghana, Panama, resources, and geoeconomics.

the top line
Brazil—Search for legacy is the wildcard for Lula 4.0
  • Lula enters his fourth term with the mood between Brasilia and the private sector unusually far apart. He will introduce more fiscal measures, but they will fall short of creating room for lower interest rates, and policy risks will rise when a slowdown drives a potentially sharp drop in his popularity.
  • The wildcard is how Lula’s search for legacy, centered on inequality, sovereignty, and democracy, shapes his final term. In the bullish case, he tackles fiscal challenges and a judicial overhaul. In the bearish case, he doubles down on redistributive policies, as he loses popular support.
  • The most likely scenario is somewhere in between. A finance minister, most likely Vice President Geraldo Alckmin, will pursue constitutional changes to mandatory spending rules. That will keep growth from a hard landing in 2027 but not prevent stagflation that keeps interest rates high.

Geoeconomics—Renminbi mix creates trade and political challenges
  • China's renminbi is strong against a basket of trade partners' currencies, but around the middle of long-term ranges against the US dollar. This mix is unfavorable for China in both ways, providing little help to the struggling economy by reducing competitiveness in labor-intensive sectors whilst also providing little comfort to a US administration seeking to rebalance the bilateral trade relationship.
  • Markets have little priced in for typical agricultural purchase announcements ahead of the APEC summit. US soybean prices remain towards the lower end of ranges seen over the last six months.

US—Fight over Cook will not prevent Fed tightening cycle
  • Cook’s lawyer said she won’t attend the White House’s 14 December hearing on mortgage-fraud allegations, a signal she will contest any dismissal. With the White House apparently following the letter of the Supreme Court’s rule on the legally required process, the fight is likely to revolve around questions of “cause,” which is no obvious win for Cook.
  • A fight over Cook, combined with White House attacks on Fed board members for rate hikes, will likely be a source of volatility in relations between the White House and the Fed next year. Markets may be underpricing this risk, seeing the dispute as a repeat of President Donald Trump’s failed attempt to fire Cook last year.
  • These tensions are unlikely to stop the Fed’s tightening cycle. With Democrats controlling the Senate, Trump probably will not be able to fill vacancies with a friendlier governor, and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is unlikely to back off the current rate path.

Ghana—Finance minister’s political ambitions will help contain 2027 fiscal risks
  • The government is likely to increase capital spending ahead of the 2028 elections: by frontloading fiscal consolidation in its first two years, it has created the room to double capital expenditure in 2027 and increase public wages by 15%. That said, the low starting deficit position—coupled with Forson’s presidential ambitions—will contain fiscal risks.
  • Ghana's heavy dependence on gold exports for foreign-exchange earnings means a sustained decline in gold prices is the main risk in 2027.
  • Meanwhile, the government’s reluctance to accumulate new external debt following its recent restructuring and improved domestic financing capacity makes an immediate return to international capital markets unlikely.

eg in brief

Panama, resources—Negotiations will likely reopen mine with First Quantum as operator: President Jose Raul Mulino announced formal negotiations with First Quantum (FQM) over the future of the Cobre Panama mine, saying any agreement must include a closure plan financed by the company. EG puts 70% odds on a reopening in the first half of 2027, though a delay into the second half remains possible. The state will likely retain the concession, with FQM as operator, allowing the administration to avoid repealing the mining moratorium. In line with expectations, the announcement also benefited Panama’s credit rating, with Moody's revising its credit outlook to stable from negative. Social pushback is a wildcard, but protests are unlikely to derail a reopening entirely. Read more here. Reach out to discuss: latinamerica@eurasiagroup.net.

Brazil—Senate impasse tests limits of revenue-raising measures: Restrictions on companies’ use of tax credits to offset tax liabilities remain the main risk to the fiscal package’s impact. The administration has yet to secure the 41 votes needed to reject an amendment that would scrap them. The administration faces 40% odds of defeat, which would cost 12.8-18.3 billion reais in expected revenue and undercut efforts to balance the 2027 budget. The administration is offering a compromise on Kandir Law transfers to states to ease negotiations without weakening the fiscal adjustment. Rapporteur Senator Eduardo Braga is negotiating language that would explicitly allow earlier repayment of deferred transfers, but no deal has been reached. Lower-house approval this week remains at risk even if the senate concludes its vote today. Speaker Hugo Motta is abroad until 19 November, and any changes to the bill would lower Planning Minister Bruno Moretti’s already low odds of becoming finance minister. Read more here.
