Burnham and Macron agree to ‘find a way forward’ on ‘Made in Europe’ rules
By Sophie Inge · Sep 3, 2026, 5:00 PM · View in your browser

LONDON — Prime Minister Andy Burnham and French President Emmanuel Macron have agreed to “work together” to protect U.K. and French interests amid concerns over the impact of the EU’s “Made in Europe” agenda.

The leaders met on Thursday morning in Downing Street on the second day of Macron’s visit to the U.K. to mark the opening of an exhibition of the Bayeux Tapestry at the British Museum.

In a statement following the leaders’ first bilateral meeting, a Downing Street spokeswoman said they discussed the U.K.’s drive to get closer to Europe, with the prime minister voicing concerns that the bloc’s “Made in Europe” agenda “could pose significant challenges for U.K. industry.”

U.K. manufacturers fear the rules in the EU’s landmark Industrial Accelerator Act would exclude them from EU procurement deals, subsidies and other financial incentives. France, in particular, has advocated for tougher provisions in the legislation.

Burnham and Macron agreed “it was vital to work together to find a way forward to protect shared interests,” the spokeswoman added.
