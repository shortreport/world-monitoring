EU rejects Trump’s case for new tariffs
By Camille Gijs · Nov 25, 2026, 4:48 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — The European Commission on Wednesday rejected Washington’s claim that Europe is fueling global industrial overcapacity, after the Trump administration proposed a 15% tariff on the 27-country bloc.

“We will study the findings. At the same time we strongly reject the premise that Europe is a source of structural overcapacity. To the contrary, Europe is among those most exposed to overcapacity,” Olof Gill, the Commission’s deputy chief spokesperson, said in a statement.

“The EU and the U.S. share a real concern about global overcapacity, much of it state-driven and concentrated in third countries. We regret the lack of joint action,” he added.

The Office of the U.S. Trade Representative, led by Jamieson Greer, found that 14 trading partners, including the European Union, China and Japan, had artificially deflated prices by flooding markets with manufactured goods. The decision follows a trade investigation launched in March.

Greer on Wednesday proposed a 15% tariff on the European Union, in line with the ceiling agreed in the transatlantic trade truce struck in July 2025 between U.S. President Donald Trump and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen.

Greer, who is expected to dial in next Monday to a meeting of European trade ministers, can expect pushback on the excess capacity probe. EU ministers are also expected to bring up the lack of progress on lowering tariffs on EU products containing steel — some of which still face U.S. duties of as high as 50%.

The overcapacity investigation, launched under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, is the second of two trade probes initiated in the spring as part of the Trump administration’s mission to rebuild its tariff wall.

After the “reciprocal” duties Trump imposed in 2025 were invalidated earlier this year by the U.S. Supreme Court, the administration imposed a 10% tariff on the European Union, citing concerns that Brussels had failed to ban the import of goods made using forced labor. The higher of the two tariff rates will apply, rather than the two being added together, a senior U.S. official said.

The official added that the proposed duties would likely take effect in January at the earliest, after USTR holds hearings and receives public comments.

The chair of the European Parliament’s trade committee, Bernd Lange, lambasted the conclusions of the USTR report as “absurd.”

“Anyone who expected calmer waters after the midterm elections was mistaken. Few seriously expected that in the first place,” Lange said in a statement, referring to a Nov. 3 vote that returned firm majorities for the Democrats in the Senate and the House of Representatives.

“Neither the Supreme Court ruling nor the verdict of the voters has swayed Donald Trump. When a court strikes down the legal basis for his tariffs, he looks for another. His aim remains the same. He uses tariffs to put pressure on trading partners and wring ever more concessions out of them.”
