Document: EU capitals seek greater say over ‘Made in Europe’ partners
By Francesca Micheletti, Sebastian Starcevic · Jul 6, 2026, 1:43 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — EU countries are pushing to curb the European Commission’s discretion over which third countries qualify as “trusted partners” under the bloc’s flagship Industrial Accelerator Act.

EU diplomats and lawmakers are discussing the industrial policy bill with the goal of reaching a final agreement by the end of the year. The proposal aims to boost Europe’s industry by favoring local content in certain areas of public procurement and funding.

Under the Commission proposal, countries covered by EU trade and procurement agreements would generally qualify for equivalent treatment to EU-origin products, while Brussels would retain the power to exclude individual countries where reciprocity or economic-security concerns arise.

According to a draft Council compromise text obtained by POLITICO, member countries want any decision to remove a third country from that list to be made through so-called implementing acts rather than delegated acts. That would give national governments a formal role rather than leaving those decisions primarily to the Commission.

The compromise would also require the Commission to create “a freely accessible digital tool” listing countries whose products qualify as equivalent to EU-origin goods — and, where relevant, countries that have been excluded from that treatment.

The Commission has yet to publish which countries it believes should qualify under the new regime. The EU has trade and procurement agreements with around 80 countries worldwide, but Industry Commissioner Stéphane Séjourné, who is overseeing the IAA, has argued that the final list of trusted partners should be closer to 20.

Séjourné’s idea for a narrow list of partners is supported by France and by at least two of the parliament’s three lead legislators on the file. But it has met resistance from EU countries that favor freer trade, including Germany, the Netherlands and Nordic member countries.
