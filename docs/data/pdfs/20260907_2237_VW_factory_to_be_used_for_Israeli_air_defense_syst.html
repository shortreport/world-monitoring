VW factory to be used for Israeli air defense system
By Jordyn Dahl, Chris Lunday, Romanus Otte · Sep 7, 2026, 3:36 PM · View in your browser

Volkswagen’s Osnabrück factory will be converted to produce the Iron Dome air defense system made by Israel’s Rafael, the automaker announced on Monday.

The German automaker said it is selling the factory to the state of Lower Saxony and financial investor Aurelius Capital to convert it into a “competence center for security and defense solutions.”

“There has been a fundamental change in our security situation and our need for protection,” Olaf Lies, the premier of Lower Saxony, said in a statement. “Germany and Europe must strengthen their capabilities and become more independent.”

Osnabrück is one of four factories that VW is looking to offload as part of a cost-cutting plan that will also cut 100,000 jobs.

CEO Oliver Blume has been courting defense companies for over a year, and has been in discussions with Rafael for months, telling investors in March 2025 that “we need to invest more in order to be safe again.”

The Israeli company has been pitching a mobile version of its Iron Dome system for the European market, and displayed it at June’s Berlin air show.

The company said the modular system could be integrated into German and NATO command-and-control networks and is intended to defend against cruise and ballistic missiles, as well as advanced aerial threats.

Iron Dome is designed for the lower end of a layered air-defense network, intercepting threats including rockets, drones, mortars and artillery shells. Germany already operates the Israeli-U.S. Arrow 3 system, which is designed to destroy ballistic missiles outside the Earth’s atmosphere.
