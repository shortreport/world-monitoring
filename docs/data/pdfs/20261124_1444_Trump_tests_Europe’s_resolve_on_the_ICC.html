DRIVING THE DAY

HOBBLING THE HAGUE: A Trump-backed proposal to curb the reach of the International Criminal Court will test Europe’s support for the court, after Washington last month imposed sweeping sanctions and threatened to dismantle it “brick by brick.”

The proposal would limit the ICC’s ability to prosecute citizens of countries that haven’t joined the court — including the U.S., Russia, China and Israel. It could shield Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping and Benjamin Netanyahu from prosecution, according to four senior government officials and EU lawmakers aware of the plans.

For Brussels, the dispute adds to wider tensions with Washington over digital taxes and support for Ukraine. It also puts EU countries at odds over how far to go in defending the court: some want to seek a deal with the U.S.; others want the European Commission to activate a blocking statute to stop EU countries complying with Washington’s sanctions.

When to watch: ICC member countries are due to debate the proposal, officially put forward by Guatemala, at the court’s annual meeting in The Hague starting on Monday, according to the officials.

What’s at stake: The proposal calls for dialogue with non-member countries on limiting the court’s ability to pursue their citizens. If adopted, it would mean “the ICC can no longer prosecute heads of state from countries that are not members of the Rome Statute” founding the court, French Socialists and Democrats lawmaker Chloé Ridel said.

A statement signed by Trump, Putin and Xi at their meeting in Shenzhen last week also called for limits on the ICC.

“The outcome of the trilateral summit between Xi, Trump and Putin clearly shows the intent by the three leaders … to empty the jurisdiction of the Court, and grant themselves and their citizens impunity from prosecution by the ICC,” said Dutch center-right MEP Dirk Gotink, who has written to U.S. lawmakers over the ICC.

“They want to save themselves,” a senior official from an ICC member country, granted anonymity to discuss the non-public process, said of Trump, Xi and Putin. (The ICC hasn’t brought proceedings against Trump or Xi. It issued an arrest warrant against Putin in 2023 over the alleged unlawful deportation and transfer of children from occupied areas of Ukraine, and against Netanyahu in 2024 for alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity arising from Israel’s military offensive in Gaza. Putin and Netanyahu have denied the allegations and rejected the court’s jurisdiction.)

The U.S. response: “President Trump and Secretary Rubio have made clear that the ICC must stop threatening American sovereignty. The United States never agreed to ICC jurisdiction over its service members, officials or nationals,” the State Department wrote in response to questions. “Either the ICC will end its threats, or we will end the ICC.”

Europe’s response: Belgium’s Foreign Minister Maxime Prévot told Playbook he is “concerned by the various reports surrounding possible new initiatives concerning the International Criminal Court.” The Shenzhen declaration’s language on the court’s jurisdiction and state officials’ immunity “adds to these concerns,” he said.

ICC backers are divided over whether to seek a deal with Washington or push for the Commission to activate the blocking statute, the senior official said. The Netherlands has denied a report that it is bending to U.S. pressure, and an EU diplomat described the Dutch as a “driving force behind the efforts of countries seeking to uphold the International Criminal Court.”

BUDGET BLOWUP

THE BIG BUDGET BRAWL: EU leaders are set to square off at this week’s European Council summit after the bloc’s 27 ambassadors clashed over the Multiannual Financial Framework last night, three EU diplomats told Gregorio Sorgi and me.

“This could be a bloody EUCO,” said one of the diplomats, adding that “if everyone goes too far, it’s too difficult to come back” in December, which is the informal deadline for governments to reach a deal on the next seven-year budget.

But in their three-hour discussion, ambassadors signaled it’s still too early to be making concessions. That’s despite some leaders appearing more conciliatory toward European Council President António Costa’s budget proposal, which suggested reducing the size of the next budget by a further €44 billion.

Northern European countries, the so-called frugals, pushed for more cuts while a rival coalition of southern and eastern states — the “Friends of Cohesion” — complained that the proposed budget is too small. A group of eastern countries rejected new EU-wide taxes, or “own resources,” arguing that the taxes would disproportionately affect them.

Franco-German fight: The discussion became so unruly that ambassadors traded barbs. Germany’s Thomas Ossowski rejected funding for far-flung territories, saying they are remote and scarcely populated, according to one of the diplomats who was granted anonymity due to the sensitive nature of the talks. France’s Philippe Léglise-Costa shot back that “I prefer to pretend I didn’t hear what I just heard,” another diplomat said.

At Thursday’s summit, leaders will also aim to make progress on a €45 billion loan to Ukraine for 2027. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz is pushing four countries — Hungary, Slovakia, Czechia and Bulgaria — to share the borrowing costs associated with the loan and has threatened to block the EU budget if they hold out.

The new Orbán: Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico is emerging as the most hard-core opponent of Ukraine financing, while Hungary, Czechia and Bulgaria are open to finding solutions, said two EU diplomats with knowledge of proceedings. A potential solution could involve paying the borrowing costs of the Ukraine funding that doesn’t finance military spending, Bulgarian Deputy Prime Minister Atanas Pekanov told Playbook.

PARLIAMENT’S MIDTERM THEATER

COUNTDOWN: The leaders of Renew, the S&D and the EPP will meet this afternoon to hammer out a deal on a written coalition pact, two people familiar with the process told Max Griera. S&D leader Iratxe García and Renew’s Valérie Hayer are hoping that the agreement, which could be finalized as soon as next week, will curb Manfred Weber’s party’s reliance on far-right votes.

Five working clusters — each comprising one representative from each group — are drafting one-page programs about files the groups will commit to working on together and how they’ll do it. POLITICO’s Parliament WhatsApp channel has the full lineup of MEPs (you should sign up).

Yet a list of shared commitments may change little, as the three already agree on most files. The test is whether S&D and Renew can use their backing for a third term for Parliament President Roberta Metsola to extract a pledge from the EPP not to vote with the far right whenever it’s hard to reach a centrist compromise, according to three people.

What really matters … is the cluster negotiating coordination, dispute resolution and sanctions in case of breaches. It will determine whether the pact has teeth, or is just theater.

Tough sell: The conservatives don’t feel the need to avoid the far right when national governments are increasingly working with them. As it happens, Spain’s Dolors Montserrat is leading in three of the clusters for the EPP while her People’s Party is poised to join a government with the far-right Vox party after Sunday’s election.

5 MORE THINGS GETTING US TALKING

SKILLS AND JOBS: The Commission is today set to unveil its European Skills Observatory — a new platform bringing together EU data on education, employment and skills to help track labor market trends — amid fears AI could be squeezing young people out of entry-level jobs, according to an EU official.

DON’T WAIT FOR COHESION: The Commission will also approve its 10th Cohesion Report, a snapshot of Europe’s regions published every three years, alongside the European Semester. But its unveiling will likely slip to next week, since Commissioner Raffaele Fitto can’t make it to Strasbourg for unexpected personal reasons, two Commission officials told Playbook.

NVIDIA FACES A POLITICAL STORM: Jensen Huang is back at the helm of the leading global chipmaker — but he’s about to get a hostile reception on Capitol Hill from members of both parties. My stateside colleagues also report that Vice President JD Vance played a central role in Huang’s return to the company.

HOW EUROPE’S SECURITY SETUP COULD CHANGE: The U.S. and Russia are pressing Europe to overhaul the continent’s security architecture in a bid to end the war in Ukraine — and that could mean anything from giving Moscow a bigger say to no change at all, Victor Jack reports.

SANCTIONING SOLDIERS: The EU wants to target former Russian soldiers, banks, crypto platforms and sports teams in its 22nd sanctions package against Moscow since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced.

BRUSSELS CORNER

WEATHER: Sunny. High of 5C

THE EVENT OF THE WEEK: After a decade of waiting, delays and construction, Brussels’ giant former Citroën garage finally reopens as Kanal on Saturday, with a free opening weekend. The press gets a sneak peek today. Expect 10 exhibitions, performances, concerts and films — all part of a transformation costing around €230 million, including the purchase and redevelopment.

Meanwhile … The former Renault garage in Ixelles is getting a new lease on life as a temporary startup hub. Up to 200 entrepreneurs will share 6,000 square meters for a year, paying just €50 per person per month. The project comes ahead of the larger WAT startup hub planned for 2028.

BRUSSELS NEEDS A HUG: After Monday’s protest, the capital could use some affection. Conveniently, CUDDLES, a six-day performing-arts festival, kicks off today at Les Halles de Schaerbeek, promising circus, stand-up, cabaret and a metaphorical embrace. Opening night features Fabulation at 7 p.m., with two acrobats performing on motorbikes.

THE MORNING AFTER: Monday’s disruption left plenty of rubbish uncollected, with more than half the city’s waste trucks out of action due to the strike. Brussels-Propreté promises a catch-up operation today, prioritizing white and orange bags.

STILL IN TROUBLE: Don’t blame the strike for this one. A major water leak and road collapse near Lambermont and Chazal in Schaerbeek mean tram 7 remains interrupted between Docks Bruxsel and Léopold III. Repairs could take days, possibly weeks.
