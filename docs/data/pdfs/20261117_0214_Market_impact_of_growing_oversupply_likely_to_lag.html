Risk premiums set near-term price floor even as oversupply builds
16 Nov 2026 12:11 EST
Discover more on the Portal
 
 
  Listen now · 3 min
  • Crude markets are heading toward an oversupply of up to 3 million barrels per day (bpd) by the first quarter of 2027 as Middle East exports recover alongside production growth in other regions, especially the Americas.
  • While supply increases have already lowered prices from wartime highs, the building oversupply will probably have limited further impact through early 2027, as continued risk premiums are likely to set a floor in the $80–$100 range.
  • Middle distillate markets will remain tight amid refinery outages in the Middle East and Russia.

Crude markets are headed for a slight oversupply from 2027. This overhang is building as exports from the Middle East have recovered and production elsewhere (especially in the Americas) has also grown (see: Hormuz flows will likely prove sustainable, pulling down oil prices, 13 November 2026). Global crude supply has surpassed demand for the first time since the outbreak of the Middle East war in late February, according to the International Energy Agency, with crude and liquid fuels supply currently outpacing demand by roughly 1.5 million to 1.7 million bpd. Supply is estimated between 105.1 million and 105.4 million bpd, while demand is between 103.3 million and 103.7 million bpd.

Spot prices for Brent crude futures will still likely trade at $80-$100 per barrel through early 2027. Prices have fallen from wartime highs above $100 as Hormuz flows have recovered to near-prewar levels, but they are likely to stabilize in the $80–$100 range through early 2027. Brent started the week in the mid-$90s per barrel, in what many traders have dubbed “expensive oversupply.” A persistent risk premium and elevated freight costs are keeping prices higher than the oversupply alone would suggest.

Heading into 2027, the surplus is expected to widen, likely bringing prices down from March. The US Energy Information Administration (EIA) projects the surplus will grow to 3 million bpd by the first quarter of 2027, with global supply potentially reaching 108 million bpd. On the demand side, it is unlikely that consumption will grow above 104.5 million bpd. That implies a large supply overhang is building, which would allow inventories to be rebuilt across Asia, Europe, and North America.

While shipping remains a risk to oil supply, workarounds are emerging quickly. The UAE will likely complete a pipeline expansion in early 2027 that will allow it to pump almost 4 million bpd to its Fujairah port on the Gulf of Oman, bypassing the Strait of Hormuz. The pipeline’s current capacity can handle slightly less than 2 million bpd.

Barring further geopolitical escalations, the looming oversupply will put further downward pressure on crude prices after the northern hemisphere’s winter heating season ends in March.

Product markets will remain tight over the coming months. This will be especially true for middle distillates like diesel, gasoil, jet fuel, and low-sulphur marine fuels, given extensive damage to refineries across the Middle East (caused by Iranian air strikes) and Russia (caused by Ukrainian air strikes). Refineries across North America, Western Europe, and Asia have maximized their runs to fill the supply gap, raising the risk of unplanned outages.
