Brussels, Charleroi airports to cancel most flights Monday amid Belgian strike
By Bartosz Brzeziński · Nov 22, 2026, 11:28 AM · View in your browser

Most flights at Brussels and Charleroi airports will be canceled Monday as air traffic controllers join a nationwide strike against the Belgian government’s austerity measures.

Belgian air traffic control provider Skeyes said it would be unable to provide normal services between 7 a.m. and 9 p.m. local time, effectively grounding commercial flights at the country’s main airports for most of the day.

Brussels Airport confirmed Saturday that most arriving and departing flights would be canceled, while Brussels South Charleroi Airport said no aircraft would be able to take off or land during the 14-hour shutdown.

Charleroi’s management criticized Skeyes for the short notice of the action, saying the company had informed it of the disruption less than 36 hours in advance, despite the airport having prepared to operate normally.

In a statement Saturday, Charleroi accused air traffic controllers of showing a “completely cavalier attitude” toward airlines, passengers and airport staff.

It said thousands of travelers and some 3,400 people working at Charleroi would be affected, and called on Belgian authorities to develop a coordinated plan to prevent recurring disruptions.

Skeyes has been approached for comment.

Monday’s protest is organized by Belgium’s main trade unions against spending cuts and economic reforms pursued by Prime Minister Bart De Wever’s government.

Public transport across Brussels is also expected to be disrupted.

Passengers have been advised to check with their airlines before heading to either airport.

Tommaso Lecca contributed to this report.
