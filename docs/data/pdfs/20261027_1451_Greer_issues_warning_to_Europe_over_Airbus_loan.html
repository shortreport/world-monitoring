Greer issues warning to Europe over Airbus loan
By Oliver Ward · Oct 27, 2026, 6:43 AM · View in your browser

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer warned Monday that Europe’s record-breaking loan to aerospace giant Airbus risks reigniting a long-running trade dispute and upending a 2021 trade truce that saw both sides pause punitive tariffs.

“Despite ongoing requests, USTR still has no details about this loan,” Greer said in a statement published Monday night. “This situation injects substantial uncertainty into transatlantic trade in the aerospace sector.”

The European Investment Bank in June approved a $3.4 billion loan to Airbus to shore up its research and development operations, the largest in the bank’s history. Reuters first reported in July that U.S.-based Boeing was pressing the Trump administration to scrutinize the arrangement, which could give Airbus’ a leg up in its development of a new generation of commercial jets.

It’s not clear what triggered the warning on Monday night. The USTR press office did not immediately respond to a request for clarification.

“I had sincerely hoped that trade-distorting European subsidies to Airbus were a thing of the past,” Greer said in his statement. “It is important this situation is resolved as soon as possible to ensure market-driven production in the aerospace sector and to avoid violation of the terms of our hard-won [large civil aircraft] deal.”

Context: Under former President Joe Biden, the U.S. and European Union reached a truce in 2021 on the 17-year trade dispute over aircraft subsidies. Both sides agreed to pause retaliatory tariffs imposed over the course of the conflict for five years — which ended in June. The deal also set up a working group to discuss subsidy limits and a forum to raise future disputes.

Though the arrangement was not formally extended, EU member states in June agreed to an open-ended extension of the tariff hiatus, keeping their freeze on duties on some $4 billion in U.S. exports.

The U.S. had suspended tariffs on $7.5 billion worth of EU exports.

Greer’s warning is only the latest flare up in the transatlantic trade relationship, as frustration mounts on both sides over implementation of the Turnberry trade deal struck last year in Scotland.

The EU is pushing the U.S. to reduce tariffs on certain steel products to comply with EU regulations implementing the deal. But the U.S. has told the European Commission it will not do so unless Brussels softens its enforcement of the Digital Markets Act, its signature Big Tech rulebook.
