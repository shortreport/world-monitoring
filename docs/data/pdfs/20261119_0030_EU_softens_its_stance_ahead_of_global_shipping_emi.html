EU softens its stance ahead of global shipping emissions talks
By Martina Sapio · Nov 18, 2026, 4:29 PM · View in your browser

The EU is softening its stance on a shipping decarbonization deal ahead of international talks that have so far failed to produce a breakthrough, according to a Council document obtained by POLITICO, dated Nov. 3.

The document is a compromise text drawn up by the Irish presidency, and says the bloc “can accept a smoother” or more gradual start to the plan’s yearly cuts in the greenhouse gas content of ship fuels, as long as the cuts steepen later so shipping reaches net zero by or around 2050. It keeps the line that early-year targets can’t fall below those in the EU’s own shipping fuel rules, known as FuelEU Maritime.

The draft also says the EU should be open to a range of targets for 2040, and to giving extra credit to ships that run on zero- and near-zero emission fuels.

It would also back an additional way for ships to comply, paying into emissions-cutting projects at a price set by the International Maritime Organization, the United Nations agency that regulates global shipping, with conditions attached.

However, EU governments agreed their final position on Nov. 13, meaning the final mandate may differ from this draft.

The shift comes as the IMO struggles to agree on its Net-Zero Framework, a planned set of global rules that would require ships to cut emissions or pay for missing their targets. A vote to adopt it was postponed by a year in October 2025 after pressure from a U.S.-led coalition of oil-producing nations. Since then, countries have filed several proposals to change it, but no version has won enough support to be pass.

Few expect a vote at the meeting of the IMO’s environment committee at the end of the month, with April 2027 seen as a more realistic target.
