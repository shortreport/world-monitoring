Mélenchon promises fuel price caps, data center crackdown
By Marianne Gros · Sep 24, 2026, 5:35 PM · View in your browser

Far-left candidate in the French presidential elections Jean-Luc Mélenchon said he will cap fuel prices at €1.70 if he is elected at next year’s poll, rebutting fears that this would lead to supply shortages.

He also said he would limit the roll-out of data centers across France, calling them an “ecological disaster.”

Speaking at POLITICO’s Playbook Paris Live event, Mélenchon said oil and gas major Total Energies “would just manage with its margins” and dismissed claims that it coud lead to supply shortages.

“Do you really think that Total would say I’m not going to sell to [France], where are they going to sell their oil? China? They don’t need it. India? they don’t need it either … Let’s not exaggerate,” said Mélenchon.

Asked about artificial intelligence governance, Mélenchon said he did not want to see “mega data centers” built all over the country, describing them as an “ecological disaster” due to the technology’s massive power and water demand. “You have local authorities who are saying no, we don’t want that here. Also you have to realize that the consumption in water and resources is just terrible,” he said.

The far-left candidate, who currently stands at 16% in the polls, also doubled down on his claim that France is in “a state of social and ecological emergency.”

In France and accross the EU, anti-data center grassroots movements are emerging as the bloc looks to expand the development of large data centers to power its digital transition.
