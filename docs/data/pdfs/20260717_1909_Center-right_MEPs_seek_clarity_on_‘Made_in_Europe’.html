Center-right MEPs seek clarity on ‘Made in Europe’ club 
By Francesca Micheletti · Jul 17, 2026, 12:09 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — Center-right EU lawmakers are pressing the European Commission to identify which non-EU countries will qualify for its proposed “Made in Europe” procurement club.

The Industrial Accelerator Act would reserve parts of public procurement for products made in the EU or in “trusted partner” countries. But while the legislation creates that category, it does not specify which countries qualify, leaving the decision largely up to the Commission.

That uncertainty has prompted six lawmakers from the European People’s Party to ask Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and industry chief Stéphane Séjourné to specify which countries would qualify based on existing trade and procurement agreements, according to a letter obtained by POLITICO.

“We would be grateful if the Commission could provide a comprehensive list of all third countries that, under the agreements currently in force, it considers benefiting from treatment equivalent to Union origin pursuant to Article 8 of the proposed Regulation,” the lawmakers write.

The letter was signed by EPP group Vice-Chair Dolors Montserrat, the group coordinators in the internal market (IMCO) and international trade (INTA) committees, and three shadow MEPs on the file.

Séjourné told lawmakers at a hearing last month that he estimated the number of “trusted partners” would total around 20. This, the lawmakers write, has created uncertainty regarding the scope of Made in Europe and “makes it important to clarify precisely which third countries the Commission considers to be covered by the provision.”

The EPP lawmakers also ask for a list of third countries that would receive EU-equivalent treatment for public support schemes, as well as clarification on the economic scope of the European preference provisions for net-zero technologies.

The concerns are not confined to Parliament, with the Council of the EU earlier proposing to tweak the process for deciding which countries receive Made in Europe status to ensure that national capitals, and not the Commission alone, have a formal role in the matter.

The EU’s free-trade partners, especially the U.K., are watching deliberations on the scope of the IAA’s Made in Europe provision amid concern that they may be shut out.

The IAA is being steered through parliament by three lead lawmakers from the IMCO, ITRE and INTA committees. They are expected to issue a draft report on Sept. 7.

EU institutions have set themselves an ambitious roadmap to wrap the file by the end of this year, leaving little time to resolve one of its most politically sensitive questions.
