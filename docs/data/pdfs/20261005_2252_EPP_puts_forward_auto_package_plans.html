EPP puts forward auto package plans
By Jordyn Dahl · Oct 5, 2026, 3:52 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — The European People’s Party is doubling down on its effort to water down emission standards for automakers in compromise amendments sent to shadow rapporteurs and obtained by POLITICO.

The amendments include averaging emission standards for automakers over five years instead of three, which was initially proposed by the European Commission in December’s Automotive Package. That package proposed walking back an earlier law ending the sale of new CO2-emitting cars from 2035.

The EPP amendments mirror the draft proposal put forward by Italian MEP Massimiliano Salini, the European Parliament’s lead negotiator on the file who is also with the EPP.

They also broaden the definition of what constitutes a zero-emission vehicle from only electric cars to those “running exclusively on eligible fuels.” The EPP has consistently advocated for alternative fuels like biofuels or e-fuels to have a bigger role in decarbonizing the auto sector.

Green advocates and left-leaning parties like the Socialists and Democrats oppose the idea, arguing those fuels are expensive to produce at scale and should be used for harder-to-decarbonize sectors like maritime and aviation.

Under the Commission’s proposal, automakers would be expected to offset extra emissions through things like investments in green steel and alternative fuels. Automakers would only be allowed to offset up to 3% of their emissions with alternative fuels. Salini’s draft report — and the EPP compromise amendments — would increase that to 10%.

The amendments are the starting position for negotiations in Tuesday’s meeting of shadow MEPs in Strasbourg.
