Taiwan elections and risk outlook

16 Sep 2026 15:43 EDT Listen to the recording

2026 local elections

Taiwan's 28 November local elections will contest mayorships and council seats across 22 localities. Eurasia Group's base call: the Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) flips some localities currently held by the Kuomintang (KMT), but the KMT retains a majority of city mayorships. Preliminary estimates suggest the DPP gains perhaps two to three seats beyond its current five, while the KMT holds fewer than its current 14 but still leads overall.

The six key battlegrounds are New Taipei City, Kaohsiung, Changhua County, Chiayi City, Yilan, and Keelung. New Taipei City is the highest-profile contest: the incumbent KMT mayor is term-limited, the race is close to a toss-up in current polling, and a KMT loss there would be highly damaging to the party. President William Lai has publicly flagged Kaohsiung — currently a DPP-held seat — as his personal benchmark for assessing DPP performance.

Critically, cross-strait issues do not drive local election outcomes. Results will be determined primarily by candidate quality and local concerns.

Implications of the 2026 local elections for different political parties

KMT: A strong local showing strengthens KMT Chairwoman Cheng Li-wun's bid for the 2028 presidential nomination. A poor showing — particularly losing New Taipei City — would undermine her. Her China-friendly positioning is broadly unpopular with the wider Taiwan electorate, meaning her presidential nomination would increase the likelihood of a Lai second term. More moderate figures — Taichung Mayor Lu Hsiu-yen and Legislative Yuan Speaker Han Kuo-yu — have broader support within the KMT and would give the party a more competitive general election profile. Taipei Mayor Chiang Wan-an is seen as a longer-term prospect, more likely for 2032 than 2028.

DPP: A poor local performance could generate internal pressure on Lai and potentially surface a challenger for the DPP nomination, though an incumbent president being defeated in his own party's nomination process has no precedent in Taiwan's political history. Eurasia Group treats Lai as the near-certain DPP presidential nominee.

Taiwan People's Party (TPP): The TPP's primary goal is to local legislative council seats, not mayorships. Failure to do so would signal long-term structural decline. The KMT views the blue-white coalition as working in its favor and expects to press the TPP into supporting the KMT presidential candidate in 2028, particularly given that former TPP Chairman Ko Wen-je remains in jail on corruption charges.

Five-year Taiwan Strait risk outlook

Eurasia Group's published five-year risk framework assigns:

  • Invasion risk: rises from 5% to 10% starting in 2029. The increase reflects Xi Jinping's expected restoration of confidence in his military following unprecedented purges — estimated at 80% of senior People's Liberation Army (PLA) officers, including most Central Military Commission members — and a gradually shifting regional military balance in China's favor.
  • Non-invasion crisis (quarantine) risk: rises to 30% starting in 2029, driven by the expected combination of a second Lai term and a post-Trump U.S. administration returning to more visible Taiwan support (regular arms sales, presidential transits, public engagement).

Even at 10%, invasion remains a tail risk. Beijing's primary objective is unification, not invasion; it views invasion as the most uncertain and risky pathway. Beijing believes it can achieve its goals through coercion and persuasion short of military force.

Quarantine mechanics and market impact

Beijing has already expanded its non-military presence around Taiwan, including coast guard and survey vessels. In June, Chinese coast guard vessels sought information from commercial vessels in Taiwan's waters for the first time.

Eurasia Group expects Beijing to move incrementally up its escalation ladder — beginning with ad hoc inspections before escalating to more disruptive options. An energy quarantine targeting liquefied natural gas (LNG) imports would be the most economically damaging scenarios. Taiwan currently holds 11 days of LNG reserves, with a target of 14 days by 2027; combined LNG and coal reserves are estimated at roughly 40 days. The Taiwanese government may choose to curb industrial power under duress to internationalize the cost of a quarantine.

Semiconductor supply chains would be directly affected under two conditions: a sustained energy quarantine that forces power cuts to industry, or an aerial quarantine that disrupts air cargo shipments.

US Policy toward Taiwan

Under the current Trump administration, Eurasia Group expects the US to remain transactional and less proactive on Taiwan. Post-midterms — where the base call is Democrats winning the House at minimum, with the Senate a close race — bipartisan congressional pressure is expected to push for smaller arms packages and potentially increased Taiwan engagements. Wholesale policy change is not expected under the current administration.

The next US administration, regardless of party, is expected to return to a more regular and visible support posture for Taiwan, which is itself a key driver of the elevated quarantine risk forecast beginning in 2029.

Key watch items

  1. New Taipei City mayoral race: A KMT loss would be a significant signal for 2028 dynamics and Cheng Li-wun's position within the party.
  2. KMT presidential nomination process: Whether Cheng Li-wun, Lu Hsiu-yen, Han Kuo-Yu, or another figure secures the nomination will materially affect the 2028 competitive landscape and Beijing's threat calculus.
  3. PLA military reconstitution: Eurasia Group expects the military leadership to begin reconstitution in late 2027; the pace at which Xi can restore confidence in the military and the advancement of Chinese military capabilities are the primary variables driving the invasion risk timeline.
  4. U.S. midterm outcomes and post-midterm Taiwan policy signals: Watch for congressional pressure on arms sales and Taiwan engagement as an indicator of the trajectory heading into 2028.
  5. Beijing's escalation ladder around Taiwan: Monitor for any expansion of coast guard or survey vessel activity, inspections of commercial vessels, or statements that could signal movement toward quarantine-type actions.
  6. TPP viability: The third party's performance in local council seats will determine whether it remains a meaningful factor in the 2028 blue-white coalition calculus.

What clients are asking

Fiscal and monetary policy implications of the elections: Speakers do not expect the local elections to affect macroprudential policy. A DPP unified government after 2028, however, would likely produce expansionary fiscal policy, with increased spending on housing, social welfare, and — most prominently — national defense.

Quarantine impact on semiconductors: A quarantine would affect the semiconductor industry only if it targeted LNG imports and was sustained long enough to force power cuts to industry, or if it included an aerial component disrupting air cargo. Taiwan's 40-day combined energy reserve buffer provides some runway, but the government may choose to accelerate industrial power cuts to internationalize pressure.

Basis for expecting increased US support for Taiwan despite broader retrenchment trends: Speakers clarified that the expectation is not that the next administration would be more likely to defend Taiwan militarily, but that it would return to a more regular rhythm of arms sales, transits, and public engagement (a posture pulled back under the Trump administration) that would elevate Beijing's threat perception.

Whether a KMT presidency would actually reduce cross-strait risk: Speakers offered a nuanced view: Beijing would likely use a KMT presidency to probe for concessions, and there would be a two-to-three year period of reduced tension. However, Beijing will eventually hit limits — particularly on economic integration and anything touching Taiwan's political status — and frustration could return risk to prior levels. Beijing also cannot afford to alienate the KMT, the one major Taiwanese party it retains channels to, which constrains how coercive it would be willing to be.

How a KMT presidency would affect invasion and quarantine odds: A KMT presidency would not significantly affect invasion odds, which are driven by PLA capability and Xi's confidence in his military rather than Taiwan's political leadership. It would, however, reduce non-invasion crisis odds, which are more directly tied to political signaling dynamics.
