Watch for moves against Federal Election Commission

 
  
 
 
 
  • Watch for moves against Federal Election Commission
  • Polling update: NATO, Maine, and Trump's key numbers
  • Warsh's working groups trend toward the mainstream
  
 
 
 

Watch for moves against Federal Election Commission. President Trump’s dismissal of all commissioners on the Election Assistance Commission (EAC) is mostly a nothingburger—but could be a worrying sign for election integrity if it presages action against partisan balance on the Federal Election Commission (FEC), on the grounds that the Supreme Court’s ruling last month allows its commissioners to be fired at will.  

  • The EAC, composed of two commissioners of each party along with a professional staff, is a federal advisory body tasked with helping states administer elections. But it has been hamstrung by lack of a quorum and a miniscule budget—only $30 million—for most of its history, meaning that its ultimate impact on election administration is relatively small.  

  • Without any commissioners, the EAC now will be effectively paralyzed. That works both ways; it will be unable to certify new voting systems, but it will also be unable to implement the White House’s proof-of-citizenship requirements to the federal voter-registration form (which had already been blocked by courts).  

  • Any moves against partisan balance at the FEC, which regulates federal campaign finance law, would be a much bigger deal. The FEC currently has two Democratic commissioners, with two Republican nominations pending; with six slots total, the agency requires a four-commissioner quorum to operate. If Trump moved to appoint four Republicans to the board, it would turn the FEC into a partisan agency that could selectively enforce campaign finance law against Democrats and rewrite regulations to benefit Republicans; that would structurally tilt the electoral playing field away from Democrats ahead of the 2028 presidential election.

Polling update: NATO, Maine, and Trump's key numbers.  

  • A poll commissioned by NATO found only 43% of American respondents believed NATO would support the US if it was attacked, the lowest score of any NATO country; the alliance-wide average was 57%. This reflects the growing, mutual skepticism between the US and its NATO allies caused by Trump’s approach to the alliance, a feeling that will outlive his presidency and complicate rebuilding ties. Polls consistently show stark partisan differences in NATO approval—with one Pew released this week finding 72% of Democrats support the alliance, compared to 42% of Republicans—a trend that is unlikely to subside anytime soon. 

  • Polling in Maine will help decide Platner’s replacement. With Graham Platner, Democrats’ Maine Senate nominee, expected to drop out formally by Monday’s deadline, Maine Democrats have shifted focus to holding an abbreviated party convention to nominate a new candidate by 27 July. Which candidate has the best chance of defeating Senator Susan Collins (R-ME) will be perhaps the key factor in that decision. A Politico poll found that former Maine Senate president (and progressive favorite) Troy Jackson would beat Colins by five points, with two other serious contenders holding only two-point leads. That polling is good news for Democrats, as it suggests that replacing Platner---who was down by five in the same poll after sexual assault allegations emerged—would substantially increase Democrats’ chances in the race.  

  • The two most important macro-political indicators—Trump’s approval rating and the generic congressional ballot—have been largely unchanged over the July 4 period. Trump’s approval, per Silver Bulletin, is hovering between 39 and 40, while Democrats maintain a lead between 6 and 6.5 points in the generic congressional ballot. Both are stronger than Republicans were in late May, but they’re still both in firmly negative territory.  

Warsh’s working groups trend toward the mainstream. The Federal Reserve yesterday released the rosters of the five task forces created by Fed chair Kevin Warsh; the names tend toward the orthodox and reputable, with a number of high-profile academics and central-bank practitioners, as well as a few voices from the corporate world.  

  • Looking holistically, the task forces are likely to take time to gather evidence, then engage the Federal Open Markets Committee on changes that should be made to the Fed’s policy framework and communications.  

  • The task force on productivity—featuring venture-capitalist Marc Andreessen, growth economist (and recent Anthropic hire) Charles Jones, and XBOX CEO Asha Sharma—is perhaps the most predisposed towards holding a strong view, in this case toward seeing AI as a major productivity enhancer, without any AI-skeptic voices like MIT economist Daron Acemoglu, whose name had previously been floated.  

  • The balance-sheet policy review will be the most immediately consequential for the market, but the roster does not provide real hints into what the outcome of the review will be. That the support work will come from Fed staff suggests Warsh will have a significant influence.

Explanation of Political Trajectories (ie, “Trajectories”)

Eurasia Group's (EG) Political Trajectories are indicators of the net impact of political factors on the macro business environment in a country over specific timeframes. For each country, two Trajectories are provided, a short-term (6 months) and a long term assessment (24 months). Trajectories are assigned by Eurasia Group’s country analysts through a structured qualitative process. Each Trajectory represents a net assessment, indicating how political changes in a country impact the macro business environment, defined as the economic and investment climate in the country. Once initiated, Trajectories are updated both periodically and as events warrant. Trajectories may be suspended from time to time if there is no active analyst opinion or analyst coverage, but the opinion or coverage is expected to resume. Trajectories can be:

Positive: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a positive impact on the macro business environment

Neutral: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a neutral or negligible impact on the macro business environment

Negative: Political factors in the country are anticipated to have a negative impact on the macro business environment

Disclaimers: Trajectories are solely for informational purposes and should not be relied upon as investment advice. Trajectories do not indicate asset price movements, and have no value in forecasting market prices. Trajectories are based on data and analysis we deem to be reliable; they are not guaranteed as to accuracy or completeness. The Trajectories indicate the political outlook in a single country, over time. Trajectories do not address sector-specific or city/regional political risks that are often different than the country-level outlook. Trajectories represent an analyst’s net qualitative assessment of a country's political outlook. EG sales, analytic staff and products may provide analysis reflecting different opinions than those expressed by the Trajectories.
