US daily crude output likely to surpass 14 million barrels
12 Oct 2026 11:08 EDT
Discover more on the Portal
  • US output is likely to surpass a record 14 million barrels per day (bpd) as producers add rigs to drill for more oil.
  • US crude and fuel producers are ramping up exports to cash in on high prices caused by Middle East and Russian supply disruptions and China’s export restrictions.
  • As Middle East flows fall because of increased Iranian attacks on shipping, the risk of a US escalation against Iran will rise if efforts to keep the Strait of Hormuz open falter.

US oil production will keep booming amid global crisis. US crude oil production will likely soon rise above a record 14 million bpd as the industry fills supply gaps caused by the global disruptions from the wars in the Middle East and Ukraine. Production stood at 13.979 million bpd on 2 October, according to official data from the US Energy Information Administration (EIA).

Output will rise further, as producers have added new rigs consistently since early September. In the week ending 9 October, the total oil and gas rig count rose above 600, its highest level since May 2024, according to energy services firm Baker Hughes.

US crude exports will remain elevated or even increase further amid high profits generated from these sales. US producers are exporting more than 4 million bpd of crude as prices surge amid Middle East and Russian disruptions that have affected importers, especially in Asia and Europe.

The average break-even price for US crude oil production is estimated at between $60 and $70 per barrel, with onshore shale field production mostly sitting on the lower end and offshore Gulf production on the higher end. Industry profits are currently very high, given high prices. Benchmark US West Texas Intermediate crude currently trades at more than $90 per barrel, while the global Brent crude price benchmark is about $106 a barrel (as of 0600 EST on 12 October). Some regional prices are in the $150s per barrel.

Similarly, disruptions will likely persist for refined products because of the Ukraine and Middle East conflicts. Refinery damage across the Middle East and in Russia has constrained supply, while China, the world’s biggest oil refiner, has restricted fuel exports to protect domestic supply. To fill the gap, US exports of middle distillates (including diesel) have surged to an average of more than 1.5 million bpd, especially to Europe and South America.

The fall in Middle East output puts more pressure on the US to escalate against Iran. Two weeks of aggressive Iranian attacks on shipping (see: High oil prices to persist despite Hormuz progress, 5 October 2026) may be affecting regional flows, as the weekly average has fallen from more than 17 million bpd in late September to 15 million bpd in early October. If the US struggles to keep Middle East output high, the pressure to escalate against Iran will grow, and the US will seek ways to degrade Iran’s military capabilities in the strait (see: Escalation risk ticks up as Trump ponders bombing after midterms, 7 October 2026).
