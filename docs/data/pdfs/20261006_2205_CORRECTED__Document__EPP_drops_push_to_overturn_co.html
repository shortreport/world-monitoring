The European People’s Party wants further flexibilities in the EU’s emissions targets for automakers, it says in a position paper obtained by POLITICO.

The European Commission’s 2025 Automotive Package proposed replacing a law that bans the sale of new CO2-emitting cars from 2035.

“The current complete ban on internal combustion engines from 2035 must be corrected in a sensible way,” said the EPP policy paper, which was positive about the Commission’s Automotive Package.

In its proposal, the Commission suggested reducing emissions by 90% for all cars sold after 2035. The Commission’s proposal “provides a solid basis for technological neutrality,” the EPP paper says. “The task now is to translate this into clear, effective legislation that gives Europe’s automotive industry the certainty it needs.”

The policy paper does not mention the EPP’s effort to dilute CO2 standards even further.

While the EPP campaigned on overturning the ban, the paper says that the “high emission reduction targets shall ensure that the transition to battery-electric vehicles keeps rolling.”

The party is leading negotiations on the regulation in the ENVI Committee and is backing an effort to effectively lower the emissions target to 73% when accounting for offsets like investments in green steel and alternative fuels.

“Environmental and climate legislation is important but must not come at the expense of millions of well-paid jobs and the survival of an entire industry in Europe,” the paper says.

The automotive sector is cutting thousands of jobs and shuttering factories in Germany, prompting an economic and political crisis in the country as the far right uses the turmoil to gain more voters.

The EPP is hosting a car summit Tuesday evening at the Schaeffler plant in Haguenau, France. Party chief Manfred Weber is attending, along with Matthias Zink, the CEO of Schaeffler and president of the EU car supplier lobby CLEPA, according to a draft agenda also obtained by POLITICO.

Weber is meeting earlier in the day with Mercedes-Benz CEO Ola Källenius, who is also the current head of car lobby ACEA.

Negotiations are underway on the package across the EU institutions, with a vote in plenary scheduled for Nov. 23.

CORRECTION: This article was updated on Oct. 6 as it misstated the EPP’s position. It calls for further flexibilities for carmakers on meeting emissions targets.
