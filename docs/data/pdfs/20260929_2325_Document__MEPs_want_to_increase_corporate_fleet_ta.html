Document: MEPs want to increase corporate fleet targets
By Jordyn Dahl · Sep 29, 2026, 4:25 PM · View in your browser

The lead negotiators for the European Parliament on the corporate fleet regulation have tabled three compromise amendments that would increase electrification targets for member states, according to the documents obtained by POLITICO.

The amendments raise the minimum target for larger member countries like Austria and Germany from having 95% of their corporate fleets electrified by 2035 to 99% of the fleets.

The rapporteurs also want only all-electric vehicles to count toward the target, rather than a combination of zero-emission and low-emission cars like plug-in hybrids.

Tiemo Wölken and François Kalfon of the Socialists and Democrats are jointly leading the file across the ENVI and TRAN committees, making clear in their draft proposal from May that they would propose higher targets than the European Commission suggested.

The policymakers are focused on creating more affordable electric vehicles for the second-hand market, viewing the corporate fleets regulation as one mechanism to create more supply.

The shorter leasing periods of corporate fleets “make them reach the second-hand market faster and in greater volumes than privately owned vehicles,” the two MEPs write in the amendments. “Realising this potential requires public policies that actively support the uptake of second-hand zero-emission vehicles on the market.”

The corporate fleets regulation is part of the broader automotive package put forward by the Commission in December 2025. The binding targets for member countries is meant to act as a counterbalance to the weakening of emission standards for automakers.

But the binding targets are unpopular with EU capitals. A coalition of nine countries led by Poland have publicly come out against the mandatory targets. Germany is also against the regulation, which creates a blocking minority in the Council.
