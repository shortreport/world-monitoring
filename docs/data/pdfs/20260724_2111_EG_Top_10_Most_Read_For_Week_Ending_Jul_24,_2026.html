24 Jul 2026 12:11

   
1 Geoeconomics: US-Iran escalation reignites energy shock's economic toll
  By EG Geoeconomics, Pietro Guglielmi, Neil Weller, et al.
 
  Renewed Iran-US escalation has materially worsened the energy price shock to growth and inflation globally, with a strong El Niño compounding the blow for some South Asian and Southeast Asian importers. ⟶
 
2 United States: Democrats' Tea Party moment will drag the party left
  By EG United States, Noah Daponte-Smith, Clayton Allen
 
  The momentum behind the progressive wing of the Democratic Party will likely force the party to the left on policy ahead of and after the midterms. ⟶
 
3 Brazil: Polling biases suggest a narrower Lula lead
  By EG Brazil Research, Christopher Garman, Raul Folino, et al.
 
  Brazil’s presidential election will be more competitive than consensus suggests, with President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva’s runoff lead over Senator Flavio Bolsonaro probably resting closer to 2 or 2.5 pp rather than the current 4-point lead indicated by the average of all polls. ⟶
 
4 Geoeconomics: Escalation in the Middle East tests global resilience buffers
  By EG Geoeconomics, Jens Larsen, Pietro Guglielmi, et al.
 
  The Middle East conflict's macro and market impact has proven more limited than feared, but the factors behind that resilience are unlikely to repeat next time. Inflationary pressures persist and developed-market monetary policy is set to stay tighter than pre-war. ⟶
 
5 Brazil: Party conventions will test whether Bolsonaro can broaden his coalition
  By EG Brazil Research, Fernanda Trisotto, Raul Folino, et al.
 
  Parties begin conventions for the 2026 presidential elections at different levels of internal cohesion. The left is united. The center remains fragmented. The right is likely to split the vote in the first round. ⟶
 
6 Peru: Upgrading trajectories on expected investment push and rare government stability
  By EG Latin America, Maria Luisa Puig, Antonio Espinosa, et al.
 
  Eurasia Group is upgrading Peru's short- and long-term political trajectories from neutral to positive, reflecting a likely investment push and unusual government stability under the incoming Keiko Fujimori administration. ⟶
 
7 Agriculture: Agriculture prices set to rise as second Hormuz shock hits buffers
  By EG Sustainability, Nick Kraft, Franck Gbaguidi
 
  A second Hormuz closure hits agriculture markets with depleted inventories, export restrictions on 15% of fertilizer trade, and El Nino risks. Commodity prices rise first, crop output falls second, food inflation follows, with the poorest importers priced out entirely. ⟶
 
8 MENA Crisis, Saudi Arabia, Yemen, Iran: Houthis likely to launch limited attacks to extract Saudi payoffs
  By Firas Maksad, Gregory Brew, Farzan Sabet
 
  The Houthis are using a maritime blockade threat to extract financial concessions from Saudi Arabia, with limited escalation expected before a deal is reached, though oil prices will remain elevated regardless. ⟶
 
9 Hong Kong, United States: Core US restrictions will largely remain despite Hong Kong status reversal
  By EG China, Dominic Chiu, David Meale, et al.
 
  US lets 2020 Hong Kong executive order expire, a symbolic gesture toward US-China de-escalation. But core restrictions on trade, sanctions, export controls, and immigration remain largely intact. ⟶
 
10 United States, Canada: Washington revives Section 338 to drive stalled USMCA talks with Canada
  By EG United States, Nancy Wei, Clayton Allen, et al.
 
  Washington revives historically dormant Section 338 to pressure Canada and push USMCA talks. The move hits even USMCA-compliant goods, raising US leverage. Its targeted design sets a precedent for use with others alleged to discriminate amid significant legal uncertainty. ⟶
