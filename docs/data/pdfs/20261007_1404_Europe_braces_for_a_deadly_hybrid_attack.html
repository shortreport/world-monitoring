DRIVING THE DAY

EUROPE BRACES FOR A DEADLY HYBRID ATTACK — AND DEBATES WHO SHOULD RESPOND: EU capitals are quietly preparing for a major hybrid attack that kills several people — an outcome that looks increasingly likely, five EU diplomats and officials told Nick Vinocur. But the discussions have exposed divisions over how far the bloc should go on security, and whether its role would duplicate NATO’s.

Nightmare scenario: Ambassadors from all 27 EU countries have held confidential talks in recent weeks in a “restricted format,” without phones or assistants, including a meeting last Wednesday that focused specifically on attacks causing mass casualties, two diplomats said. The diplomats were granted anonymity to discuss the confidential exchanges.

Discussions have intensified since an explosive-laden drone was found at Leipzig airport in August. While the EU has long had a “hybrid toolbox” for coordinating responses to cyberattacks, election interference and sabotage, the latest talks have considered emergency response teams, freeing up EU funds for an affected country and how EU leaders should communicate.

“We have to be prepared for something serious,” a senior EU diplomat said. “Unfortunately, Leipzig showed us that a very serious incident, something that causes multiple casualties, is not out of the question.”

Rising danger: The EU says Russia has stepped up cyberattacks, sabotage and other covert operations since its full-scale invasion of Ukraine. On Tuesday, sailors were reported missing after drones struck two ships in the Black Sea. “Given the scope and scale of Russia’s action there are bound to be casualties sooner than later,” a second EU official said. “It’s sheer luck there haven’t been any yet.”

Some capitals fear the EU is overreaching on security and duplicating NATO, which has underpinned European security for nearly 80 years. “If there is confusion or duplication, that risks slowing down the decision-making,” said Oana Lungescu, a former NATO spokesperson.

Several diplomats are also worried that sensitive information shared in EU meetings could leak. “It either shows up in the media or goes straight to Moscow,” said a diplomat from an eastern flank state.

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has proposed an “emergency security protocol” and a “European Security Council,” with both expected to feature in a European Security Strategy due early November. Some diplomats say new structures could take months to establish and overlap with existing tools. “We already have mechanisms in place,” one senior national diplomat said, pointing to the EU’s crisis response system, IPCR.

Putin doesn’t care: NATO should handle intelligence-sharing and military deterrence, while the EU focuses on civilian response, Lungescu said. But Moscow makes no distinction between the institutions: “Frankly, [Russian President Vladimir] Putin doesn’t care whether decisions are taken by Coreper 2 or the NAC,” she said, referring to meetings of EU ambassadors or NATO’s decision-making body.

BREXIT RESET PITCH

BRITAIN WANTS A PIECE OF “MADE IN EUROPE”: The U.K. wants its goods to count as European under the EU’s proposed new industrial rules, its Brexit minister Hamish Falconer told Playbook’s Zoya Sheftalovich — but he dismissed reports that London is discussing matching the bloc’s tariffs on Chinese electric cars.

Falconer is in Strasbourg today to meet European Parliament President Roberta Metsola and Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič, making his pitch for a closer relationship ahead of a U.K.-EU summit penciled in for next month.

“A straightforward and unfussy arrangement” is what the EU and U.K. should aim for, avoiding new barriers for British and European companies, Falconer said.

The EU’s “Made in Europe” plan would give preference to goods made in the bloc in some public contracts and support programs. The U.K. wants British industry included, with Falconer arguing that supply chains already cross the Channel: “For Europe to be more competitive and able to weather some of the shocks that we’re all talking about, we need to do it together.”

What does Britain have to offer in return? “The prime minister’s been absolutely clear that he is ambitious, and those are the instructions that I am under — to be ambitious, to look at all of the options,” Falconer told Zoya. He added that Andy Burnham would say more in a set-piece speech closer to the summit. Burnham “thinks the status quo doesn’t work well,” Falconer said. “It doesn’t work well, I think, for the U.K., and it doesn’t work well for the EU.”

On China: Falconer pushed back on the idea that Brussels was demanding that the U.K. slap tariffs on Chinese electric cars, saying he was “surprised” to have read those reports. “That’s not the kind of conversation that I’m having with my European counterparts,” he said. Falconer argued the U.K. is “not a backdoor for goods” and has “very robust arrangements” in place. The two sides should tackle shared challenges, he said, “but let us not do harm to our collective industrial base.”

20-SECOND PLAYBOOK PRIMER

Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni has applied to the European Union Intellectual Property Office to trademark her voice … but what is the EUIPO? Based in Alicante, Spain, it’s an EU agency responsible for managing intellectual property rights that are valid across the bloc, as well as trademarks and EU designs. It used to be known as the Office for Harmonization in the Internal Market.

FRANCE VS. EVERYONE

COUNCIL TUSSLES OVER WHO GETS DEFENSE CASH: Today will see the latest flashpoint between France and other EU governments over whether countries from outside the bloc, such as Canada, Ukraine and the U.K., can access defense funding in the next seven-year budget, Gregorio Sorgi reports.

The discussion among EU ambassadors is crucial for Paris, which argues that access to the planned €131 billion of defense funds should be limited to EU companies. It is clashing with a bloc of countries, including Germany and Italy, that insist defense firms from allied countries should be able to access the money.

Ambassadors will discuss a compromise put forward by the Irish presidency of the Council of the EU, obtained by POLITICO’s Marianne Gros, that envisions the participation of foreign countries that sign an association agreement with the EU.

LE PEN’S BUDGET THREAT: Far-right presidential frontrunner Marine Le Pen has unveiled plans to halve France’s net contributions to the EU budget from around €10 billion to €5 billion — a frightening prospect for EU governments wary that they might have to fill the gap if she wins the election next spring.

To reduce national contributions, Le Pen suggested harmonizing tobacco and alcohol taxes across Europe and transferring the revenues to the Brussels budget. She also proposed linking national budget contributions to carbon emissions.

Reality check: The ideas are unlikely to be accepted by Northern states wary of expanding the EU’s taxing powers and by countries with carbon-intensive industries, like Poland.

BRUSSELS CORNER

WEATHER: Sunny spells turning to rain by the evening. High of 23C.

STARTING IN STRASBOURG … Yes, I know this is Brussels Corner. But your Playbook author is in Strasbourg. Wednesday here is basically Thursday in Brussels: one last night out before everyone leaves. But even the nightlife can’t escape political polarization.

Right turn: The American-style bar Les Aviateurs, which keeps going until 4 a.m., has increasingly become a right-wing haunt. The trend has been building for a while, and several Parliament officials and assistants said it is no longer a hangout if you’re not part of the EPP or ECR.

The Socialists, meanwhile, have been colonizing Aedaen Place, also known as “the speakeasy.” The Greens sometimes go there too, although some have migrated to Le Hoop.

Left splits at night: France Unbowed MEPs and staffers head almost religiously to Petit Tigre every Wednesday. But Italy’s Five Star Movement, the second-biggest delegation in the Left group, is more itinerant, with Boucanier a favorite — partly for the carpaccio à volonté. Vegetarians get a mysterious half-pizza, half-salad creation (thoughts and prayers with my fellow Italians).

FOR THOSE WHO STAYED IN BRUSSELS: Don’t worry, we haven’t forgotten you.

Tintinology 101: Brussels today launches the international symposium “Les cultures de Tintin — Objectif centenaire,” beginning the countdown to Tintin’s 100th birthday in 2029. There’s even a “Tintinophile” walk through central Brussels, meeting outside City Hall at 10 a.m. and departing a half-hour later.

Serious Tintin business: At 4 p.m., proceedings move to the Palais des Académies for talks on Hergé’s Brussels legacy, including a roundtable with cartoonists Philippe Geluck and Pierre Kroll. It’s free, but registration is required.

Also tonight: The 39th Tels Quels Festival opens at Les Grands Carmes at 8 p.m. with an improv show, kicking off 10 days of drag, film, slam, debates and queer culture.
