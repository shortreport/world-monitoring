Document: EU plans media carveout in state aid overhaul
By Francesca Micheletti · Aug 18, 2026, 10:56 AM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — The European Commission is planning to carve out certain press and media funding from scrutiny in the latest version of its revised state aid rulebook, obtained by POLITICO.

The Commission is expected to deliver by year’s end a revised version of the General Block Exemption Regulation (GBER), the bloc’s main state aid framework. The regulation lays out the conditions under which EU countries can award aid to industry without first having to notify Brussels.

A newer version of the document than the one put out for consultation earlier this year includes an article laying down conditions for “aid schemes to support the production and dissemination of press publications” by small- and medium-sized enterprises.

It also spells out definitions of “start-up” and “innovative enterprise” that had been left blank in the previous version, in what appears to be an overall effort to increase support for innovation, risk finance, and research and development.

The 200-page document includes revisions to environmental, transport and infrastructure aid provisions, particularly in relation to clean vehicles, maritime transport, district heating and the InvestEU funding program.

It also introduces measures for aid in the context of the Emissions Trading System for buildings, road transport and other sectors.
