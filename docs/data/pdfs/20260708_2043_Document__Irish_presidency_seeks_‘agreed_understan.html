Document: Irish presidency seeks ‘agreed understanding’ on Made in Europe rules
By Giorgio Leali · Jul 8, 2026, 1:42 PM · View in your browser

The Irish Council presidency on Wednesday asked EU countries to share their views on the most delicate aspects of the Industrial Accelerator Act, including creating a European preference in public procurement, according to a document obtained by POLITICO.

The presidency asks member countries for input on three contentious issues: how to minimize the IAA’s administrative burden; whether proposed conditions on foreign investment might deter investors; and how to speed up permitting and approvals in the new industrial “acceleration” areas foreseen by the bill.

The questions underline the fault lines that have emerged since the Commission unveiled the proposal in March, with some governments backing a stronger industrial policy while others fear it would create new bureaucracy and raise trade barriers.

The IAA aims to boost European industry by favoring local content in certain areas of public procurement and funding. But countries such as Germany argue the proposal would add bureaucracy just as the EU is trying to simplify regulation. Its “Made in Europe” rules would also exclude many countries that have free-trade agreements with the EU from a preferred club of “trusted partners.”

In the five-page document, circulated ahead of a meeting of member countries’ ambassadors to the EU on July 15, the Irish presidency acknowledges the concerns of some capitals.

“There is wide support for the objectives set out in the IAA, though many Member States continue to have reservations about its complexity, and the additional burden of regulation on public bodies, manufacturers, and consumers. Some Member States have doubted its practicality and questioned the credibility of implementation timelines,” the document reads.

“Before fine-tuning the policy interventions included in the proposal, it is vital to establish an agreed understanding of how the Council intends to strike an appropriate balance between achieving the IAA’s policy objectives and simplification,” the presidency wrote.
