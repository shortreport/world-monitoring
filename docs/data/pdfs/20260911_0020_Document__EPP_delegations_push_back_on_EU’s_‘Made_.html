Document: EPP delegations push back on EU’s ‘Made in Europe’ plans
By Jordyn Dahl · Sep 10, 2026, 5:19 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — National delegations from the European People’s Party are divided over how far the Industrial Accelerator Act should go in setting manufacturing targets or strict local content requirements, according to contributions from 12 member states obtained by POLITICO.

Delegates from Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany, Luxembourg, Sweden and Slovenia are the most skeptical, saying the Commission’s ambition for manufacturing to make up 20 percent of the EU’s GDP should not be a binding target.

The center-right group’s broader input comes a day after a report by the three lead MEPs, or rapporteurs, on the IAA called for strict local-content requirements, with public funding reserved for products and components made in the 27 member states.

Notably, none of the rapporteurs is from the EPP, the largest group in the Parliament. Socialist Pierre Jouvet, Renew’s Christophe Grudler and the Greens’ Anna Cavazzini were tasked by the European Parliament’s internal market, industry and trade committees with drafting the joint report on the IAA proposal launched by the Commission in the spring.

The proposed regulation, a pillar of Commission President Ursula von der Leyen’s industrial agenda, aims to harness public procurement of green and energy-intensive technology to power a broader European economic revival. But the views of the Parliament and Council of the EU are diverging, heralding a tough autumn battle over the bill.

The EPP’s national delegations want trading partners and close allies like the U.K. and European Economic Area countries to be included in the “Made in Europe” club of countries that have free-trade and procurement deals with the bloc and would gain EU-equivalent status.

The delegations from Spain and Italy are the most in favor of stricter Made in Europe requirements that apply on an industry-by-industry basis. Spain, for example, wants countries to be opted in on a product-by-product basis.

The views of the national delegations are more closely aligned with those of the member states, which favor a product-based approach to “Buy European” requirements rather than a country-by-country opt-in or opt-out system.
