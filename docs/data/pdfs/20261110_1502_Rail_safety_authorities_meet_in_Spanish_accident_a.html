DRIVING THE DAY

SPANISH CRASH ON EU RAIL SAFETY AGENDA: Rail safety authorities are meeting in Brussels today for the EU’s Railway Interoperability and Safety Committee, or RISC, with the last deadly Spanish rail accident looming over discussions.

Watch AOB: Today’s agenda, seen by Martina, covers the usual mix of signaling, military mobility, telematics and wider rail safety issues, before ending with “any other business.” A person close to the talks, granted anonymity to speak about the closed-door meeting, told Martina that they expect Spain’s accident to be discussed then.

What is RISC?: The committee meets roughly every three or four months and brings together national authorities to discuss rail safety and interoperability across the EU. That makes it a natural forum for member countries to exchange information after a major accident, even when it happens too late to make the formal agenda.

Spain in focus: The Urda crash, which killed 69 people at the end of October, has put fresh scrutiny on how trains are managed when signaling systems fail. Investigators are examining how a Renfe trainwas allowed to continue after the automatic signaling system was no longer controlling it, before it collided with a stationary Iryo train. Madrid formally asked the EU for help with the investigation last week.

Signaling issues: The accident happened on a section where the EU’s ERTMS signaling system — which can automatically brake trains that exceed permitted speeds or fail to obey signals — had not been deployed, reviving scrutiny of Europe’s patchwork of national train-control systems. But it is far too early to say whether ERTMS would have prevented the crash.

AVIATION

DIGITAL FAIRNESS ACT TO TACKLE DYNAMIC PRICING TECHNIQUES: The Commission will present its Digital Fairness Act on Wednesday, a proposed overhaul of consumer protection rules for online platforms that would include new regulation on dynamic pricing techniques — where prices are adjusted in response to factors such as demand, timing or availability — some of which are used in the travel industry.

Demand for transparency: According to the upcoming proposal, scooped Monday by Eliza Gkritsi, “in a dynamic pricing context, traders must disclose how many items are available to consumers at the advertised price.” In addition, “Where a trader provides online customer service after the conclusion of a contract, the trader shall ensure that, upon request of the consumer, communication with a human being is made available.”

No more dark patterns: The proposal will also include a list of “dark patterns,” a blacklist of deceptive design practices for online platforms. Among these is the practice of “persistently displaying statements throughout the ordering process about the availability or popularity of a product or promotional offer to create an impression of scarcity.”

Carriers in the crosshairs: While the draft proposal does not specify whether it will restrict pricing practices used by airlines, dynamic pricing techniques used by some carriers could be affected by the proposed rules. The draft also provides for fines ranging from 4% to 6% of companies’ total annual revenue in case of violations, with the Commission having the power to enforce them.

No comment: Airlines International Representation in Europe, which represents Poland’s LOT and Turkish Airlines, among others, told Tommaso: “Compliance with airline-specific consumer rules is already supervised by national enforcement bodies and consumer authorities. We will look closely at how the proposed enforcement model and level of fines would fit alongside this existing system, to avoid overlapping competences and double enforcement for the same conduct.”

EU AGENCY SUES IBM, LEONARDO AND ATOS OVER EES LAUNCH DELAYS: EU-LISA, the EU agency responsible for managing large-scale IT systems, filed a lawsuit last year against IBM, Leonardo and Atos over delays in launching the Entry/Exit System, which registers biometric data of non-EU travelers entering the Schengen area for short stays. The lawsuit, first reported by the Financial Times, was confirmed by the agency. The three companies formed a consortium that won the contract to develop the system in 2019.

Development at fault: “EU-LISA confirms that legal proceedings have been initiated against the consortium, in line with the Agency’s responsibility to protect the EU’s financial interests,” the EU agency told Morning Mobility in an emailed statement. “The proceedings concern the system’s development phase and have no impact on its current operation,” EU-LISA added, noting that it considers the system “fully operational.”

CARS

UK EXPECTED TO LOWER EV MANDATE: Ministers in the U.K. are at loggerheads over how deeply to slash mandated electric vehicle sales targets, Graham Lankree reports. The auto industry expects an announcement before the Christmas holidays as ministers come under additional pressure from the EU to put up tariff barriers against Chinese EVs.

Big fight: There’s currently a “big fight” between the environment, transport and business ministers about how to change the Zero Emission Vehicle mandate, a U.K. auto industry insider told Jordyn. This follows months of pressure from automakers to change the targets, which resulted in ministers launching a consultation that ended in late October.

Current state of play: The mandate currently requires 80% of new car sales to be zero-emission by 2030, rising to 100% by 2035. “We’d asked for a reduction to 50%. But 60% feels about the right landing zone,” the industry insider said.

Ratchet rethink: Changes would mean “the trajectory from now to then would change too,” they said, pointing to the steadily rising requirements for EV and hybrid car sales in the coming years. Under the current mandated rules, 38% of a manufacturer’s annual new car sales must be zero-emission in 2027, up from 33% this year.

Ministers’ tug-of-war: Over the weekend, The Guardian reported the consultation has pitted Trade Secretary Jonathan Reynolds — who has backed industry calls to slash zero-emissions vehicle sales target to 50% by 2030 — against Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander, who is backing a 60% target, and the Net-Zero Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh, who wants the target to remain at 80%.

Eyes emoji: Reynolds is scheduled to give a keynote at the U.K. auto lobby SMMT’s annual dinner on Nov. 24. The U.K. trade chief used the event in 2024 to announce a fast-track consultation on changes to the mandate. Jordyn will be in attendance.

On target: “We’re sticking to our commitment, phasing out new cars that run only on petrol or diesel by 2030,” said a No. 10 spokesperson on Monday, adding that “every new car and van will be zero emission by 2035.”

TRANSPORT BUDGET

CEF NEGOTIATING POSITION UP FOR VOTE: The European Parliament’s transport and industry committees are set to vote today on their negotiating position for the next Connecting Europe Facility, the EU’s main pot of money for cross-border transport infrastructure. The Commission has proposed €51.5 billion for transport in 2028-2034, including €17.5 billion for military mobility.

Expected to pass: The file was unexpectedly uncontroversial in TRAN discussions, and approval today is expected, clearing the way for Parliament to move toward negotiations with EU countries over the final shape of the fund. The Council already agreed its partial negotiating position last December, while the overall budget figures remain tied to the wider EU budget talks.

MARITIME

EU FAST-TRACKS BLACK SEA SECURITY HUB: Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said on Monday that Brussels will accelerate work on a Black Sea Maritime Security Hub with Romania and Bulgaria and put €50 million behind it, as attacks on commercial shipping and other security incidents in the region intensify.

INDUSTRIAL ACCELERATOR ACT

IAA SHADOWS DISCUSS FDI: The Industrial Accelerator Act’s shadow rapporteurs are meeting today to discuss the regulation’s proposed foreign direct investment requirements. The FDI aspect is about the only part of the IAA that the entire European People’s Party agrees on, one Parliament official told Francesca Micheletti. The center-right group is aligned on needing to make the FDI requirements workable and avoid additional red tape, the person said.

Running out of time: As we previously reported, the shadow lawmakers are meeting weekly as they try to stick to an increasingly unlikely timeline of voting on the bill this year.

Extra burden: The FDI requirements have been criticized for adding a new layer of complexity to investments in the bloc, which are already subjected to the standard FDI screening and merger controls. The Commission proposal would introduce additional screening for foreign investments in key strategic sectors like batteries and takes a page from China’s book by pushing forced joint ventures.

Balancing conditions: Under the Commission proposal, foreign investments must meet four of six criteria, including limited foreign ownership and technology transfers. The Renew group in Parliament wants all six conditions to be met. The Socialists and Democrats group wants the screening mechanism extended to investors or subsidiaries. The Greens are pushing for lower ownership and screening thresholds.
