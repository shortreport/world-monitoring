EU auto suppliers losing ground to China
By Jordyn Dahl · Sep 17, 2026, 10:59 AM · View in your browser

Europe’s auto suppliers are announcing fewer job cuts, but pressure from Chinese imports is mounting.

Imports of Chinese automotive parts came to €5 billion in the first half of this year, a 23% increase compared to the same period in 2025, according to the latest figures from auto suppliers’ lobby CLEPA shared exclusively with POLITICO.

In 2020, the EU imported €1.8 billion worth of Chinese automotive parts, roughly the same as from the U.K. and Japan combined.

Europe’s automotive sector is bleeding jobs as imports from China increase while local sales stagnate and energy prices rise. Far-right political parties are seizing on the tumult to win over voters.

More worrying for the export-focused sector and for Europe’s economies is the sharp drop in automotive exports, which fell about 41% to €3.2 billion in the first half of 2026, compared to €5.4 billion in the first half of 2024.

“China captures an ever-growing share of Europe’s manufacturing base, while shutting the door on our exports,” Benjamin Krieger, the secretary-general of CLEPA, said in a statement. “Job losses are the visible symptom of a deeper problem: unfair competition.”

In the first half of the year, auto suppliers announced nearly 19,000 job cuts, down 35% from 29,250 layoffs in the second half of last year.
