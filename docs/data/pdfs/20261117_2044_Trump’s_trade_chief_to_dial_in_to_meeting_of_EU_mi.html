Trump’s trade chief to dial in to meeting of EU ministers
By Camille Gijs, Caroline Hug · Nov 17, 2026, 12:44 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer will skip a visit to Brussels to meet EU trade ministers at the end of November and will join the meeting virtually instead, an EU diplomat told POLITICO on Tuesday.

Greer had been due to attend an informal meeting of EU trade ministers in Dublin at the end of October, but declined citing scheduling conflicts. The Irish Presidency of the Council of the EU then invited Greer to attend their next meeting, on Nov. 30 in Brussels, POLITICO reported earlier. He will now attend this online.

The change in plan comes after President Donald Trump’s Republicans suffered a resounding defeat in the Nov. 3 U.S. midterm elections. The Democrats won big majorities in both houses of Congress, strengthening their power to scrutinize trade policy.

The ministerial gathering comes as the European Commission faces growing pressure to secure a reduction in U.S. duties on products containing steel, some of which are still tariffs of 50% — above a ceiling of 15% percent foreseen in the transatlantic trade deal struck last year at Trump’s golf resort in Turnberry, Scotland.

In line with the EU regulations implementing the truce, the Commission must report to the European Parliament and member governments by Dec. 1 on whether Washington is keeping its side of the bargain on steel tariffs.

The huddle also takes place against the backdrop of worries across EU countries and industry about the U.S. MATCH Act, which aims to rein in China’s access to chipmaking equipment and could have the far-reaching implications across the bloc.

The bill is gaining political momentum in Washington following Jensen Huang’s dramatic departure as CEO of Nvidia. Huang was involved in at least two extramarital affairs, including with entrepreneur Alice Huang, a Chinese national suspected of ties to Chinese intelligence.

The Office of the U.S. Trade Representative was contacted for comment.
