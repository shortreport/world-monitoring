DRIVING THE DAY

BACK IN THE LION’S DEN: Is Ursula von der Leyen the right person to make the case for the EU in the midst of a French presidential campaign that could have devastating results for the bloc’s future?

The European Commission president will deliver a major speech on the EU’s space policy at Emmanuel Macron’s space summit at Paris’ Grand Palais today, in front of an audience of industry leaders, government officials and military chiefs, Playbook’s Nick Vinocur writes.

Her message (we’re paraphrasing slightly): It’s only thanks to the EU that Europe is able to mount ambitious projects that can compete with other global powers.

Some see her address as timely: “When you consider that some of these CEOs were cozying up to [far-right leader] Jordan Bardella, this is really welcome,” said an EU diplomat following the developments, granted anonymity because they’re not authorized to speak on the record.

But others doubt that her pitch will land. “It’s a red-alert situation. I have never seen France as ill-disposed toward the EU as now,” said an EU official. “Brussels and the migrants — these are the two bogeymen.” In other words: Von der Leyen’s intervention might not have the pro-EU impact Brussels hopes it will.

It’s the second time in a month that von der Leyen has addressed an elite audience in France. The Commission president spoke to French business leaders on Aug. 27, highlighting Brussels’ work on simplifying EU regulation, building a capital markets union and making it easier for businesses to set up shop.

But the political backdrop is grim for Brussels. Far-right leader Marine Le Pen is leading the polls ahead of next year’s presidential election and far-left Euroskeptic Jean-Luc Mélenchon has a realistic shot at joining her in the second round. A Le Pen presidency could have dire consequences for the EU — and there are few strong voices in the presidential race willing to defend the bloc.

The EU official points out that there are two clearly pro-EU candidates running for France’s top job: Center-left MEP Raphaël Glucksmann and centrist former Prime Minister Gabriel Attal. “Edouard Philippe [from Macron’s camp] is pro-European, but not passionately so,” the official said. “It’s hard to get people focused on Europe at the moment.”

It’s still early days in the campaign, with seven months to go, and Brussels will be hoping that voters will pay more attention to the implications of a Le Pen or Mélenchon victory as the election gets closer. But selling the EU’s message to French elites is one thing — getting through to ordinary voters is another.

COSTA’S BUDGET TOUR GOES SOUTH

COSTA HEADS SOUTH: After a fraught meeting in Berlin on Wednesday, European Council President António Costa’s budget talks will today involve Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, Portugal’s Luís Montenegro and Malta’s Robert Abela.

The backdrop: Southern and eastern European countries pushing for a bigger budget are worried that the Irish Council presidency, which is steering the talks, will make major cuts in its next negotiating document (known as a negobox) in October, according to two diplomats from those countries.

Red lines: “Any cuts to agriculture and cohesion would be a no-go for us,” said one diplomat. There will be cuts, but they won’t be as drastic as those suggested by Germany, said a third diplomat.

Merz wants less: German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Tuesday renewed calls for Ireland to slash the budget by “several hundreds of billions.” During a joint press conference with Costa, Merz said the proposed increase is “simply prohibitive because, at the end, it is the European taxpayer who has to foot the bill.”

Club Med’s warning: But Spain, Portugal and Malta — who are members of the “friends of cohesion” camp — are expected to warn Costa against budget cuts, arguing they would leave Europe ill-equipped to fund old priorities while investing more in defense and competitiveness. “We cannot do more with less,” Spanish Secretary of State for the EU Fernando Sampedro said last week.

Visegrád’s special guest: With a few weeks to go until Ireland presents its negobox, the friends of cohesion are making their voice heard. Irish Taoiseach Micheál Martin will join a meeting of the Visegrád Group (the Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland and Slovakia) in Bratislava, to discuss the budget negotiations.

20-SECOND PLAYBOOK PRIMER

Next week, EU ministers responsible for cohesion policy will hold talks in Ireland. But what is cohesion policy? It’s the EU’s attempt to reduce economic and social differences among countries and regions and it has always formed a large part of the long-term budget (almost a third of it, in the current period). The EU splits regions into three groups based on their gross domestic product per person (compared to the EU average): less developed regions (with GDP per inhabitant is less than 75% of the EU average); transition regions (75%–100%); and more developed regions (more than 100%).

MIDTERM MANEUVERS

METSOLA’S MOMENTUM: Roberta Metsola is picking up support for a history-making third term as European Parliament president, with the right-wing European Conservatives and Reformists broadly backing her and the Socialists and Democrats softening their opposition.

Right turn: ECR Co-Chair Nicola Procaccini told Max Griera that support for Metsola is widely shared within his group. “She is balanced, she is fair and that is exactly what we ask,” he said. Metsola’s recent public praise for Italian PM Giorgia Meloni’s “commonsense solutions” on EU simplification also went down well with the group, he said.

Door left ajar: Meanwhile, S&D President Iratxe García didn’t rule out backing Metsola Tuesday. “I’m not going to set any condition from the start,” she said — a softer line than the Socialist leaders’ pre-summer position insisting the presidency should pass to the center-left at midterm.

Mark your calendars: The European People’s Party is preparing to formally select its Parliament presidential candidate at the end of November, according to a draft schedule seen by POLITICO. Metsola is expected to be renominated. Both the EPP and S&D will elect their own leaderships on Oct. 20.

Green rebellion: Elsewhere, Nicolae Ștefănuță has formally entered the Oct. 7 race to replace Bas Eickhout as Greens co-chair, campaigning for a more confrontational approach toward the EPP. “It is clear we need to be more in the opposition because the [anti-far right] firewall was never respected,” he said in his 17-page pitch sent to colleagues. Belgium’s Sara Matthieu and Denmark’s Kira Marie Peter-Hansen are also widely floated as contenders.

Hayer stays: At Renew, Valérie Hayer looks secure, with no challenger expected, with the group’s main factions favoring continuity.

BRUSSELS CORNER

WEATHER: Sunny intervals and slight breeze. High of 21C.

WHO’S CHICKEN? The Brussels bubble was clucking with surprise after former Commission President Jean-Claude Juncker posted on X yesterday. The Luxembourgish grandee appeared to log on to the platform for the first time in seven years to post an AI mock-up of himself dressed in a chicken costume, holding a pair of tacos.

Cluckup: Juncker, who maintains an office in the Berlaymont, couldn’t be reached for comment. But a spokesperson for the Commission said: “The former President’s account on X was indeed hacked. Necessary steps to address the matter were taken.” The post was quickly deleted.

ART ATTACK: From today through Sunday, the city becomes one giant gallery for Brussels Art Week, with public institutions, private foundations, auction houses and artist-run spaces opening their doors for four days of exhibitions and events. It’s your excuse to wander into buildings you normally walk past.

SOME CRISES ARE BETTER THAN OTHERS: British historian Timothy Garton Ash — chronicler of the 1989 revolutions and longtime thinker on Europe’s future — is coming to Brussels on Sept. 24. He’ll sit down with journalist Caroline de Gruyter and De Standaard editor Karel Verhoeven at Passa Porta for an event cheerfully titled “A Good Crisis (and How Not to Waste It).” On the menu: Euroskepticism, Europe’s place in the new world order and, presumably, how to make the most of … the current mess.

ALSO STARTING TODAY: The Marni Jazz Festival at Théâtre Marni near Flagey, running through Sept. 19 … and EAREYE Festival, with three days of dance workshops and performances curated by Swedish choreographer Alma Söderberg.

A NUMBER WORTH SAVING: As today is World Suicide Prevention Day, a reminder that Belgium’s Community Help Service runs a confidential, anonymous English-language helpline 24/7. Its trained volunteers receive thousands of calls a year, including from people feeling suicidal. CHS Helpline: 02 616 26 26.
