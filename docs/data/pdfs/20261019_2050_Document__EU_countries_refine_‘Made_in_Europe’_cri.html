Document: EU countries refine ‘Made in Europe’ criteria in industry bill
By Francesca Micheletti, Tom Schmidtgen · Oct 19, 2026, 1:50 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — EU countries have further refined their approach to determining which foreign-made goods can qualify for “Made in Europe” status under the proposed Industrial Accelerator Act, putting them at odds with a more restrictive position favored by European Parliament lawmakers.

The updated position was included in a third draft compromise on the IAA shared by the Irish presidency of the Council that was obtained by POLITICO.

The European Commission would have six months after the IAA enters into force to establish lists of third countries whose goods qualify for “partner origin” treatment, according to the Irish presidency draft, which will be discussed at a working party meeting on Oct. 26-27.

The draft also retains automotive provisions within the IAA, despite a Franco-German agreement reached last week proposing to move them into a separate legislative package.

It builds on the Council’s earlier approach to determining which foreign-made goods qualify for European preference, while drawing a clearer distinction between public procurement and other forms of public intervention.

Countries eligible for other forms of public intervention falling under the regulation’s scope are “any third country with which the Union has concluded an agreement establishing a free trade area or a customs union, and subject to the Union having relevant commitments under that agreement.”

The Commission shall use implementing acts to include or exclude third countries, the text says, in line with a previous version.

On foreign direct investment, offshore and onshore wind technologies are added to the sectors for which foreign investment would be subject to tight screening and conditions.
