Commission turns to artificial intelligence to write better laws
By Marianne Gros · Nov 4, 2026, 1:04 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — The European Commission is now using artificial intelligence to draft new laws and simplify existing ones, it said in an annual review of progress on its ongoing simplification drive.

As part of a major plan to cut red tape and administrative costs for businesses, the EU is making deep reforms to how it creates and enforces rules on everything from tech, agriculture and migration to environmental protection and taxation.

To do that, the Commission is deploying new digital tools internally to “modernise” lawmaking. The tools aim to help its staff to “identify similarities across legal acts, pointing at potential overlaps, inconsistencies or duplicates, as well as opportunities for simplification,” it said in an annual simplification report presented on Wednesday.

According to the document, the Commission is also beefing up legal training and staff to make sure new rules are not overly complex.

Since the start of her second term as Commission president, Ursula von der Leyen has made simplification a cornerstone of her political agenda. So far the EU has produced 12 so-called omnibus packages to revise and simplify existing legislation, which the Commission estimates will save the EU economy €22.3 billion in costs.

Brussels is also turning the screws on EU governments by pressuring them to implement laws properly. According to the report, the Commission launched 874 infringement procedures between August 2025 and July 2026, which is “the highest number in the last 10 years.”

The Commission is also pushing back on governments piling on extra requirements when transposing European legislation into national law, a concept known as “gold-plating.” The executive expects to sign a “pact against gold-plating” with EU governments by the end of the year.
