Political Risk Monitor - July 2026

The purchasing power parity (PPP)-GDP-weighted average of Eurasia Group’s political trajectories improved sharply in June. It rose well above recent peaks and reached levels last seen before President Donald Trump’s election in October 2024.
29 Jul 2026 10:41 EDT

Chart of the month

The current geopolitical risk environment reflects two offsetting forces. First, inflationary pressures have been building, amplified by the war with Iran and its effects on energy markets and supply chains. So far, however, these pressures have not prompted policy action, and volatility in global political stability remains subdued. Markets, by contrast, have reacted more quickly, as expectations tend to shift rapidly after geopolitical events (for an illustration of this dynamic in the context of midterm elections, see US midterms likely to shift expectations of Fed actions, 27 July 2026). Second, growth pressures are emerging. Upward revisions for economies exposed to AI software exporters partially offset downward revisions on countries caught in the war’s shadow.

Trends in global political stability

Political stability continued to decline in most countries in June, though gains in some smaller countries partly offset that trend. Society was the component that recorded the largest declines during the month. This overall stability remains broadly consistent with our assessment that the impacts of the Middle East conflict have been more limited than anticipated. Global growth remains resilient, supported by AI-driven investment and fiscal tailwinds. If geopolitical risks remain contained, stability scores should show even less volatility and may improve, though underlying economic conditions will likely remain strained (see: Escalation in the Middle East tests global resilience buffers, 23 July 2026). Against that backdrop, Romania’s political stability remains near historic lows, while the Dominican Republic’s is near historic highs.

Trajectory map

These maps present Eurasia Group’s updated short-term (six-month) and long-term (24-month) political trajectories. In June (since the last report) and so far in July:

  • Peru: The short- and long-term trajectories were upgraded from neutral to positive, reflecting an improved outlook for government stability under President-elect Keiko Fujimori, who is expected to complete her five-year term. Her administration will likely promote investment and economic policy continuity, while a fragmented but right-leaning congress will probably back her agenda and nominees, helping improve execution capacity. The main risks stem from strong opposition in several regions and among some segments of the population, which could intensify and trigger protests if Fujimori fails to improve security, responds poorly to El Niño disruptions, or weakens checks and balances (see: Upgrading trajectories on expected investment push and rare government stability, 22 July 2026).

EG geopolitical trends

  • The purchasing power parity (PPP)-GDP-weighted average of Eurasia Group’s political trajectories improved sharply in June. It rose well above recent peaks and reached levels last seen before President Donald Trump’s election in October 2024. The pace of improvement also accelerated significantly from recent months. In this context, resilience remains the key theme, as the Middle East conflict has had a more limited effect on the global macroeconomy and markets than many expected.
  • Volatility of political stability among the 89 countries tracked by Eurasia Group’s GPRI declined again in June, extending the moderation underway since August 2025. The downward trend has gathered pace and points to a more cautious stance among policymakers as the effects of the Middle East shocks unfold and elections approach in a number of countries.

Tracking changes in macroeconomic vulnerability and political capacity

Each month, Eurasia Group updates country scores that estimate forward-looking macro vulnerability (risk of welfare loss) and government political capacity (ability to respond to shocks and market pressure). See our methodology here.

Notable year-on-year movements

  • Hungary’s political capacity improved after opposition leader Peter Magyar and his Tisza party won a decisive victory and secured a two-thirds majority in the new parliament. They prevailed despite long odds and efforts by then-prime minister Viktor Orban to tilt the election and skew media coverage. Meanwhile, macroeconomic vulnerability worsened as rising fiscal pressure will probably undermine Magyar’s policy agenda and delay structural reforms in healthcare and education.
  • The Philippines’political capacity worsened as tensions rose between the administration of President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. and the rival Duterte family. Ongoing impeachment threats on both sides have deepened the strain and contributed to Marcos’s falling approval ratings. Macroeconomic vulnerability also increased because the country relies heavily on Middle East oil and gas imports, leaving it exposed to disruptions related to the Iran war. Looking ahead, El Niño poses growing economic risks, while efforts to curb food inflation would probably widen the deficit and further constrain fiscal space
  • South Africa’s political capacity worsened as President Cyril Ramaphosa faces ongoing impeachment proceedings, although removal in the near term remains unlikely. The process may continue until the African National Congress (ANC) elective conference in December 2027, when the party will choose a new leader. The ANC could then select a successor to Ramaphosa, who would serve out the remainder of his term through 2029. Meanwhile, macroeconomic vulnerability improved as fiscal consolidation helped South Africa achieve budget surpluses despite weak economic growth and external pressures.
