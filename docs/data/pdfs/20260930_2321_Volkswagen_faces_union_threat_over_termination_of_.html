Volkswagen faces union threat over termination of wage agreements
By Jules Darmanin · Sep 30, 2026, 4:20 PM · View in your browser

Germany’s largest industrial union Ig Metall threatened a “fierce dispute” Wednesday after the German carmaker told workers it was terminating multiple collective agreements, according to a union statement.

The agreements, which were on issues such as bonuses, trainings and social protection deals, would have taken effect on Jan. 1, 2027. They are “a clear declaration of war on the workforce,” Ig Metall’s chief negotiator Thorsten Gröger said.

“I expect a fierce dispute at the turn of the year,” Daniela Cavallo, the group’s labor chief, said in the statement.

The escalation in the dispute could worsen Volkswagen’s yearslong crisis. The group already announced sweeping cost-cutting measures earlier this month, including factory closures and 100,000 job cuts by 2030. Labor representatives are in talks with management over the plans.

It would also spell more trouble for Germany’s carmaking industry, which has struggled to fend off competition from China.

Spokespeople for Volkswagen did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
