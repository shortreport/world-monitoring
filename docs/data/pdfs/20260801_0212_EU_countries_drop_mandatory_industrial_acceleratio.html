EU countries drop mandatory industrial acceleration areas from IAA
By Francesca Micheletti · Jul 31, 2026, 7:11 PM · View in your browser

BRUSSELS — EU countries would no longer be required to designate industrial acceleration areas under the Industrial Accelerator Act, according to a second compromise text circulated by the Irish presidency of the Council.

“Member States may designate industrial manufacturing acceleration areas on their territory to cluster industrial manufacturing projects,” says the text seen by POLITICO, which was dated July 30. Under the Commission’s March proposal, each member country would have been required to designate an acceleration area within 12 months.

The text introduces the legal category of “partner origin” to determine which products from third countries can be treated as equivalent to EU origin. It sets out detailed conditions based on existing procurement and free trade agreements.

The Commission would adopt implementing acts — rather than delegated acts — establishing and maintaining lists of partner countries. That would give national governments a formal role in approving those decisions.

The text also revises the foreign investment chapter, allowing the Commission to intervene only at the request of national investment authorities, rather than opening reviews on its own initiative.

It also gives EU countries more flexibility on permitting by replacing the Commission’s proposal for a single permitting procedure with a system centred on a digital single access point and a coordinating authority, leaving permitting decisions with national authorities.

Key sectoral changes concern steel and automotive. The compromise specifies that the origin of steel is determined by the country where it is melted and poured, in line with the proposed EU measure on global steel overcapacity.

The compromise also retains a flexibility mechanism for automotive origin requirements, allowing manufacturers to rely on a self-declaration where at least 85 percent of the relevant vehicles they assembled the previous year complied with the EU-origin rules.
