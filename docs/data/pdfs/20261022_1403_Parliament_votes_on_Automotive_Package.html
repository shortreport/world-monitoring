DRIVING THE DAY

AUTO PACKAGE PLENARY VOTE DELAYED: A plenary vote on the package has been postponed to December to give time for negotiations on the Industrial Accelerator Act that “delivers a clear commitment on the automotive part,” according to a Parliament note seen by POLITICO. “Only if all parties agree that sufficient commitment is guaranteed on the IAA” can the vote take place in December. The IAA is being negotiated by three committees and is on a separate track from the auto package.

Timing update: The regulation reforming emissions targets for automakers is continuing to move through the Parliament, with the TRAN and ITRE committees voting on their opinions today. A “platform” of groups — the European People’s Party, the Socialists and Democrats and Renew — reached a compromise position that was scooped by POLITICO earlier this week.

What to expect: These are the compromise amendments that will be voted on in each of the two committees today. The shadow rapporteurs met on Wednesday afternoon and agreed to the compromise position, two people with knowledge of the discussions told Jordyn. TRAN and ITRE are giving opinions to ENVI, which is the lead committee on the CO2 regulation. ENVI is expected to vote on the file on Nov. 4.

The details: While many of the specifics mirror those put forward in the Franco-German deal on car emissions, Parliament’s position would give automakers a 2035 emissions reduction target of 87.5% from 2021 levels while Berlin and Paris want a 90% cut. It would also create a new category for vehicles running alternative fuels, which would then also qualify as zero-emissions under the regulation.

Not everyone is happy: “To put it bluntly, this is bad for the ramp-up of e-mobility,” Green MEP Michael Bloss, one of the shadow rapporteurs on the Automotive Package, said in a press briefing Wednesday. “It drastically lowers the requirements for electric vehicles, which in turn hinders the industry’s ability to position itself for the future. After all, there is no long-term future in internal combustion engines.”

A good compromise: The proposed Parliament position has stronger emissions targets than initially proposed by the EPP, which is leading the negotiations, said Renew’s shadow rapporteur Sigrid Friis. “There are elements in the deal I would rather have been without, but being pro-European also means being able to find common ground and deliver results, when knowing the alternative would be much worse,” she said.

IAA shadows meeting: Progress is continuing on the IAA, as well, with the shadow rapporteurs on the regulation meeting lead negotiators this morning in Strasbourg to start working on the 5,300 amendments submitted earlier this month.

Opening salvo: The meeting will be the first real exchange of views on the file, two parliamentary officials told our colleague Francesca Micheletti, adding that it will involve the three co-leaders and 21 shadow MEPs.

TRUCKS

ITALY’S PROTEST PROBLEM: Truckers and farmers protesting fuel prices and the cost of living blocked roads, motorway junctions and ports across southern Italy — disrupting food and fuel supplies. The mounting pressure is forcing the government into talks with the haulage sector.

Not one movement: Stefano Orlacchio, president of the opposition Democratic Party’s youth wing in Campania, told Martina the truckers’ protest is a broader struggle by people “suffocated by the cost of living and rising prices.” Protesters told Italian media they are being squeezed by high fuel prices, while some also blame EU environmental rules and carbon taxes for making the situation worse.

And it’s spreading: Disruption is also being felt in Lazio and Lombardy, in central and northern Italy, where fruit and vegetables from southern Italy are arriving late or in smaller quantities. Concerns are also growing over medicine deliveries and supplies to islands, where food and fuel shipments have been held up.

Public transport too: Campania transport operator EAV said diesel shortages could force cuts and cancellations to bus services, with some rail services also at risk from Saturday if regular fuel supplies are not restored. Universities have moved teaching online because of transport and fuel-supply problems, prompting students protests.

Salvini’s meeting: Infrastructure Minister Matteo Salvini met haulage representatives in Milan on Tuesday. Orlacchio said “nothing particularly substantial came out of it,” but national trade bodies including ANITA, Confartigianato Trasporti and Unatras said the government had reopened dialogue and committed to “immediate liquidity” for transport firms. The government pledged to immediately unlock €480 million in previously allocated support, with further resources to follow, but the concessions have not ended the protests, with blockades expected to continue until at least Oct. 27.

Why Brussels should care: What began as a southern Italian transport protest is turning into a wider logistics shock, hitting food production, fuel distribution, public transport and interregional supply chains. That could start to affect other countries that depend on Italian food supplies, including Germany, while also feeding into a broader protest trend spreading across parts of Europe.

AVIATION

FLIGHT PATH LAWSUIT: Some 370 families, representing around 900 residents of the Brussels region, joined a lawsuit against RNP 07L, an approach route used by aircraft landing at Zaventem’s Brussels Airport that has sparked noise complaints in the northwestern part of the Belgian capital. The announcement came from Free Air 4 Brussels, an NGO campaigning against excessive aircraft noise.

Double hit: The route is being challenged in two separate lawsuits against the Belgian federal state, one brought by the Brussels regional government and the other by affected families.

The RNP 07L route was introduced in 2025 and has been controversial ever since. It shifted much of the aircraft noise from eastern to northern Brussels, resulting in “eight times more people impacted,” according to Vincent Vanwijnsberghe, chair of Free Air 4 Brussels.

Reasons for the change: Aircraft landing at Brussels Airport previously used a VOR approach, a navigation procedure relying on ground-based radio signals. New EU Performance-Based Navigation rules require airports to introduce satellite-based approach procedures along with other requirements. These procedures are currently only used on one of the three runways at Brussels Airport, which explains why the Belgian government chose RNP 07L.

EU’s fault? In response to a written question from MEP Kathleen Van Brempt, Transport Commissioner Apostolos Tzitzikostas said that “the PBN regulation does not regulate the design and the use of PBN procedures. The terminal airspace design remains in the remit of the local authorities.”

Crucke route: Anti-noise activists have dubbed RNP 07L the “Crucke route,” after Belgium’s Mobility Minister Jean-Luc Crucke. “With the old VOR approach, we had around 3,200 landings [per year] only during the day,” Vanwijnsberghe told Tommaso. “Now, with the new Crucke route we have between 9,700 and 9,800 landings [a year]. It means three times more and it’s day and night.”

What’s next? The government is exploring alternatives to RNP 07L and is expected to decide on the route’s future by Oct. 31. Belgium’s mobility ministry did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

AIRPORT CHARGES HAVE LIMITED IMPACT ON FARES: An Oxera analysis commissioned by airport lobby ACI Europe found that airport charges have a limited impact on airfares. Other factors, such as increasing concentration in the airline sector, are more strongly associated with the final price, according to the study, which examined nearly 6,000 European routes.

Spoiler alert: The document, which Tommaso reviewed ahead of its release today, found that “a 10% increase in airline concentration is associated with fares that are 1.06% higher.” A similar increase in airport congestion is associated with fares approximately 0.93% higher.

By contrast, “a 10% increase in airport charges is associated with an average fare increase of approximately 0.35%.” The study concludes that “lower airport charges therefore do not mean lower fares.”

CARS

EV SALES UP, LED BY CHINESE BRANDS: Electric vehicles now account for nearly 23% of new car registrations in the EU market, according to the latest data from car lobby ACEA. That’s up from 16% in September of last year. While overall car sales are up 5.7% in the first nine months of the year, Chinese brands are seeing the most success.

Who’s up: State-owned automaker Chery saw its sales grow 247% year-over-year from 39,500 in the first nine months of 2025 to 137,000 this year. EV giant BYD saw similar success with its sales up 154%, while sales for Leapmotor, which is partnered with Stellantis, increased 394% year-over-year, going from 14,700 in the first nine months of 2025 to 73,000 this year.
