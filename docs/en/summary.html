[Heads Up] US and China are negotiating a rare earths-for-soybeans trade ahead of the Trump-Xi summit.

Treasury officials from both countries held online talks this weekend to pre-negotiate terms before the Shenzhen summit between President Trump and Xi Jinping. Washington is pushing Beijing to ease rare earth export restrictions; in exchange, the US wants China to follow through on soybean purchase commitments made in the Busan agreement. If China fails to deliver, expect retaliatory tariff threats. Failure to reach a deal at the summit raises the risk of renewed confrontation into 2027.

[Changed] Senate control remains undecided a week after midterms, and Trump is already alleging fraud.

Democrats are confirmed to have scored a strong House wave — 63 races shifted toward Democrats versus just 1 toward Republicans in final ratings. The Senate remains a toss-up, with Democrats needing a net of 4 seats and 7 races still uncalled, including traditionally safe GOP states like Kansas and Iowa. Trump has publicly claimed electoral fraud, setting up weeks of legal disputes and political instability. This uncertainty is already weighing on markets and complicating the administration's ability to project a unified front at the upcoming China summit.

[Changing Now] Vance is running a parallel back-channel on Iran while Treasury tightens the sanctions vise.

Vice President Vance is leading quiet negotiations on a new Iran nuclear framework even as Treasury sanctioned eight Canadian exchange firms this week for evading Iran sanctions. Washington has also posted a $20 million bounty for intelligence on Iran's nuclear program. Watch the next 72 hours for signs of whether this is genuine diplomatic opening or sanctions-first posturing — European allies are already criticizing the administration's hardline tilt, and a failed track could push toward confrontation by early 2027, including risks to Gulf shipping.

[Medium Term] China is pulling ahead on both EVs and advanced chips, reshaping industrial leverage for the next decade.

BYD's Yangwang U9 hit 503.17 km/h, a production-car speed record, underscoring how fast Chinese EV technology is closing the gap with — and surpassing — Western and even Tesla benchmarks. Separately, TSMC is reportedly considering producing advanced AI memory for Nvidia, deepening Taiwan's strategic indispensability in the global chip supply chain exactly as Beijing's coercive posture toward Taipei draws fresh international scrutiny. Plug-in and EV vehicles now account for 25% of global new car sales. The combination — Chinese manufacturing dominance plus Taiwan's chip chokehold — means rare earth and semiconductor leverage will remain the central battleground in US-China relations well into 2027, regardless of how this week's summit goes.
