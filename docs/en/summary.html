[Heads Up] Trump says midterm politics won't stop his Iran campaign — expect the strikes to continue.

US forces have hit Iran for 10 consecutive days since July 11, targeting Hormuz Strait military capacity. Iran has retaliated by striking a US base in Bahrain and an Amazon data center; a Kuwaiti-flagged tanker was hit with crew injuries. Mediators' 10-day ceasefire proposal has stalled because Washington is setting preconditions for talks. Trump told reporters directly that midterm elections will not factor into his war planning — treat this as confirmation there is no near-term off-ramp.

[Changed] Trump revived a 1930s trade law to threaten Canada with 50% tariffs, days after a friendly meeting with Carney.

The White House invoked Section 338, a dormant Depression-era statute, to threaten 50% retaliatory tariffs on Canadian goods — wine, autos, even hockey sticks. This is a significant expansion of the administration's trade toolkit beyond the tariff authorities used against China. The reversal came abruptly after what looked like a cordial exchange with PM Mark Carney, spooking markets already jittery over Iran. USMCA's framework is now in question; watch for Ottawa's response this week.

[Changing Now] Hormuz disruption is starting to show up in food prices, not just oil.

Eurasia Group flags that agriculture prices are set to rise as a second Hormuz shock hits supply buffers — fertilizer and grain shipping routes run through the same chokepoint as crude. Combined with tanker attacks continuing daily, expect energy and food price pressure to compound over the next 72 hours. This is a new transmission channel beyond the oil-price story markets have been watching. Any further tanker incident will likely accelerate the move.

[Medium Term] China's EV industry is heading toward a shakeout — margins have hit the floor.

Chinese auto sales are down 20%, on pace for the worst year since 2021, with manufacturer margins collapsed to 1.5% — just 1,500 yuan profit on a 100,000 yuan car. Expect bankruptcies and inventory dumping within 8-9 weeks as the price war becomes unsustainable, with knock-on pressure on Japanese and European rivals. Separately, TSMC's $26.5B expansion in the US and record-pace Kumamoto build-out signal the industry's broader hedge against Taiwan geopolitical risk — a multi-year structural shift, not a one-off announcement.
