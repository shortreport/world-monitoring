[Heads Up] A sunken ship in the Strait of Hormuz is the most dangerous flashpoint on the board.

Iran says the vessel struck a mine; this comes two days after an IRGC commander publicly threatened to sink a U.S. warship. Mediators are scrambling to open a back-channel between Washington and Tehran, but the sequence—mine incident, threat, counter-threat—is the classic setup for miscalculation. Eurasia Group puts moderate odds on armed escalation within 2-4 weeks, likely timed around the November midterms. Any U.S. retaliatory strike or further Iranian provocation in the Strait would hit oil shipping routes immediately.

[Changed] Chip supply chains moved decisively toward the U.S. and away from China risk.

TSMC posted 68.7% net profit growth on AI demand and is now actively discussing a Texas expansion, including talks with Musk's Terafab. Separately, Japan and the U.S. governments confirmed a ¥2-3.6 trillion semiconductor investment package for New York and Texas fabs, with Tokyo Electron and Tokyo Ohka already committed. The EU also locked in a deal capping Chinese hybrid/PHEV imports at roughly half of 2026 levels starting December—real relief for European automakers, but Brussels signals it's temporary. Together these moves confirm the diversification-away-from-China trend is now capital, not just rhetoric.

[Changing Now] Xi and Trump meet in Shenzhen with an AI deal on the table—watch for a collapse or a facade.

The summit is set against a backdrop of Q3 bilateral trade up 4.1%, suggesting both sides want a stabilization narrative. But the core dispute—U.S. industrial protectionism versus China's AI technology ambitions—remains unresolved, and analysts expect any agreement to be narrow or largely symbolic. Watch the next 48-72 hours for whether the two sides produce concrete AI-governance language or just a photo-op. A visible split here would spill over into the already-tense chip and trade relationship.

[Medium Term] Allied trust in Washington is eroding faster than the headlines suggest.

Trump's net approval hit -27 points, his worst of the term, with Democrats now favored to take the House; data centers have become an unexpected wedge issue even in red states. Japanese polling shows younger voters now rank Trump's America as a bigger threat than China—even as Tokyo commits trillions to U.S. chip plants. That contradiction (big investment, deep distrust) is the real story: allies are hedging economically while diversifying diplomatically, a pattern that will shape trade and security negotiations well into next year regardless of who controls Congress after November.
