[Heads Up] Iran war has no exit ramp — Hormuz remains the live wire.

Eurasia Group's assessment is blunt: Trump's last-minute pause on escalation does not create an off-ramp with Iran. Iran fired fresh missiles at US targets overnight; Washington and Riyadh are hitting back at Iran-aligned proxies. Oman-brokered talks on a Hormuz Strait management split have stalled — Iran rejected the proposal outright. A full closure of Hormuz would hit global oil supply within days, not weeks; watch for any tanker incidents or insurance-market signals in the next 72 hours.

[Changed] Kumamoto quake knocked out a chunk of the chip and auto supply chain overnight.

Toyota shut three Fukuoka plants and Honda idled its Kumamoto plant through July 31. TSMC's Kumamoto fab checked out safe with only minor damage — the bullet was dodged on the most systemically important site. But the disruption cascaded into beverage and electronics plants across the region. Expect the real supply-chain pain — for Apple, Tesla, and other downstream buyers — to surface in August-September, not immediately.

[Changing Now] Washington is opening three fronts against Beijing at once — robots, rare earths, and Taiwan.

The US just banned Chinese-made humanoid robots, which control 85% of the global market — a direct shot at China's fastest-growing export category. In response, China is expanding its rare earths export-control leverage, per Eurasia Group's daily brief — a chokepoint the US has no quick substitute for. Simultaneously, Forbes and other outlets are pushing Trump to approve a $14 billion Taiwan arms package, which Beijing will almost certainly answer with protest or military posturing. Any one of these three could trigger a market-moving retaliation announcement within days.

[Medium Term] China's EV push into Japan is the quiet story that outlasts today's crises.

BYD's low-price kei-car entry into Japan — a market Toyota has owned for decades — signals China is now competing on Japan's own turf, not just in Europe and emerging markets. BYD's stated ambition to overtake Toyota as the world's top automaker by 2030 is no longer dismissed as bluster given its EU plug-in hybrid dominance. Combine this with the US-China tech decoupling (robots, rare earths, semiconductors) and Japan sits at the fault line of a restructuring global industrial order. Separately, weak Trump approval numbers and tight Senate races (Michigan, Maine) mean midterm outcomes will start shaping Fed rate expectations well before November — a signal markets are only beginning to price in.
