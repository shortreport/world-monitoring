[Heads Up] The US-Iran ceasefire is a pause, not peace — and Eurasia Group says there's no off-ramp yet.

After 13 straight days of strikes, the US halted attacks on July 25, reportedly over ammunition shortages, and diplomatic talks have begun. Oil fell 7%, with WTI dropping to the $83 range on de-escalation hopes. But Trump's own statements leave the door open to resuming strikes, and the core disputes — Iran's nuclear program and regional influence — remain unresolved. Treat this as a tactical timeout, not a settlement; watch for signs talks are collapsing in the next few weeks.

[Changed] US global standing has flipped: China now polls better than America in Canada and Mexico.

For the first time, international favorability toward China has overtaken the US, including among two of America's closest neighbors. This comes as Congress commits to annual bipartisan China trade delegations following a positive visit — a sign lawmakers see economic engagement with Beijing as necessary regardless of the administration's posture. The shift suggests erosion of the US-led alliance structure is no longer theoretical but measurable in polling data. This matters directly for any coalition-building on Taiwan, Iran, or trade.

[Changing Now] China is tightening its grip on rare earths just as Washington doubles down on Taiwan.

TSMC is adding $100 billion to its Arizona buildout, Congress has approved new Taiwan defense funding, and US think tanks are now framing Japan as the linchpin of Indo-Pacific strategy against China. Simultaneously, Eurasia Group flags Beijing expanding rare-earth export controls — a lever that hits chipmakers and automakers alike. Watch the next 72 hours for Beijing's response to the Taiwan funding and TSMC news; a punitive rare-earth move or new military pressure signal is the tell.

[Medium Term] Auto industry margins are cracking under tariffs even as demand holds — a preview of the next 6-12 months.

Hyundai's Q2 profit fell 20% despite higher sales, a direct signal that tariff and cost pressures are squeezing every automaker with global supply chains, not just one. In response, Nissan and Honda are merging software platforms to jointly counter US and Chinese EV competitors, while a new Senate bill targets Chinese, Russian, North Korean and Iranian tech in connected vehicles — forcing costly redesigns industry-wide. Combined with Fed policy likely shifting on midterm expectations, expect margin compression and forced consolidation to become the dominant auto-sector story through year-end.
