Beijing targeted cabinet ministers in both countries specifically because they maintain diplomatic relations with Taipei, prompting Washington to publicly back both nations against what it called Chinese "coercion." Expect this to escalate alongside broader US-China friction heading into November. This is a preview of tactics China may deploy more broadly if Taiwan relations deteriorate further.

[Changing Now] EU auto policy is being rewritten in real time, and Chinese manufacturers are already finding the loophole.

A tentative EU-China deal caps hybrid imports at 350,000 vehicles in 2027 (rising 6% annually), with safeguards starting Dec. 1 — a win for Stellantis, Renault and VW. But Chinese luxury brands like XPeng are sidestepping the whole framework by manufacturing inside Europe through partners like Magna and Stellantis itself, directly undercutting Mercedes, BMW and Porsche on price. Separately, a Franco-German deal on "Made in Europe" rules is reportedly "hours or days" away, even as a center-right revolt threatens to gut the broader Industrial Accelerator Act over WTO concerns. The next 72 hours will show whether Brussels can close the local-manufacturing loophole before it becomes the dominant market entry strategy.

Tokyo is quietly exploring hedges against US dependency. If this sentiment spreads to other allies before November's midterms, it compounds Washington's leverage problem just as China tightens the screws on Taiwan's remaining partners — a dual erosion of US influence on two fronts simultaneously.
