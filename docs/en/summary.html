EXECUTIVE BRIEFING
Tuesday, July 21, 2026  |  World Intelligence Monitor
[Heads Up] US strikes on Iran have run 10 straight days, and de-escalation is now off the table.

American forces have hit Iranian targets every day since July 11, and Eurasia Group assesses that the deaths of 2 US troops make a negotiated cooldown far less likely in the near term. Iran's Revolutionary Guard claims it destroyed a US drone hangar in Bahrain, and Trump has signaled he will retaliate further for the troop deaths. Oil markets are already pricing in the risk: Eurasia Group's oil desk expects prices to keep rising as multiple conflicts squeeze supply simultaneously. Watch the Strait of Hormuz closely — a full closure would be a global energy-shock event, not a regional one.

[Changed] Beijing has shifted from state-level pressure to squeezing ordinary Taiwanese citizens and businesses.

China's campaign now targets children's choirs, baristas, and other civilian-level targets in Taiwan, alongside stepped-up coast guard patrols through the Taiwan Strait that are already disrupting East Asian shipping. Eurasia Group's read is that Beijing is using the current US truce window to expand maritime control incrementally, not to prepare a single dramatic move. A US senator has now publicly warned of a full economic blockade risk, pushing Washington toward considering countermeasures. This is a change in tempo and visibility, not a new policy — but it raises the odds of a miscalculation into August.

[Changing Now] Japan's defense minister has opened the nuclear weapons question publicly for the first time.

Citing accelerating regional rearmament, Japan's defense chief is now calling for an open debate on nuclear options — a marked departure from postwar policy. This lands as Secretary Rubio plans Quad talks with India, Australia, and Japan, and as Tokyo quietly explores 'Plan B' security arrangements outside strict reliance on the US umbrella. Watch for how China reacts rhetorically and militarily in the next 72 hours; Eurasia Group flags August–September as the window where an overreaction could trigger a direct incident. This is happening in parallel with, and is partly driven by, the Iran conflict's demonstration that US attention and resources are stretched thin.

[Medium Term] The US-China tech and minerals split is now structural, not a negotiating position.

Chinese rare earth magnet exports remain 20% below pre-trade-war levels even after the Trump-Xi agreement, showing the decoupling has outlasted the diplomacy meant to fix it. Taiwan's chip dominance is becoming the central fault line, and Eurasia Group expects additional US sanctions as soon as September. Separately, BYD executives are now openly declaring they've passed Toyota globally without needing the US market at all, while Chinese domestic auto sales just posted their worst drop since 2021 — a sign China's EV industry is pivoting hard toward export dominance. Together, these threads point to a 2027 horizon where Western industrial policy is reacting to a Chinese lead already locked in, not preventing one.
