[Heads Up] US and Iran are close to a temporary deal on Hormuz Strait shipping lanes, brokered by Oman, but the two sides are already contradicting each other publicly.

Trump says talks are progressing and has warned Iran this is its 'last chance'; Tehran denies direct talks with Washington while confirming progress through Oman. A deal could land within days, and oil markets have already priced in de-escalation. But the gap between the two narratives is wide — if talks collapse, expect rapid military escalation, compounded by reports that US missile stockpiles are thinning and could limit response capacity within weeks. Watch for confirmation of an actual routing agreement versus continued rhetorical posturing.

[Changed] Japan and the US executed a historic coordinated yen-buying intervention exceeding ¥4 trillion (roughly $27B+) on July 31.

Treasury Secretary Bessent called it symbolic of deepened US-Japan alignment, not just a market fix. Both governments are signaling willingness to intervene again, and are setting up dollar-funding mechanisms through intermediary firms for future action. This is a step-change from prior ad hoc interventions — it reads as institutionalized policy coordination. The risk: if Japan delays rate hikes, carry-trade pressure could resurface and force a third intervention before month-end.

[Changing Now] A $14 billion Taiwan arms package is headed to Trump's desk just as his China trip approaches, setting up a direct test of his Taiwan policy.

Former House Foreign Affairs Chair Michael McCaul is delivering the sale proposal to Trump personally; Taiwan's President Lai Ching-te has already honored McCaul, underscoring how high-stakes this is for Taipei. China is pressing hard for cancellation. The real test comes when Trump travels to China this month — if Taiwan becomes a bargaining chip in broader trade or Iran-adjacent diplomacy, expect immediate market and regional security reaction. Watch whether the package gets signed before or after that trip.

[Medium Term] A US startup CEO's warning that China may be pulling ahead in 'physical AI' — robotics and embodied systems — points to a slower-burn but higher-stakes shift than this week's headlines.

This comes as Washington prepares another $20 billion round of tariffs on China next week, deepening a trade fight that's now inseparable from the tech-competition narrative. If China's lead in physical AI holds, it has direct implications for defense manufacturing and industrial competitiveness over the next 6-12 months, not just consumer tech. Separately, Europe's energy crisis (Romanian drought forcing nuclear plant shutdowns and auto plant closures) is a preview of recurring industrial disruption that will keep resurfacing each summer — worth tracking for supply-chain exposure, especially for Japanese automakers reliant on European parts.
