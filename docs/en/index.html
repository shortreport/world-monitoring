The World in Brief: Le Pen says she will run for president
2026-07-08 09:49 JST

Today's Top Stories

  • Marine Le Pen, France's far-right populist leader, has announced her candidacy for next year's presidential election following a court's decision to reduce her prison sentence and shorten her electoral ban. Though her conviction on embezzlement charges was upheld, she has vowed to appeal again.
  • Nigel Farage, leader of Britain's Reform Party, has announced his resignation from Parliament and plans to stand for re-election in his local Clacton constituency. He has cast the move as part of a broader battle between the people and entrenched interests, amid intensifying scrutiny of financial matters.
  • The US Treasury has revoked its temporary authorization for Iranian oil sales following reports of attacks on shipping by Iranian forces in the Strait of Hormuz. The measure had been introduced under a ceasefire agreement reached last month. Brent crude has risen to around $75 per barrel.
  • Donald Trump, at a NATO summit, has criticised European allies and renewed his proposal for American annexation of Greenland. He has also suggested that both Russia and Ukraine are seeking an end to the war.
  • A bomb attack near where French President Emmanuel Macron was visiting in Damascus has left at least 18 people wounded. Though the explosion occurred close to his location, French authorities confirmed his safety and his scheduled meeting with Syrian President Bashar al-Assad proceeded as planned.
  • Hungary's state television MTVA has ceased broadcasting, displaying a black screen and an apology message. Prime Minister Péter Magyar, who took office in April, has ordered sweeping reforms to eliminate propaganda from the Orbán era and transition the channel toward independent media.
  • The International Olympic Committee has provisionally lifted its ban on Russia's Olympic Committee, which had been in place since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The move could expand opportunities for Russian athletes to compete at the 2028 Los Angeles Games.

The Day Ahead

  • The IMF releases its World Economic Outlook on Wednesday. Global economic growth is expected to remain above 3%, supported by the continuation of the Iran ceasefire and Brent crude falling below $80 a barrel.
  • Strong investment in AI infrastructure and semiconductors is underpinning demand, helping the world economy offset the effects of rising energy prices and trade friction. The global economy is proving more resilient than expected.
  • Copenhagen tops The Economist Intelligence Unit's liveability index for the second consecutive year, with Vienna in second place. Tokyo is the only megacity to crack the top ten, ranking tenth.
  • The Iran conflict has depressed city scores across the Middle East. Dubai has fallen four places and Muscat has dropped fourteen. Chinese cities have risen thanks to improved healthcare scores.
  • UniCredit announces the results of its bid for Commerzbank on Wednesday. The bank, which already holds over 40%, is seeking to make additional purchases on the open market to avoid triggering antitrust rules.
  • Trinidad and Tobago's anti-homosexuality law faces a challenge in the British Privy Council on Wednesday. Activists hope the law, which was struck down as unconstitutional in 2018 but was revived in March 2025, will be struck down again.
