The World in Brief: Le Pen says she will run for president

Today's Top Stories

  • Marine Le Pen, France's far-right populist leader, has announced her candidacy for next year's presidential election following an appeals court's reduction of her prison sentence and political ban. Though her embezzlement conviction was upheld, she vowed to appeal again.
  • Nigel Farage, leader of Britain's Reform Party, has announced his resignation from Parliament and will stand as a candidate in a by-election in his local Clacton constituency. He framed the move against a backdrop of increased scrutiny over financial matters as a battle between the people and entrenched interests.
  • The US Treasury has revoked its temporary authorisation for Iranian oil sales following reports of attacks on vessels by Iranian forces in the Strait of Hormuz. The measure had been introduced under a ceasefire agreement reached last month. Brent crude rose to around $75 per barrel.
  • Donald Trump, at a NATO summit, criticised European allies and reiterated his proposal for American annexation of Greenland. He claimed both Russia and Ukraine wish to reach an agreement to end the war.
  • An explosion in Damascus during French President Emmanuel Macron's visit has injured at least 18 people. Though the blast occurred near Macron's accommodation, French authorities confirmed his safety and his scheduled meeting with Syrian President Assad proceeded as planned.
  • Hungary's state broadcaster MTVA went off air, displaying a black screen and an apology message. Prime Minister Péter Magyar, who took office in April, ordered sweeping reforms to counter propaganda broadcasting under the previous Orbán administration, with plans to transition the outlet to independent media.
  • The International Olympic Committee has provisionally lifted its ban on the Russian Olympic Committee, which had been imposed following the 2023 invasion of Ukraine. The move could expand opportunities for Russian athletes to compete at the 2028 Los Angeles Games.

The Day Ahead

  • The IMF releases its World Economic Outlook on Wednesday. Global economic growth is expected to remain above 3%, supported by the continuation of the Iran ceasefire and Brent crude oil staying below $80 a barrel.
  • Robust investment in AI infrastructure and semiconductors is underpinning demand, allowing the world economy to offset some of the impact from rising energy prices and trade friction. The global economy is proving more resilient than expected.
  • Copenhagen tops the Economist Intelligence Unit's liveability index for the second consecutive year, with Vienna in second place. Tokyo is the only megacity to crack the top ten, ranking tenth.
  • The conflict in Iran has depressed city scores across the Middle East. Dubai has fallen four places and Muscat has slipped fourteen. Chinese cities have climbed the rankings thanks to improvements in healthcare scores.
  • UniCredit announces the results of its takeover bid for Commerzbank on Wednesday. The Italian bank, which already holds over 40%, is seeking to acquire more shares on the open market to avoid antitrust concerns.
  • Trinidad and Tobago's anti-gay law faces scrutiny at the British Privy Council on Wednesday. Activists hope the court will overturn the legislation once more, after it was ruled unconstitutional in 2018 before being revived in March 2025.
