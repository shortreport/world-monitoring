The World in Brief: Le Pen says she will run for president
2026-07-08 09:49 JST

Today's Top Stories

  • Marine Le Pen, the French far-right populist leader, has announced her candidacy for next year's presidential election after an appeals court reduced her prison sentence and political ban. Though her embezzlement conviction was upheld, she has pledged to appeal again.
  • Nigel Farage, leader of Britain's Reform party, has announced his resignation as an MP and will stand for re-election in his Clacton constituency. He frames the contest as a battle between the people and entrenched interests, citing increasing scrutiny over financial matters.
  • The US Treasury has revoked its temporary licence allowing Iranian oil sales. The move follows reports of attacks on vessels by Iranian forces in the Strait of Hormuz and reverses a measure introduced under last month's ceasefire agreement. Brent crude rose to around $75 a barrel.
  • Donald Trump criticised European allies at a NATO summit and renewed his proposal for American annexation of Greenland. He claimed that both Russia and Ukraine want to end the war.
  • A bomb attack in Damascus during President Macron's visit to the French capital wounded at least 18 people. The explosion occurred near his accommodation, but French authorities confirmed his safety and his talks with Syrian President Assad proceeded as planned.
  • Hungary's state broadcaster MTVA went off air, displaying a black screen and an apology message. Since taking office in April, new Prime Minister Péter Magyar has ordered sweeping reforms to eliminate propaganda from the Orbán era and transition the channel to independent media.
  • The International Olympic Committee has provisionally lifted its ban on the Russian Olympic Committee. The sanctions were imposed after the 2023 invasion of Ukraine, but Russian athletes may now have expanded opportunities to compete at the 2028 Los Angeles Games.

The Day Ahead

  • The IMF releases its World Economic Outlook on Wednesday. With the Iranian ceasefire holding and Brent crude expected to remain below $80 a barrel, global growth is forecast to stay above 3%.
  • Strong investment in AI infrastructure and semiconductors is underpinning demand, allowing the world economy to offset some of the impact from higher energy prices and trade friction. Global resilience is proving stronger than expected.
  • Copenhagen tops the Economist Intelligence Unit's liveability ranking for the second consecutive year, with Vienna in second place. Tokyo is the only megacity to crack the top ten, at tenth.
  • War in Iran has dragged down city scores across the Middle East. Dubai has fallen four places and Muscat fourteen. Chinese cities have risen thanks to improved healthcare scores.
  • UniCredit announces the results of its takeover bid for Commerzbank on Wednesday. Already holding over 40%, it aims to acquire more shares on the open market to avoid triggering antitrust rules.
  • Trinidad and Tobago's anti-gay law faces a legal challenge in the Privy Council on Wednesday. Activists hope for its repeal after it was ruled unconstitutional in 2018, only to be revived in March 2025.
