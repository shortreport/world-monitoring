Today's Key Takeaways
2026-07-10 12:59 JST  ·  ~1 min read

Canada's new prime minister signaled today he wants to deepen commercial ties with China, including potential Chinese electric vehicle manufacturing on Canadian soil. Simultaneously, the EU is wrestling with how to write "Made in Europe" rules that would preference local content and scrutinize foreign investment—a direct response to the same competitive pressures Ottawa faces. Both moves reflect a global auto industry caught between efficiency and self-protection.

These aren't separate debates. If Canada opens its door to Chinese EV makers while the US tightens its stance, manufacturers face a fragmented North American market. Meanwhile, the EU's proposed local-content rules could force automakers—especially those with global supply chains—to restructure operations or face procurement penalties. Add Iran sanctions back into the mix and raw material costs could shift unpredictably. The industry's assumption of a stable, integrated continental supply chain is unraveling.

Watch whether the US signals explicit objections to Canadian Chinese investment within the next two weeks, which would force Carney's hand faster than current pressure.
