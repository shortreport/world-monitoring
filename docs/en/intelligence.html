Key Takeaways
2026-07-15 07:02 JST  ·  ~1 min read

The most consequential development is the emerging fragmentation of global EV supply chains along political lines. Brazil's reinstated tariff quotas on imported EV components, combined with the USMCA joint review stalling over stricter rules-of-origin ahead of the 2026 deadline, show that North America and Latin America are both hardening trade barriers around EV parts just as China's automakers and suppliers expand their footprint in these same markets. Japanese suppliers showing up in force at Guangzhou's electronics summit while simultaneously needing briefings on how to navigate USMCA and Mercosur rules confirms that supply chain strategy now requires country-by-country political calculation, not a single global sourcing plan. Executives with exposure to Mexico, Brazil, or U.S. assembly should treat the August webinars as early warning intelligence on where compliance costs and content requirements are headed before the 2026 deadline hits.

The second signal worth flagging is the steady, unglamorous continuation of EV demand in core European markets — Germany's registrations still led by BEVs and PHEVs
