Burnham and Macron agree to ‘find a way forward’ on ‘Made in Europe’ rules
SEP 4 00:00  ·  2 MIN READ
Today's Key Takeaways
2026-09-08 14:48 JST  ·  ~1 min read

EU regulators restarted negotiations today on car CO2 emission standards, with the environmental working group debating concrete targets for the first time since talks stalled. The bloc is under pressure to set rules that manufacturers can actually meet while competing against Chinese and American rivals who face looser requirements. The EU trade chief is separately meeting with corporate leaders on September 18 to discuss reducing dependence on Chinese supply chains, signaling the commission views automotive sourcing as a critical vulnerability.

The stakes are simple: stricter CO2 rules without relief on tariffs or supply chain costs could price European carmakers out of their own market. China's EV makers are undercutting them on price, and the U.S. has locked up domestic battery supply. If Brussels tightens emissions rules while Chinese competitors avoid the same standards, manufacturers will shift production or lose sales.

Watch whether the EU ties any CO2 relief to progress on reshoring critical components, particularly batteries and semiconductors.
