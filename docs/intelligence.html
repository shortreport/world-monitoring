Today's Key Takeaways
2026-09-28 11:33 JST  ·  ~1 min read

Britain said today it will keep tariffs on Chinese-made electric vehicles under review rather than locking in a policy. This leaves the door open for those vehicles to enter the British market, depending on political and economic conditions over the next year or two. For carmakers with Chinese supply chains or plans to source EVs from China, this is a potential opening—but only if Britain decides tariffs aren't worth the cost.

Germany and Spain are deadlocked over what counts as "Made in Europe" under new EU industrial rules. Germany wants to set the bar high, requiring up to 80 percent local content, while Spain is pushing for a tiered approach that would be easier to meet. The stakes are real: stricter rules protect domestic production but risk making European cars too expensive to compete globally. Watch whether the EU Commission can broker a compromise before these rules take effect.
