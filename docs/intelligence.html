Today's Key Takeaways
2026-08-21 10:51 JST  ·  ~1 min read

Korean manufacturers are pulling investment out of China and redirecting it to the U.S. and Vietnam, according to Japan's trade agency. The shift reflects deepening concern about U.S.-China tensions and the deteriorating competitive position Chinese rivals pose. China's auto market, long a growth engine for foreign automakers, is becoming less attractive as domestic competitors undercut prices and grab market share.

This matters because it signals where the industry sees the next decade playing out. Companies are betting that tariff walls and deglobalization will be permanent features, not temporary friction. The U.S. and Southeast Asia are the safer bets. Automakers that don't make this same calculation risk being shut out of high-tariff zones and facing supply chain bottlenecks.

Watch whether Trump's reported tariff deal with Canada signals a broader rollback of trade barriers or a preview of bilateral agreements that lock others out.
