Today's Key Takeaways
2026-09-25 13:30 JST  ·  ~1 min read

Germany and Spain are deadlocked over how strictly to define "Made in Europe" rules for electric vehicles and green technology. Germany wants looser standards that allow more non-EU parts, while Spain is pushing for tighter local-content requirements. The fight matters because automakers depend on global supply chains—too rigid a rule raises costs and limits where they can source components, while too loose a rule undermines the EU's push to move manufacturing away from China.

The real stakes emerge in January when the EU's carbon border tax kicks in. That mechanism will penalize imports with high embedded carbon, forcing every automaker to choose: build in Europe under strict rules, or face tariffs. Watch whether Germany or Spain wins this argument by early October, when the EU is expected to finalize its industrial acceleration law.
