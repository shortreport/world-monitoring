Today's Key Takeaways
2026-11-11 13:48 JST  ·  ~1 min read

China's battery maker CATL and German energy storage firm Intilion are partnering to deploy 3 gigawatt-hours of battery storage systems across Europe, signaling aggressive Chinese expansion into the continent's power infrastructure. Meanwhile, Germany's EV subsidy program is burning through its budget two years ahead of schedule, exhausted by surging electric vehicle demand. Both developments point to a widening gap: Chinese companies are building out the supply chain Europe needs to support mass EV adoption, while European governments are running short of cash to actually buy the vehicles.

For automakers, this creates a squeeze. Battery costs are falling as Chinese competitors scale up production capacity, which should help margins. But European subsidies—which have been critical to EV affordability and sales volume—are disappearing faster than anyone planned. Dealers and manufacturers betting on subsidy-driven demand face a harsh reset. Separately, EU ambassadors are still deadlocked over an auto package of regulations, leaving the industry in limbo on rules that could reshape competition within months.

Watch whether the EU can agree on the auto package before Ireland's presidency ends in December, and whether any country extends subsidies to prevent a demand cliff.
