Today's Key Takeaways
2026-09-11 10:20 JST  ·  ~1 min read

The Trump administration is preparing to crack down on Chinese goods flowing through Mexico and Vietnam, likely imposing additional penalties on products transshipped through these countries to evade tariffs. This marks a shift in enforcement strategy—the administration appears ready to tighten monitoring of indirect routes into the U.S. market rather than relying solely on direct tariffs on Chinese imports.

For automakers, this creates a two-front compliance problem. Companies that source components from China or manufacture in China for export face higher costs if they route goods through Mexico or Vietnam to avoid tariffs. At the same time, the EU is tightening its own rules in opposite directions: some member states are pushing back against strict local manufacturing requirements in the Industrial Acceleration Act, while key European Parliament members want those requirements strengthened. This leaves manufacturers caught between a U.S. crackdown on workarounds and European fragmentation over what "made in Europe" actually means.

Watch whether the Trump administration moves to formally define transshipment penalties before taking office.
