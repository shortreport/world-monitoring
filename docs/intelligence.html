Today's Key Takeaways
2026-11-04 10:04 JST  ·  ~1 min read

Thailand's cabinet approved a tripling of import duties on electric vehicles to 35 percent starting January 2027, up from 10 to 15 percent today. The move directly targets Chinese EV makers flooding Southeast Asian markets at low prices. For Western automakers, it opens a window: higher tariffs on Chinese competitors make local assembly or partnerships with Thai producers more economically attractive than importing finished vehicles.

Europe's parliament rejected binding EV fleet targets for corporate buyers, shifting them from mandatory to voluntary. This weakens what Brussels intended as a demand-side lever to force automakers into faster electrification. The auto industry gains breathing room on compliance, but loses clarity on how quickly European fleet emissions will actually fall—making long-term investment planning harder, not easier.

Watch whether other Southeast Asian countries follow Thailand's tariff move in the next two quarters.
