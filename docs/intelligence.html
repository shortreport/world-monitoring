Energy costs are rising across the Middle East due to regional conflict, and the risk of a major U.S.-Iran military escalation has jumped from 25 percent to 35 percent. Higher oil prices would ripple through global supply chains and manufacturing costs at a time when automakers are already managing thin margins on new vehicle production.

Europe is fracturing over how to respond to Chinese electric vehicle imports. Germany is pushing the UK to raise tariffs on Chinese EVs in exchange for EU support on industrial policy, while EU trade officials have begun talks in Beijing aimed at reducing the trade deficit—with threats of tariffs if no agreement emerges. This creates a patchwork of competing tariff regimes that will complicate sourcing and pricing strategies across Europe and beyond.

Watch whether U.S.-Iran tensions escalate further before year-end, which could push oil above $100 a barrel and force a shift in EV adoption timelines.
